Specialized project guide

Under the Loupe

Give the viewer one real coin and one detail to find. Let them inspect it before the dealer reveals the answer. Aim for a 20–35 second vertical short.

  1. Pick the specimen. For the Wisconsin quarter episode, use a 2004-D with a readable date and mint mark. Have the dealer confirm whether the ear of corn shows the normal design, Extra Leaf High or Extra Leaf Low. For a variety claim, capture the attribution label.
  2. Hook. Start on the quarter and ask, “Before this goes back in your change, can you spot the leaf collectors check?”
  3. Film the inspection. Capture both faces, a locked overhead shot, a macro of the lower left of the ear of corn and a second pass with side lighting. Hold the clean macro long enough for the viewer to make a call.
  4. Reveal and close. Freeze the shot, add a pointer and reveal the finding on this coin in the last three seconds. A normal example works. Close with “What would you check next?”
  5. Hand off. Export the captioned vertical MP4, paste the actual hook and CTA into the log and keep the clean close-ups for reuse.

More planned video formats

Check Your Change
Start with a circulating coin and ask viewers to find one mark. Use a 2017-P cent and a comparison cent, focus on the mint mark, then explain the difference. Ask which coin they will check next.
Call the Hammer
Record both faces and one feature of a named lot before the sale. For the recap, cut to the real auction close and reveal the verified winning bid or pass. Ask where the viewer would have stopped.
First Bid / Primera Puja
Film a staff bidding demonstration beside a real lot card. Ask whether a bid buys one coin or the whole group. Reveal the listed quantity and cut separate English and Spanish versions with matching captions.
Last Coin in the Roll
Film an intact roll and its opening in one continuous overhead shot. Keep the coins in order, reveal how many the dealer set aside and ask which date viewers would hunt.
Real or Fake Friday
Open with a question about one visible feature. Compare a documented shop example with a reliable reference and explain what that check establishes. Close with the next check a dealer would make.
Spot in 60
Use a dated quote and a real coin to explain one term, such as bid versus ask. Show the source, time, currency and unit. Include “not financial advice; metals prices can fall” in the caption and pinned comment.
Estate Desk / The Offer Slip
With written permission, follow one coin from an estate tray to the dealer’s separate appraisal. Mask identifying details. Reveal only an authorized, documented offer or payment.
1994 Receipt Drawer
Pair a dated, redacted shop receipt with the matching issue. Ask what the buyer took home and reveal the recorded amount at the end. Use a present-day comparison only when the items and terms match.
From the Door to the Desk
Follow a staff host from the Vancouver storefront to the counter with one unidentified coin. Film the dealer’s first question and where they inspect the date or edge.

Reusable assets and raw files

Intro or outro. Export a vault opener or a closing card with the verified sale date. Test the picture and sound against a real lot clip.

Raw files. Gather original images, cleared stock photos, video clips or clean coin close-ups for a future story. Name each file by subject and shot, and include the source, usage rights and credits. Upload one file or bundle a set in a ZIP under 25 MB. In the notes, say what the footage will help the next editor make.
