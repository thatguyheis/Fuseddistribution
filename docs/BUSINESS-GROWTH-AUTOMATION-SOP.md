# Daily Business Growth Blog and Reel SOP

This procedure applies only to the Codex automation `daily-business-growth-blog-and-reel`, which Nick authorized for two business-growth blog/reel pairs per Pacific day from the v2 365-day plan. It is a narrow exception to the repository's coin-only production scope. Keep `ops/content-scope.json`, coin-history scope, and all other publishing scope unchanged. Luna prepares and approves the next Pacific day's queue at 20:30. The local render job starts at 02:00 and completes topics serially so local TTS and video rendering stay in the overnight window. Attribute render attempts to the Pacific date on which the 02:00 batch starts.

The scheduled owner uses GPT-5.6 Luna with low reasoning for monitoring and routine production, following Nick's token-efficiency preference. For each preparation run, it uses one GPT-6 Luna low sub-agent for a bounded, read-only source and package review using only the current topic files. The helper cannot edit, schedule, render, deploy, or publish. Codex owns review and delivery; routine steps do not return to Nick. Keep the daily checkpoint in `.workflow-state/business-growth-ai-365/` and resume the same topic after a blocker rather than selecting a duplicate. Record every candidate separately in `inProgressTopics[]` and the dated run report; never overwrite a topic's IDs or evidence. A completed topic may wait for Buffer reconciliation without preventing the next topic from advancing.

Use `docs/REEL-PRODUCTION-RUNBOOK.md` for routine execution. The scheduled business exception writes with GPT directly; do not invoke `build-post.sh` or another local LLM drafting pass. A silent stdout stream is not proof that inference has stalled. Preserve checkpoints, finish editorial repair from verified sources and advance independent work on the second topic when the first is blocked.

## Package readiness and visual quality

`approved-sources.mjs` is the mandatory preflight before a ready handoff. It now runs actual script validation and the same presentation validator used by rendering. The closing question must be spoken verbatim; every shot needs a positive numeric weight, title, eyebrow and credit. It verifies counts, profile, safe zones and exact asset hashes. New business packages also run deterministic blog QA into a separate report, preserving editorial approval. Repair failures before handoff instead of labeling file presence as a QA pass.

Use the deterministic `public/blog/scripts/build-html.mjs` builder for the reviewed article, rather than hand-writing a minimal HTML shell. Inspect the resulting article and run local QA before editorial sign-off.

New business packages require `visualQualityVersion: 2` in enrichment-plan.json, at least two explanatory graphics in different forms, and a reserved unobstructed scene for each. Prefer a flow, a concrete comparison and a bar or pie where real data or explicit example counts justify one. Bar/pie charts need units, period, source and supported values; percentage pies total 100. Label example counts prominently, including in narration. Never fabricate viewer statistics or performance claims. The automatic target-circle motif is removed. Topic-specific graphics and examples must add information rather than fill space. Keep the exact approved hook and one spoken closing question.

Recovery may retry a failed topic once after newly approved source hashes pass all gates. Unchanged failures remain blocked. The night monitor repairs deterministic package defects, regenerates a valid dated handoff, then invokes the same serial runner within the existing time, lease, circuit and attempt limits. Daytime checks finish delivery and prepare repairs; they do not start heavy work unless Nick explicitly requests manual production.

## Hook, CTA, and performance learning

- Treat platform recommendations as testable creative guidance, not universal guarantees. TikTok's business guidance emphasizes the proposition in the first three seconds, a hook/body/close structure, and one clear CTA; Meta describes Instagram average watch time as a way to assess whether the hook holds attention; YouTube recommends inspecting retention dips, spikes, and top moments and comparing against similar-length videos.
- For every new reel, record one hook hypothesis and one primary success metric. Use the opening seconds to state a concrete outcome or problem and show a relevant visual. Avoid a false curiosity gap: pay off the promise early. Keep the original question or story alive with useful steps, meaningful visual changes, and source-backed graphics rather than repeated title cards.
- The final spoken/on-screen CTA should ask for one action. Use a specific, low-effort comment prompt when discussion is the goal, a save prompt for a reference/checklist, a share prompt when another person benefits, or a link prompt only when there is a relevant destination. Keep secondary actions out of the same CTA.
- Reconcile each platform's native analytics after enough data is available: Instagram average watch time, total watch time and sends; TikTok average watch time/completion and CTA response where available; YouTube Shorts viewed-versus-swiped-away, average view duration and audience-retention moments. Compare like with like (platform and similar duration), use the account's rolling baseline and include sample size. YouTube notes retention data may take 1–2 days and detailed highlights require sufficient views; never label an early or tiny sample as a winning hook.
- Change one creative variable at a time when possible. Record measured results in the existing feedback/performance workflow and carry forward only improvements supported by evidence. Do not optimize for views alone or insert invented performance benchmarks into the SOP.

## Narration and captions

- Primary base.en recognition gets one automatic small.en fallback for only suspicious segments. Repetition is measured against the approved narration so legitimate repeated terms do not fail. Both models and audio/narration cache keys are recorded; changing captions does not regenerate correct speech. If the fallback remains suspicious, preserve evidence and continue the next approved topic.

- Use the pinned Chatterbox Standard voice with Nick's reference for speech generation through `node video/scripts/generate-audio.mjs --post=<slug> --voice=chatterbox`. The current profile is exaggeration 0.75, CFG 0.3, temperature 0.8, min-p 0.05, top-p 1.0 and repetition penalty 1.2. Resemble's guidance says higher exaggeration can speed speech and lower CFG can temper that; the matched sample established a runtime baseline. Check measured segment duration, headroom and decode on every reel. Listen to a matched sample only when evaluating a future profile change. Whisper is offline speech recognition and word timing for captions, not a text-to-speech engine. Run it after narration through `node video/scripts/generate-captions.mjs --post=<slug>`, then check names, timing and cutoffs.
- Generate a fresh cryptographic random seed for every synthesized narration segment. Record it in `audio-manifest.json` and `.workflow-state/tts-runs/`; derive separate chunk seeds from that segment seed. Retries may reuse a completed exact audio checkpoint; a new synthesis attempt receives a new seed. Seeds make stochastic renders reproducible, while changed text or settings must invalidate the audio cache.
- Keep sentence-aware TTS chunks at or below 240 characters. The current Chatterbox `punc_norm` changes `...` to a comma and space and an em dash to a hyphen, so do not use those marks to promise a dramatic pause. Prefer clear sentence breaks and commas. Use `?` or `!` only where the words call for them; avoid stacked `!!` or `?!` as an unverified prosody control. Punctuation hints vary by model and are not guaranteed. After a settings change, synthesize and listen to one short sample, check duration and clipping, then run the approved batch serially.
