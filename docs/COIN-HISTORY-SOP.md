## Standard coin history format

The approved Fort Vancouver revision 2 is the current creative reference: `content/coin-history/fort-vancouver-centennial-half-dollar/narration-v2-approved.txt`, `build-plan-v2.mjs`, `production-status.json`, and the final 720p MP4. ### Story and transcript

Use the coin as the doorway into a specific history story. Normally devote most of the narration to what the person, place, event, institution or technology actually did; return to the coin for the payoff. Do not spend the middle listing slab grades, mint marks or design details unless those details are the central historical question.

Each transcript must have a clear hook and promise, a chronological or cause-and-effect explanation, and a direct payoff. Include at least four substantive sourced details that explain how something worked or changed: people and their work, dates with consequences, trade routes, practical uses, institutions, geography or policy. Names and dates alone are not sufficient enrichment. Explain the connection between details rather than assembling disconnected trivia. Avoid unsupported superlatives, fictional dialogue and invented historical scenes presented as evidence.

Aim for roughly 280-330 words and 110-130 seconds at the approved voice's measured pace, keeping the existing 135-second ceiling. Word count is a planning estimate, not a substitute for measuring audio. If an approved draft misses the minimum, the owner-authorized local workflow may repair it only with unused, source-mapped facts, then rerun the complete factual and hook audit and replace the transcript hash approval. Never pad, speed up or silently cut narration. A documentary closing is valid, but the automated reel format ends with a spoken, source-neutral question card of ten words or fewer; include it in the transcript before hashing and approval. Derive the share hook and discussion prompt from this approved plan and transcript, avoiding duplicate model calls for information already audited.

Prepare candidate concepts with a hook, proposed media style, history overview, available catalog IDs and primary sources. Separate verified facts from unresolved research questions and disclose missing usable evidence in the workup. Use four to eight distinct article sections; assign each sourced fact once, give each heading a different question, and do not add a mirrored “why it mattered” section that repeats the same claim. The authorized production workflow may auto-approve a topic and transcript after the automated audit passes: every factual sentence maps to a primary source, at least four substantive history details are present, the hook has a visible and verified payoff, the script fits the measured duration target, the topic is distinct from recent coverage, and no unresolved identity, rights or sensitive-claim flags remain. Save the exact transcript, audit results, approval evidence and SHA256. The user's October 20 authorization is the approval authority for this automated process. Any later substantive change requires the audit and hash record to be regenerated; caption spelling and punctuation fixes may retain observed speech timing when spoken wording is unchanged.

### Real and generated imagery together

Use the actual owner coin photos for coin evidence, relevant real location or object photographs for context, and clearly labeled AI imagery for supporting atmosphere. Historical paintings and engravings are illustrations, not photographs; modern reconstructed sites are not original period buildings. Obtain and record creator, source URL and license where applicable. User-supplied files do not automatically become public-domain or public stock assets.

For every coin evidence image, record the exact coin identity, catalog ID or verified source, source hash, crop hash, and visible design/date/inscription that supports the match. New packages run the local `gemma3:4b` image match once before queue admission and again against the unchanged crop before rendering. Both reviews must pass at confidence 0.85 or higher and remain bound to the episode, coin identity and image hash. A missing, uncertain, mismatched or changed image blocks the episode. Context imagery must still map to a specific sourced story claim and must not be presented as coin evidence.

Inspect full originals before trusting catalog titles. Recheck identity against authoritative designs and visible inscriptions; update mistaken catalog entries and mark stale idea briefs. Never infer a specimen's date, mint, grade or paired reverse from another file. Do not generate, retouch or sharpen missing coin details into evidence.

Plan approximately 15-22 purposeful shots for a two-minute episode, adjusting to narration. Prefer source-matched real photographs and archival material over unrelated stock footage. Include relevant AI supporting scenes, reusing already reviewed images where suitable; a new image generation is not required when the library already supplies the right scene. Stock video is optional and must explain the subject, not fill a quota. Do not invent portraits, maps, documents, buildings or events as archival evidence. For useful new local illustrations, use the 384 x 640, 20-step profile and allow up to four hours per image on the local machine; do not stop generation early just to shorten the run. Keep generated originals and reviewed outputs in the coin asset library with prompts, model, seed, hashes, provenance and illustrative labels so later episodes can reuse them. Run one heavyweight local job at a time and never present generated scenes as archival evidence.

Preserve wide compositions when the layout matters, using contained imagery over restrained background treatment. Use close views only where source resolution supports them. Add sourced timelines, route diagrams or comparisons when they explain the story. When owner supplied coin evidence exists, its hash verified image is the first frame and remains the visual anchor. Supporting photos may show documented places, processes, or period context only when source and caption match. Never let generated backgrounds or stock search results depict a named person, exact coin or historical event as evidence. If a historical image cannot be verified, keep the owner coin visible and use a restrained source mapped timeline or label card instead of unrelated filler. Keep readable captions in the safe region and restrained motion. Titles must clear before the next title appears during a dissolve. Every scene must earn its place.

### Production, delivery and reuse

Use a reviewed `presentation-plan.json` with shots, source paths, roles, labels and hashes for this format, rendered with `--quality=presentation`; see `docs/REEL-PRESENTATION-PROFILE.md`. Existing preferred-media plans remain supported for legacy episodes. The planning requirement alone does not switch a legacy scheduled renderer into this mode: confirm the selected production invocation stages the approved presentation plan before generating.

Default output is 720 x 1280, 30 fps. Target at most 44,000,000 bytes and enforce strictly less than 50,000,000 bytes on the final mastered upload file. Use the pinned full Chatterbox Standard profile in `video/tts-runtime.json` for every new generation: exaggeration 0.4, CFG weight 0.5, sentence-aware chunks up to 280 characters, 600 speech-token cap per chunk, seed 1337 with per-chunk derivation, CPU with two threads and the supervised 45-minute batch ceiling. The audio preflight synthesizes changed segments with the same 45-minute bound; do not apply a short timeout that can reject a healthy Standard generation. Preserve lossless narration masters, reviewed Whisper word timings and cleared background music. Target approximately -16 LUFS with true-peak headroom; measure the encoded result. If the mastered render exceeds 44 MB, run the measured two-pass H.264 size fit at `video/scripts/fit-upload-size.mjs`; it preserves the high-quality original master, retains the 192 kbps audio track, and targets 44 MB. Recheck duration, decode, captions, exact-file visuals and release QA after size correction. Never accept a file at or above 50 MB.

Run automated transcript-to-speech comparison, caption alignment, OCR/readability and safe-zone checks, image/source hash validation, claim-to-media mapping, frame sampling across every shot and transition, audio clipping/silence/loudness checks, playback and full decode. Release duration is measured from the mastered MP4 and must be 100-135 seconds. If real Chatterbox timing exceeds 135 seconds, shorten only redundant or vague passages while preserving source-mapped facts and the hook payoff, regenerate the transcript audit and hash approval, then synthesize only changed cached segments. Do not speed up, clip speech or weaken the factual gate to force fit. Record resolution, frame rate, duration, byte count, transcript and media hashes, source rights, test versions and each result in release QA. Re-encoding invalidates all output-bound results and requires the automated checks to run again. Routine manual listening or visual review is not a release gate. If a factual claim, exact coin identity, license, crop feature, caption or visual match cannot be supported by the configured automatic checks, try one approved source or layout fallback and rerun QA; if it still fails, stop that item with a machine-readable failure reason. Never mark a failed or uncertain item ready.

Preserve originals and prior masters. Store real references with provenance and rights state; store AI raw/final files with prompt, model, seed where available, subject tags and review state. Reuse only relevant approved assets with matching hashes. Keep narration and assets cached, run one expensive local workload at a time, and use existing workflow passes rather than creating new recurring LLM checks. A ready local file is not evidence of public hosting or posting; retain existing scoped publishing, scheduling and blog-embed verification gates.

## Required match between claim and image

Correction from Nick: the first Morgan episode misidentified an O mint mark that the pictured coin does not have. Historical sources about New Orleans did not verify the identity of that photographed specimen. Leave that existing article and reel unchanged as requested; never treat its approval, script, crop or catalog label as proof for future content.

Before approving any new coin article or reel:

1. Inspect the full original and a non-generative close view of the exact feature. Record the asset ID, file hash, crop coordinates, observed inscription/design and the claim it supports in the episode claim map.
2. Verify the historical fact independently with an authoritative source. A correct general fact does not establish that the pictured specimen illustrates it.
3. Confirm that the displayed image matches each specific date, mint, design or variety claimed by its narration, caption and hook. Do not transfer identity between neighboring files, apparent obverse/reverse pairs, slab labels or previously approved posts. Holder text may only be attributed as holder text, not independent visual proof.
4. If glare, wear, resolution or uncertainty prevents a confident reading, mark the feature unverified. Omit the specimen-specific claim or select another clearly verified image and supported topic. Never sharpen or generate missing detail into evidence. Escalate only when the missing identity is essential and cannot be resolved from available evidence.
5. Recheck the final crop and encoded frame: the supporting feature must remain visible and the caption must match. Unverified or mismatched claims block new editorial approval and release. File hashes prove that bytes are unchanged, not that a visual interpretation is correct.

Record corrections in the catalog immediately and withdraw stale labels from future reuse. The prior Morgan release is a documented exception requested by Nick, not a precedent for accepting uncertainty.

For coin-history post metadata, generate exactly six concise alt-text words while preserving the coin identity and visible side. If a verified source label has fewer than six words, append neutral descriptors such as “coin image”; never invent a date, mint mark or grade to satisfy the length check.

## Existing production and release gates

Keep the existing full Chatterbox Standard configuration, measured audio timing, local Whisper captions, approved music, Remotion transitions and distribution limit. Use the number of narration sections the approved story needs; do not force a six-scene script. The Fort Vancouver reference uses nine narration sections and 19 shots. Aim for 110–130 seconds and never exceed the current 135-second distribution ceiling. Before TTS, require the narration estimate to clear its preflight minimum; after rendering, measure the encoded reel and require 100–135 seconds and a file below 50 MB. Each visible card label must describe the narration on that card. The final card must be a readable question, and its spoken words must appear in the hash-approved transcript. If the local TTS circuit is held, the coin worker may run one bounded Chatterbox probe; it clears the circuit only when a fresh segment synthesizes and decodes. Failed QA or synthesis stays blocked and retries with backoff up to six hours. Never create a release-ready record from missing QA evidence.

Before synthesis, the local worker validates the approved transcript hash, adds a source-neutral discussion question of at most ten words when one is missing, and rebuilds the reel script from the recorded beat labels. It updates the related source hashes as one transaction; an unmappable beat count remains blocked. The release gate independently requires coin-history reels to end with a spoken question card. Keep the scheduled worker at its 15-minute interval; it owns one topic at a time, skips active render locks, and resumes after full Chatterbox synthesis, the encoded-frame review, and release QA.

`preferred-media.json` must contain reviewed images and SHA256 hashes, catalog asset IDs where used, and honest scene roles. Complete plans cover every segment with sequential indices. New mixed plans set `mode: mixed` and list only protected evidence scenes; supporting slots use local generation and stock. Optional reviewed cutaways keep evidence moving without inventing detail. Include the plan hash in editorial `qa.json`. `video/scripts/owned-media.mjs` validates the whole plan before staging; changed or missing scenes fail closed. Without this plan the normal media selection remains available. Selecting owned scenes skips stock search and generated-background work for those scenes, saving time and tokens.

Run local blog QA, narration preflight, source-hash approval and the normal renderer. The exact-MP4 visual review gate runs automatically against six encoded frames sampled through the video while stopping before the EOF boundary, which can otherwise add a false black frame to the contact sheet. Bind its report to both the final video hash and contact-sheet hash before release. The caption timing audit samples three points per narration beat and accepts a nearest timed word within 0.75 seconds, allowing natural speech pauses without overlooking longer uncovered spans. The release gate enforces duration, file size, voice, captions, music rights and all source hashes. Do not call a rendered file released, hosted, scheduled or posted without evidence for that state.

Record episode use against catalog IDs after preparing a package, then update its status as production progresses. The reusable package command accepts a complete source checked episode manifest, verifies the approved transcript hash, claim/source mapping, catalog image identity and hash, then creates blog inputs and adds the episode to the queue only after the first image match passes. Maintain three complete unprocessed packages each morning. The local worker writes a daily queue health alert when the prepared count is lower and tracks per stage duration, attempts and pair completion by Pacific date. The local coin-draft worker publishes a blog only after hash-bound blog QA passes, confirms the public URL, and prepares a reel. Host and queue only reels that pass the exact-MP4 release gate through the shared Buffer process. Reconcile the corresponding blog video embed once the hosted or YouTube video is verified. Verify an actual player and source URL in live HTML, not just the presence of social-video.json. Regression tests in tests/blog-video-url.test.mjs cover approved hosted and YouTube URLs and reject unsafe hosts/schemes. The local coin-draft worker does not call a remote model or publish directly to social platforms.
