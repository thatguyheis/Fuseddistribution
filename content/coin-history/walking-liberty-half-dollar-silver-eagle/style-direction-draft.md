# Reel style direction draft: object-led history

This adapts the attached finance explainer playbook for Fused coin history. Treat the playbook as a visual reference, not production authority. Keep the existing coin-history rules, approved Fused colors, real-photo evidence, Chatterbox Standard voice profile, and release checks.

Borrow these techniques:

- Open with the actual coin image and a short question, then hold long enough to read it.
- Use a pinned-photo treatment for owner coin photographs, preserving the reviewed crop and keeping the coin large enough to inspect.
- Introduce history through a moving date line: 1916 → 1947 → 1948 → 1986 → 2021. Animate one date at a time, with a restrained wipe or slide between milestones.
- Keep captions to short spoken phrases, generally 1–4 words at a time, timed to the voice. Use a high-contrast caption band in the established Fused palette and keep it within the safe region.
- Reserve hand-drawn circles and highlight strokes for details the narration names. Do not use annotation to imply a mint mark, grade, or feature that the photograph does not establish.
- Let photo moves, timeline builds, and match cuts carry the motion. Use hard cuts for factual section changes; use one brief slide or paper-card transition to mark the design's return in 1986. Avoid constant camera movement and decorative transitions without narrative purpose.
- End on the two verified photographs side by side and a simple design-lineage takeaway.

The attached playbook's cream grid, yellow-on-black typography, and full palette are not applied wholesale in this sample. The proposed direction uses the page's paper texture and annotation ideas lightly, while retaining Fused's dark teal and cyan identity. This keeps the experiment focused on pacing, emphasis, and readability rather than changing the brand.

## Sample sequence

1. Hook: actual half dollar photo, “Same Liberty?” Phrase caption: “Same Liberty?”
2. Reveal: crop move from the rising sun to Liberty's outstretched arm; add a small “1916” date stamp.
3. Timeline: 1916 → 1947, as the half dollar's Walking Liberty run ends.
4. Turn: 1948 Franklin card replaces the Liberty marker. Caption: “A new half dollar.”
5. Return: slide the line forward to 1986, then match cut to the actual Silver Eagle obverse. Caption: “The design returns.”
6. Modern example: hold on the photographed 2021 Eagle date, with a clean reverse-change note; do not imply the 2021 reverse is visible in this obverse photo.
7. Payoff: both real obverse photos side by side; “Different coins. One design lineage.”

The attached playbook would improve subtitle rhythm and section transitions here. Keep the historical date cards as the main graphic system; do not add unrelated charts or financial-style numeric emphasis to a design-history story.
