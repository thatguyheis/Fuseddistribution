# Why a British Fur Trading Company Appears on a U.S. Half Dollar

Rough draft v1 | October 13, 2026 | For Nick's review | Not published

Why is a British fur trading company named on an American half dollar?

Look closely at the reverse in our photograph. A standing figure holds a gun across his body. Behind him are a fort and a mountain. Around the design, the words “FORT VANCOUVER CENTENNIAL” and “HUDSON'S BAY COMPANY” turn a small coin into a question about Pacific Northwest history.

The answer starts a century before the coin was issued.

## The two dates tell the story

The 1925 Fort Vancouver Centennial half dollar marked 100 years since the Hudson's Bay Company founded the fort. On the portrait side, the dates 1825 and 1925 frame the anniversary. This is Vancouver, Washington, the location named on the coin. [U.S. Mint](https://www.usmint.gov/learn/coins-and-medals/commemorative-coins/fort-vancouver-centennial-half)

The London based company established Fort Vancouver in 1825 as the headquarters of its interior fur trade. So the company's name is part of the reason this place existed, rather than an unexpected advertisement on American money. [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/fova/learn/historyculture/hbcfort1.htm)

## A portrait on one side, a place on the other

Our portrait photograph shows the name Dr. John McLoughlin below the bust. McLoughlin served as the fort's chief factor, the official responsible for its operations, from 1825 to 1845. [National Park Service](https://home.nps.gov/fova/planyourvisit/placestogo-mcloughlin-house.htm)

Turn to the other photograph and the design opens into a scene. The U.S. Mint describes a frontiersman, a fort and a mountain peak. It credits Laura Gardin Fraser with both sides of the coin. [U.S. Mint](https://www.usmint.gov/learn/coins-and-medals/commemorative-coins/fort-vancouver-centennial-half)

The fort itself supported a much larger community than one portrait or figure can show. Its workers included carpenters, blacksmiths, bakers, trappers and traders, while the surrounding village could grow to hundreds of people when fur brigades arrived. [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/places/fortvancouver.htm)

## What the photographed holder tells us

The label in our obverse photograph reads PCGS MS63. That is the grade printed on the holder; we have not independently checked its certification record for this article. The history here comes from the coin's readable design and documented sources, rather than a claim about its price or rarity.

There is another interesting detail about the issue: the U.S. Mint says these coins were produced in San Francisco without a mint mark. That is documented production history, not a letter we are claiming to see in the photograph. [U.S. Mint](https://www.usmint.gov/learn/coins-and-medals/commemorative-coins/fort-vancouver-centennial-half)

## A local story you can hold

Read the two sides together: a name, a place, a company and a pair of dates. The British company's name belongs on this American commemorative because the coin marks the founding of Fort Vancouver by that company, 100 years earlier.

Which draws you in first: the portrait, the fort scene, or the story hidden in the lettering?
