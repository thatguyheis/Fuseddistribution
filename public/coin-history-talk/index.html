Timeline

A short history
of pocket change.

Follow the metal from the first stamped pieces of electrum to the coins in circulation today.

c. 600 BCLydia stamps electrum

In what is now western Turkey, a stamp on a piece of gold and silver alloy gave traders a mark they could recognize.

1792A Mint for the United States

The Coinage Act established a national Mint in Philadelphia and set standards for the coins it would strike.

1878The Morgan dollar arrives

George T. Morgan’s portrait of Liberty went into production after passage of the Bland Allison Act. Branch mints placed their identifying letters beneath the eagle.

1918Silver dollars for the furnace

Under the Pittman Act, the United States melted more than 270 million silver dollars. Replacing them brought the Morgan design back into production in 1921.

1925Fort Vancouver in silver

A century after the Hudson’s Bay Company established its trading post, Laura Gardin Fraser paired John McLoughlin’s portrait with a view of the fort on a commemorative half dollar.

Read the Fort Vancouver story
1933Gold leaves American pockets

Restrictions on private gold ownership took effect as the Mint struck its last Saint-Gaudens double eagles. The 1933 pieces never entered regular circulation.

1965Change loses its silver

The Coinage Act replaced silver in circulating dimes and quarters with copper and nickel. The half dollar retained a smaller share of silver.

2026Two dates on a coin

Designs bearing the dates 1776 and 2026 mark the 250th anniversary of American independence.

Rare facts

Look again.

Some of the best clues are the details that seem wrong at first.

When was the first 1804 dollar struck?

In 1834. The Mint used the old date for silver dollars made for diplomatic gift sets.

Why does a 1943 cent stick to a magnet?

Most 1943 cents were struck in zinc coated steel to conserve copper for the war effort. The steel core attracts a magnet.

How many 1913 Liberty nickels are known?

Five. They surfaced after the Mint had switched to the Buffalo design. The circumstances of their striking are still debated.

What did a 1933 double eagle fetch in 2021?

The example sold at Sotheby’s that June brought $18,872,250.

What penalty did the first Mint Act set?

Section 19 of the 1792 act prescribed death for certain frauds by Mint employees, including deliberately debasing the coins.

How quickly did Kennedy appear on a coin?

Congress authorized his half dollar about a month after the assassination. The first issues entered circulation in 1964.
