Interactive timeline

2,600 years of money, in one scroll.

Each stop on the timeline opens into a full story. The live site adds coin photos, maps, and short films at every point.

600 BC

Lydia strikes the first coins

In the kingdom of Lydia, in what is now Turkey, lumps of electrum are stamped with a mark. A stamp you could trust turned metal into money.

Pillar: Coin History
1792

The Coinage Act builds a Mint

Congress creates the United States Mint in Philadelphia. Section 19 makes debasing the nation's coins a crime punishable by death.

Source: Coinage Act of 1792
1878

The Morgan dollar arrives

George T. Morgan's silver dollar goes into production after the Bland Allison Act. Branch mints, including New Orleans, add their own small letters.

Pillar: Mint History
1918

270 million dollars go into the furnace

The Pittman Act melts 270,232,722 silver dollars to help Britain during the First World War. Congress then requires the silver to be replaced.

Source: Pittman Act of 1918
1925

Vancouver gets its own coin

The Fort Vancouver Centennial half dollar marks a century since the Hudson's Bay Company post was founded. Our hometown story, and a coin we hold in hand.

Local story
1933

Gold leaves American pockets

An executive order requires most gold coin to be turned in. The 1933 double eagles are struck but never released, which sets up one of the great coin mysteries.

Source: Executive Order 6102
1965

Silver leaves the change jar

The Coinage Act of 1965 ends silver in dimes and quarters. Older coins quietly disappear from circulation as people save them.

Source: Coinage Act of 1965
2026

Coins with two dates

For the nation's 250th, circulating coins carry the dates 1776 and 2026. Five new quarters drop Washington from the front for the first time since 1930.

Source: U.S. Mint semiquincentennial program
