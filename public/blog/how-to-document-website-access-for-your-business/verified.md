# Document Website Access for Google Business Profile

When a customer opens your Google Business Profile, the website link should take them to the right business site. Keep a short record of the URL, the account that manages the profile, the important service pages, and the person who can update them. This website access documentation makes handoffs easier and gives you a clear checklist when a page or link changes.

This guide is for a local business that already has a [website](/blog/how-to-prepare-website-content-before-hiring-a-designer/) or is preparing one. It does not require server work or custom code.

## Why Document Your Website Access for Local Search

Your Business Profile and your website do different jobs. The profile helps customers find business information on Google Search and Maps. Your website gives them the fuller details, such as services, hours, contact options, and booking instructions.

Google's [Business Profile guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3039617?hl=en) says you can add the full website URL to a claimed and verified profile. It also says to make sure Googlebot is not blocked from the site. Writing down the exact URL helps you catch a common handoff error: the profile points to an old homepage, a staging site, or a page that no longer exists.

Keep one access record with these fields:

- Business Profile name and the Google account that manages it.
- Website homepage, including `https://`.
- Domain registrar, hosting provider, and content management system.
- Main service pages and the person responsible for each one.
- Date checked, what changed, and who approved the change.

Do not put passwords in a public document. Store credentials in your normal password manager and record only where the authorized owner can retrieve them.

## Step 1: Identify Your Website Access Points

Start with the homepage. Open the address you plan to share with customers and confirm that it loads on a phone and a desktop. Check the spelling of the domain, the secure `https://` connection, the phone number, the service summary, and the main contact action.

Next, list the pages a customer is most likely to need. For example, a plumbing company might record its plumbing repair, water heater, service area, and contact pages. Use real page names from the site. Do not create a long list of possible keywords or pages that do not exist.

For each page, write down:

1. The page title and full URL.
2. The service or question it answers.
3. The person who can edit it.
4. The date it was last reviewed.

This is the useful part of business website access documentation. A future designer, employee, or agency can see what exists without guessing which page is current.

## Step 2: Set Up Your Website Link for Google

Sign in to the Google account that manages your Business Profile. On the profile, edit the website field and enter the complete URL, including `http://` or `https://`. Google's [official editing instructions](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3039617?hl=en) specifically call for the full URL.

After saving, open the public profile as a customer would and follow the website link. Confirm that it lands on the intended homepage or service page. If your business uses a booking, ordering, or appointment link, document that separately so the main website link does not get replaced by the wrong destination. Keep the profile and website details consistent. The business name, phone number, hours, service description, and location or service area should be reviewed together whenever one of them changes. If you need the setup steps, see this [Google Business Profile setup guide](/blog/google-business-profile-setup/).

This does not guarantee a ranking or a specific number of calls. It simply reduces avoidable confusion for customers and for the people maintaining the listing.

If a developer or agency manages the site, ask for a plain language handoff: the live domain, the editing system, the hosting contact, the backup process, and the change owner. You should be able to identify who can change the website link for Google without searching through old email threads.

## Step 3: Verify Access and Ensure Visibility

Use [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console/) to confirm that the site is connected to the right property. Google's [Search Console documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/search-console-start) explains that the service reports how Google crawls, indexes, and serves a site. Its URL Inspection tool can show the current index status of a page and let an owner or full user test the live URL.

Check the homepage and the most important service page. Record the inspection date and any issue that needs follow up. If a page was recently changed, use the inspection result as a diagnostic signal, not as a promise that the page will appear immediately.

Also check the basics a customer can see:

- The website link in the profile opens without a redirect loop or error.
- The homepage explains what the business does and how to contact it.
- Service pages use clear titles and do not repeat the same copy for different places.
- Important pages are linked from the site navigation or another relevant page.
- The contact form, phone link, booking link, and confirmation message work.

If Search Console reports a crawl or indexing problem, fix the underlying page or access issue before creating another version of the same page. Keep the issue, owner, and next check in your documentation record.

## What Happens Next: Use Documentation for Reviews and SEO

Review the record whenever the domain, website platform, phone number, service area, or Business Profile manager changes. A quarterly check is a practical starting point, with an extra check after a redesign, domain move, or major service change.

Use the record when asking customers for a review or updating your profile. Make sure the link you share goes to the intended page and that the page gives the visitor enough context to contact the business. For review guidance, see [Google's review management help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en).

The goal is not a complicated SEO binder. It is a current, private handoff note that answers four questions: Which profile is ours? Which website is live? Who can change it? When did we last verify the customer path? Keep those answers current and website access becomes easier to manage.
