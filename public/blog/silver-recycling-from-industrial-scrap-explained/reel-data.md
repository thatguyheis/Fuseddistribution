# Silver Recycling From Industrial Scrap Explained

hook: Scrap weight is not silver content.

## segments
- type: overlay
 text: IDENTIFY THE INDUSTRIAL SCRAP BEING DISCUSSED
 narration: Write down the material category used by the source. Keep a distinction between a specific waste stream and a broad label such as electronics. If the source gives a facility, process or reporting period, preserve that context. A useful record explains what was included rather than attaching a silver label to an unidentified pile of material.
- type: overlay
 text: SEPARATE SCRAP MASS FROM SILVER CONTENT
 narration: Give gross material weight, stated silver content and recovered silver separate fields. Record units beside each quantity. If a number is estimated, keep that label. Without those distinctions, a recycling story can accidentally compare the weight entering a process with the amount of one metal leaving it. Avoid calculating a recoverable quantity from a product photograph or a generic internet example.
- type: overlay
 text: FOLLOW THE RECOVERY BOUNDARY IN THE REPORT
 narration: Look for whether a report describes material collected, processed or recovered. Note the geography and period as well as the stage. The same material may appear in records at more than one stage, so adding every listed quantity can create a misleading total. Read the categories before doing arithmetic. The distinction between mine and refined production illustrates a similar accounting problem.
- type: overlay
 text: KEEP RECYCLING CLAIMS WITHIN THE EVIDENCE
 narration: Save the edition, URL and date of the report with your notes. If you cite the USGS study above, make its historical period visible. Do not describe its material mix or totals as current conditions. Use a contemporary, clearly defined source when your question concerns today's activity, and document any differences between the datasets.
