## HOOK

**Visual:** Check the request first
**Duration:** 27s minimum

Narration: A polished AI reply can still miss what a customer asked for. Put the original request beside the draft before anyone approves it or lets it trigger an action. The reviewer should check the requested outcome, the facts behind the reply and any promise in its wording. Treat the draft as a proposal, not evidence that a task is complete. NIST’s AI Risk Management Framework discusses defined responsibilities and documented oversight processes. It does not prescribe one review method for every business.

---

## OVERLAY: Keep the original

**Visual:** Read the request and draft side by side
**Duration:** 25s minimum

Narration: Keep the customer’s original message available. A summary can drop a condition, date or distinction that changes the answer. When the reply depends on an order or policy, show the authoritative record too. Make clear which version of the draft is being reviewed. For example, a customer may ask whether work can finish before a certain date. A reply that describes the service but confirms that date without checking capacity has added an unsupported promise.

---

## OVERLAY: Compare the meaning

**Visual:** Does the reply answer the request?
**Duration:** 23s minimum

Narration: First identify the result the customer wants. Check whether the proposed reply addresses it and preserves important qualifications. Then verify factual statements against business records or current policy. Fluent wording is not proof that a detail is correct. If the source is missing or two records disagree, pause and ask for the detail that would settle the question. Do not ask a reviewer to guess what the customer meant.

---

## OVERLAY: Check commitments

**Visual:** Dates and promises need evidence
**Duration:** 24s minimum

Narration: Look for commitments about dates, prices, eligibility, refunds or delivery. A reply can describe a service accurately and still promise a date that nobody has confirmed. If a source record is missing or contradictory, hold the draft and ask for the information needed to resolve it. Use a reviewer who has authority over the policy behind the commitment. Record whether the reply was sent so any later correction can address customer impact.

---

## OVERLAY: Define the decision

**Visual:** Approve, revise or hold
**Duration:** 25s minimum

Narration: Give the reviewer the original request, the relevant evidence and the proposed reply together. Ask for a clear decision: approve, revise or hold. Keep drafting permission separate from sending a reply, changing a record or issuing a refund. A missed review deadline is not approval. When reviewers find repeated mismatches, save the error type and improve the instructions or source access. Test the revised workflow with ordinary and incomplete requests before changing its review boundary.
