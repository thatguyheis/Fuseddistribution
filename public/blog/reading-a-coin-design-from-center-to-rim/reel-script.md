## HOOK

**Visual:** Start at the center. Notice the rim.
**Duration:** 29s minimum

Narration: A deliberate viewing order can help you notice more than the most recognizable image on a coin. Start at the center, move through the surrounding space and inscriptions, then inspect the rim. Keep the edge as a separate view. This is a way to organize observations, not a method of grading or authentication. The U.S. Mint anatomy guide names the field, relief, inscriptions, rim and edge. Use those terms where they describe the feature you mean.

---

## OVERLAY: DESCRIBE THE CENTRAL SUBJECT

**Visual:** Describe the central subject
**Duration:** 26s minimum

Narration: Write what is visibly depicted before describing its intended meaning. A building, figure or plant can be observed without claiming that you know why the designer chose it. If the issuer explains the design, cite that explanation separately from your own interpretation. Notice how the subject occupies the available space. Does it reach toward the lettering, or leave a broad open area around it? Record the relationship in plain language.

---

## OVERLAY: LOOK AT THE FIELD AND RELIEF

**Visual:** Look at the field and relief
**Duration:** 26s minimum

Narration: The Mint describes the field as the flat portion of the surface not used for design or inscription, and relief as the raised design. Use light carefully when inspecting those areas. Reflections can hide details or exaggerate apparent differences between photographs. Our guide to photographing a coin inventory explains keeping useful views. Preserve original images and label additional crops. If a feature cannot be seen clearly, say so.

---

## OVERLAY: FOLLOW THE INSCRIPTION TOWARD THE RIM

**Visual:** Follow the inscription toward the rim
**Duration:** 25s minimum

Narration: Read the visible words, dates and marks, keeping uncertain characters clearly marked. Observe spacing and placement without assuming that an unfamiliar appearance proves a variety or error. The raised border around a face is the rim; the outer border seen from the side is the edge. The distinction matters when you describe what interests you. Our article on the coin collar provides production context for the edge.

---

## OVERLAY: TURN THE VIEWING ORDER INTO A RECORD

**Visual:** Turn the viewing order into a record
**Duration:** 17s minimum

Narration: Use a short sequence in your notes: central subject, surrounding field, inscription, rim and separate edge view. Add image references and unresolved questions. This makes the record easier for another person to follow than a general statement that the piece looks unusual.
