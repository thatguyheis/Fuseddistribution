## HOOK

**Visual:** A drawing becomes a struck design.
**Duration:** 21s minimum

Narration: A coin design moves through more than a drawing stage before it appears on a finished coin. The U.S. Mint describes an approved line drawing becoming a three dimensional sculpt, with a final digital model used in making production tooling. Following those stages helps explain why a sketch and a struck object are different kinds of evidence.

---

## OVERLAY: READ THE ARTIST SKETCH AS A DESIGN PROPOSAL

**Visual:** Read the artist sketch as a design proposal
**Duration:** 24s minimum

Narration: Start by identifying what the image represents. Is it a candidate drawing, an approved design or a photograph of a finished coin? Save the original caption and source with your notes. A cropped image shared without context may remove the very label that answers the question you are trying to investigate. Describe visible choices such as the main subject, lettering and placement of supporting details.

---

## OVERLAY: FOLLOW THE DESIGN INTO A SCULPTED MODEL

**Visual:** Follow the design into a sculpted model
**Duration:** 24s minimum

Narration: The Mint explains that a medallic artist turns the approved drawing into a sculpt, using physical or digital methods. The model gives the design a three dimensional form. That adds a different way to examine the design from the lines and shapes visible in a flat drawing. When comparing a model image with the drawing, note what the source identifies as the same design and version.

---

## OVERLAY: DISTINGUISH THE COIN MODEL FROM PRODUCTION TOOLING

**Visual:** Distinguish the coin model from production tooling
**Duration:** 21s minimum

Narration: The Mint says the final digital model is used to carve a master hub in the process of making the die that stamps the design. This connection explains why a model image is not a photograph of the working tool. Our article on how dies transfer a design gives additional context for the tooling stage.

---

## OVERLAY: COMPARE THE FINISHED COIN WITH ITS SOURCE DESIGN

**Visual:** Compare the finished coin with its source design
**Duration:** 21s minimum

Narration: Look at the final coin with consistent lighting and magnification, then return to the documented design images. Record the details you can compare directly, such as the relationship between the subject and the inscription. Avoid making an attribution or condition judgment solely from a small promotional image. Keep designer and sculptor credits as the issuer records them.
