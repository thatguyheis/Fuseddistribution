Narration: AI tools can help a small business produce marketing work faster, but the best results come from using them for narrow, repeatable jobs. ## STAT: 1 AUDIENCE HOMEOWNERS MILES WHO

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 22s minimum

Narration: Give the tool your audience, offer, proof, tone, channel, length, and action you want the reader to take. Tell it not to invent prices, reviews, guarantees, or customer stories. A useful prompt can follow this format: 1. Audience: homeowners within 20 miles who need seasonal maintenance. Offer: a fixed-price inspection with the real price supplied in the prompt. Proof: years in business, service area, and verified credentials.

## OVERLAY: CHANGE ONLY HEADLINE SUPPORTING

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 20s minimum

Narration: Change only the headline, supporting photo, and offer. This keeps weekly work recognizable and reduces review time. Your inbox, calls, estimates, and reviews contain better content ideas than a generic trend list. Remove personal details, group repeated questions, and ask an AI tool to organize them by customer stage. Categories might include first questions, comparison questions, purchase concerns, and after-sale support.

**Duration:** 20s minimum

Narration: If several questions reveal a missing service page, use the guidance in Bing Places for Business setup and your local profiles to keep service details consistent across search touchpoints. AI can help draft welcome emails, appointment reminders, quote follow-ups, and reactivation messages. The automation should still depend on real customer actions, not guesses. A new lead can receive a welcome note.

## OVERLAY: PLACE SENSITIVE CUSTOMER DETAILS

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 19s minimum

Narration: Do not place sensitive customer details, payment data, medical information, or private job notes into an AI service unless your agreement and settings explicitly permit that use. Keep prompts limited to the information needed for the message. AI can help organize location details, service descriptions, frequently asked questions, and post ideas, but it should not invent local facts.

## OVERLAY: EDITING TIME MATTERS GENERATION

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 21s minimum

Narration: Editing time matters more than generation speed. A tool that writes in ten seconds but needs thirty minutes of repair is not saving time. Track minutes from source material to approved output. Include setup and review, not just the first response. Choose the smallest tool set that wins on the work you actually do. Recheck the scorecard each quarter because product features, limits, and prices change.

## OVERLAY: COMPARE NEW PROCESS PREVIOUS

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 22s minimum

Narration: Compare the new process with your previous four weeks when possible. Do not credit the tool for reach or sales without a clear link. A faster content process is useful, but it should eventually support a customer action. Paid promotion and organic work need separate tracking. If you are deciding where to spend, review pay per click versus organic SEO before assigning budget to AI-generated campaign ideas.

## OVERLAY: FIRST JOB WORKS ADD

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 22s minimum

Narration: If the first job works, add one related task. For example, move from drafting one weekly email to adapting that approved email into two social posts. Do not add five new tools at once. The right AI marketing setup in 2026 is the one your team can understand, review, and measure. Start with a real bottleneck, protect your source facts, and keep final accountability with a person.
