# Best Apps to Track Your Silver Portfolio Value

There is no single best app for every silver holder. The right choice depends on whether you need a physical inventory, a market price view, or both. CoinStats and Delta document portfolio tracking for digital assets and market investments. Google Sheets and Microsoft Excel document flexible tables and formulas. A careful setup can use one tool for calculations while preserving custody and identity records in a separate inventory.

## Match a silver tracking app to the question

If you own coins and bars, begin with identity and custody. Record an inventory ID, denomination, date, mint mark, stated fineness, gross weight, fine troy ounces, photographs, invoice, storage location, and notes about condition. For bars, add maker, serial number, assay card, and packaging. Keep this source record in a controlled file or binder. An app total cannot authenticate a coin, confirm a delivery, or replace a missing invoice.

If you own a silver ETF, mining share, or another financial instrument, the question is different. You need a market symbol, quantity, transaction date, cost, fees, account, and price history. That is a securities record. It should be labeled separately from physical metal so a portfolio chart does not imply that a bar exists.

## CoinStats: best for a crypto centered portfolio with manual entries

CoinStats official help documents connected exchanges and wallets, manual portfolios, transaction entry, and analytics. That makes it useful for supported digital assets and their transactions. The reviewed manual entry instructions do not establish custom physical bullion entries, so keep coins and bars in a separate inventory. The reviewed documentation does not establish automatic bullion inventory, vault verification, serial number capture, or authentication.

Use CoinStats when you value a consolidated digital portfolio, wallet and exchange connections, and portfolio analytics. Before adding a metal related item, verify whether the selected asset is physical bullion, an ETF, a mining company, or another instrument. Record the distinction in your independent inventory and notes. CoinStats is not a substitute for a custody ledger.

## Delta: best for a read only investment view

Delta by eToro describes itself as a read only investment tracker. Its official documentation covers stocks, funds, ETFs, crypto, wallets, indices, forex, broker connections, and following assets for price tracking. That makes Delta a good fit for a securities portfolio where you want a consolidated view across accounts and market categories.

Delta is not documented as a physical bullion register. It does not hold or withdraw funds, and the reviewed sources do not establish a workflow for a coin photograph, bar serial number, assay card, or depository confirmation. A silver ETF or mining share in Delta is a market proxy. Keep its symbol and account separate from the inventory of coins and bars.

## Google Sheets: best for a transparent custom ledger

Google Sheets is a strong choice when you want every field visible and formulas you control. Google’s documentation supports formulas such as `=SUM(A2:A100)` and table references that update when rows are added or removed. Create columns for inventory ID, category, item, date, quantity, gross grams, fine troy ounces, cost, fees, storage, document link, and review status.

For total gross grams across the recorded lot and a known decimal fineness such as 0.999, calculate fine ounces with `=GrossWeightGrams/31.1034768*Fineness`. Keep the conversion constant visible and label the result as an estimate when the weight or fineness is only stated. For a hypothetical bid, calculate `=FineOunces*BidPerFineOunce`; for a purchase premium as a decimal, calculate `=(DeliveredCostPerFineOunce-ReferencePrice)/ReferencePrice` and format the result as a percentage. Do not fill a current price unless you have an exact dated source. A spreadsheet is a calculation tool, not a live quote or appraisal.

Google Sheets works well for shared estate inventories and audit trails when access is controlled. Use separate tabs for physical metal, securities, transactions, documents, and assumptions. Protect formula columns and record who changed a row. Do not publish serial numbers or storage locations in a public link.

## Microsoft Excel: best for a structured offline workbook

Excel is a strong choice when you want an offline workbook with tables, formulas, filters, and a durable file under your control. Microsoft documents creating formatted tables and formulas such as `=SUM(A1:A2)`. Use a table with one row per item or matched group, then add a second transaction table for purchases, transfers, sales, appraisal events, and storage changes.

Excel is especially useful when an estate, business, or household already has controlled Microsoft file storage and a review process. Use the same physical and financial separation as in Sheets. Keep fine ounces, reference prices, bids, premiums, and fees in separate columns. A formula can show a scenario, but it must not be presented as a guaranteed sale value.

## Ranking by use case

Choose Google Sheets when the priority is a transparent, custom physical inventory with shared review. Choose Excel when the priority is a structured offline workbook, table based sorting, and controlled file storage. Choose Delta when the priority is a read only view of stocks, ETFs, funds, crypto, and connected investment accounts. Choose CoinStats when the priority is crypto and wallet tracking with manual portfolio support.

For a mixed portfolio, the best practical setup is often a two layer record: Sheets or Excel for physical identity, custody, and calculations, plus CoinStats or Delta for the market instruments they document. Reconcile the layers by date. Never add an ETF balance to the physical fine ounce total, and never use a metal themed ticker as proof of a coin or bar.

## Review checklist

When you import a CSV, preserve the original file and note the import date. Check whether quantity means pieces, shares, grams, or ounces before using it in a formula. For physical silver quoted by gross alloy weight, calculate fine ounces from documented weight and fineness. If a specification already states one fine troy ounce per piece, use that amount times quantity without applying fineness a second time. For a security, quantity means shares or units and the instrument’s own price convention applies. These fields should never be silently combined. If an item is uncertain, place it in an unknown group until a qualified person identifies it. A conservative blank is more useful than a confident but unsupported number.

Label each holding as physical metal, ETF, mining equity, crypto, cash, or unknown. Store the evidence for identity and ownership. Check the formula units. Record the date and source for any reference price. Mark estimates and hypothetical bids clearly. Reconcile transactions after every purchase, transfer, sale, appraisal, or storage change. Ask a qualified appraiser about unusual or disputed items and a tax professional about basis and reporting.

The best tracker is the one whose documented features match the question you need to answer. Use a market app for a market view and a controlled workbook for physical records. Keep those meanings separate and your portfolio value will be easier to explain, audit, and correct.
