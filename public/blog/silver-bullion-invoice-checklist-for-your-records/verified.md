Keep a clear record of each silver bullion transaction while the invoice and order details are easy to find. A consistent checklist helps you compare what you ordered with what arrived, answer later questions, and locate the original purchase documentation. It does not decide your tax treatment or replace advice from a qualified professional.

## Why keep a silver bullion invoice checklist?

A transaction history is most useful when it connects the invoice to the specific products and shipment. A dealer invoice may identify the date, items, quantity, and amount charged, while a separate order confirmation or carrier record may contain shipping details. Save the original documents together so you can trace each line item back to the transaction.

For tax purposes, keep accurate records of items that may affect the basis of property. The IRS explains that basis for property you purchase is usually its cost, but the right treatment depends on the facts and tax rules that apply to you. Read [IRS Publication 551](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p551) and ask a tax professional how a specific bullion purchase should be classified or reported. A personal spreadsheet is an index to your records, not a tax determination.

## What invoice details should you capture?

Start with the dealer's original invoice and preserve it unchanged. Then make an index with enough detail to identify the transaction without relying on memory:

- Purchase date and order or invoice number.
- Dealer name and the contact information shown on the invoice.
- Product description for each line, such as coin, round, or bar, plus the quantity.
- Stated weight and purity when the invoice identifies them.
- Item price, discounts, tax, shipping, handling, and any listed insurance charge as separate amounts.
- Payment method and a reference to the confirmation or receipt, without storing full card or bank account numbers.
- Shipping date, carrier, tracking reference, delivery date, and any condition notes kept with the order.

Copy the seller's terms as they appear rather than paraphrasing coverage or promising that a charge guarantees a particular outcome. If the invoice omits a detail, mark it as not shown and ask the dealer for clarification. Do not infer purity, insurance, or the tax treatment from a product nickname.

## Use this transaction history checklist

For each order, create one record and link it to the source files:

1. Match the invoice number to the order confirmation and payment receipt.
2. Check each product line against the order: description, quantity, weight, and stated purity.
3. Record merchandise, tax, shipping, and other charges separately as listed.
4. Save shipment and delivery records with the invoice, including any damage notes or correspondence.
5. Store the files in a secure folder and keep a backup. Use a neutral filename such as `2026-09-24-dealer-order-1842-invoice.pdf`.
6. Note where the original is stored and when you last checked the index.

If a shipment is delayed or arrives damaged, keep the related order and carrier records together. It does not replace the carrier's current requirements or the seller's terms.

## Digital files or paper invoices?

Use the method you can maintain reliably. A digital folder makes search and backup easier; a paper file can be useful if you already keep purchase records that way. Either method should preserve the original invoice and make it easy to find by date, dealer, or invoice number. If you keep both, make sure they refer to the same transaction and note which copy is the original. A simple index can use columns for purchase date, invoice number, dealer, product description, amount as listed, delivery reference, and storage location. Add a brief note when a document is corrected or a detail is confirmed by the seller. Review the index after each order so it remains a useful map to the source files.

Use access controls for digital folders, and avoid putting unnecessary personal or payment information in filenames or shared spreadsheets. Back up the records to a separate location you control. For a paper file, protect it from moisture and heat, and keep a simple index elsewhere so you can locate a document if the folder is misplaced.

## Review the record before filing it

Before closing an order, compare the invoice against the confirmation and receipt. Confirm that each amount is copied correctly and that missing details are marked instead of guessed. Add the delivery record when available, then file the source documents together. If a seller later corrects an invoice, retain the corrected copy and note which document supersedes it rather than silently replacing the history.
