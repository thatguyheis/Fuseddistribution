## claims
- CE1: First U.S. Mint commemorative coin, authorized in 1892 for the Chicago exposition. Source: https://www.usmint.gov/learn/coins-and-medals/commemorative-coins/columbian-exposition-half
- CE2: The act authorized no more than five million coins and connected payment to specified exposition preparation costs and conditions. Source: https://www.usmint.gov/learn/history/historical-documents/columbian-exposition-commemorative-coin-legislation
- CE3: Columbus profile on obverse, Santa Maria and two globes on reverse; Barber and Morgan design credits. Source: https://www.usmint.gov/learn/coins-and-medals/commemorative-coins/columbian-exposition-half
- CE4: The World's Columbian Exposition was planned to open in 1893, after Congress authorized the half dollar in 1892. Source: https://www.loc.gov/resource/gdcmassbookdig.guidetocolumbian00knig/
- CE5: The 1892 act authorized up to five million coins using uncurrent subsidiary silver, with proceeds tied to exposition labor and material costs and financial conditions set by Congress. Source: https://www.usmint.gov/learn/history/historical-documents/columbian-exposition-commemorative-coin-legislation
- CE6: The Mint calls the Columbian half dollar its first commemorative coin and identifies the 1893 Queen Isabella quarter as the next commemorative issue. Source: https://www.usmint.gov/learn/coins-and-medals/commemorative-coins/columbian-exposition-half
- CE7: Charles Barber designed the Columbus obverse and George Morgan the reverse showing the Santa Maria between two globes. Source: https://www.usmint.gov/learn/coins-and-medals/commemorative-coins/columbian-exposition-half
- CE8: The act supported exposition preparation expenses before opening, with payment conditioned on documented fair funding and eligible costs. Source: https://www.usmint.gov/learn/history/historical-documents/columbian-exposition-commemorative-coin-legislation
