The 1892 Columbian Half Dollar was authorized before the 1893 World’s Columbian Exposition to support exposition preparation expenses. Congress authorized the half dollar in 1892, with the intention of funding costs before the fair opened. The act stipulated that payment for the coins would be linked to documented fair funding and eligible costs. Specifically, the legislation authorized a maximum of five million coins. 

The act connected the proceeds from the sale of these coins to labor and material costs associated with the exposition. Furthermore, the act established financial conditions that Congress would need to meet before funds could be disbursed. This arrangement ensured that the Mint’s first commemorative coin would directly contribute to the fair’s development. 

The authorization of the 1892 half dollar was a key component of the overall plan. The act’s provisions were designed to provide immediate financial support for the preparations underway for the 1893 event. The Mint’s designation of the 1892 date records as working before the fair opened underscored this critical preparatory phase.
