Congress authorized the first U.S. Mint commemorative coin in 1892 specifically for the Chicago exposition. This coin, the Columbian half dollar, was intended to mark the 400th anniversary of Christopher Columbus’s arrival in the Americas. The Mint identified this half dollar as its inaugural commemorative coin, establishing a precedent for future U.S. Mint designs. Following the authorization of the Columbian half dollar, the Mint also designated the 1893 Queen Isabella quarter as the next commemorative issue. This sequence highlights the Mint’s deliberate planning for a series of commemorative coins linked to significant historical events. The 1892 act authorized up to five million coins, utilizing uncurrent subsidiary silver. The proceeds from the sale of these coins were to be directly linked to the preparation and expenses associated with the upcoming 1893 World’s Columbian Exposition.
