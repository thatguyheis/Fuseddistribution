Charles Barber designed the obverse of the 1892 Columbian Half Dollar, depicting a profile portrait of Christopher Columbus. This design choice reflected the exposition’s theme of exploration and the historical significance of Columbus’s voyages. Simultaneously, George Morgan was credited with designing the reverse. The reverse featured the Santa Maria, a ship central to Columbus’s first voyage, alongside two globes. This imagery underscored the global reach and ambition of the World’s Columbian Exposition. 

The two designers, Barber and Morgan, each contributed distinct elements to the coin’s design. Barber’s portrait provided a dignified representation of the historical figure, while Morgan’s depiction of the Santa Maria and globes visually communicated the exposition’s focus on international exchange and discovery. 

The act specifically identified Barber as the designer of the Columbus profile and Morgan as the designer of the reverse, showcasing the Santa Maria between the two globes. This attribution was crucial in establishing the coin’s connection to the exposition’s central themes and the commemorative nature of the issue. 

The 1892 date records, documenting the design credits, were established prior to the opening of the World’s Columbian Exposition, confirming the coin’s role in supporting exposition preparation.
