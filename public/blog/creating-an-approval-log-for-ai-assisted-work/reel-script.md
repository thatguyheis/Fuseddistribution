## OVERLAY: START NAME TASK PURPOSE

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 18s minimum

Narration: Start with a name for the task, its purpose, and the person responsible for the outcome. Add the date and the system or model used when that detail helps someone understand how the work was produced. Reference the input and source material rather than copying more personal or confidential information into the log than the reviewer needs.

---

## OVERLAY: WORK PAUSE FAIL PARTWAY

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 19s minimum

Narration: If work can pause or fail partway through, an exception handling log can track the owner and next action separately from content approval. Write down the checks that matter for the task. A reviewer might confirm that important claims match the current source, that the draft answers the request, and that it does not make an unauthorized promise.

---

## OVERLAY: CHECK THE LIMITATIONS

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 22s minimum

Narration: A log does not make an unsafe workflow safe by itself; the workflow still needs a responsible owner, appropriate controls, and a way to stop when evidence is missing. Record who reviewed the work and whether they approved it, requested a revision, or held it. Link or identify the exact approved version. A revision after approval is a new version and should not inherit the earlier decision automatically.

## OVERLAY: CHECK THE LIMITATIONS

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 20s minimum

Narration: A content hash can help identify a file precisely, but it does not establish that the file is accurate or that the reviewer had authority. Keep approval for the content separate from permission to take an action. Approving a draft does not by itself authorize sending it, changing a record, or making a commitment. State the allowed next step and who owns it.

---

## OVERLAY: CHECKS PERFORMED EVIDENCE CONSULTED

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 24s minimum

Narration: Checks performed, evidence consulted, and known limitations. Reviewer, decision, and the exact version approved. Authorized next action, follow-up owner, and retention location. Set access and retention rules for the record, especially when source material contains customer or employee information. Keep only what the business needs to explain and improve the decision. Use recurring corrections to find weak instructions, missing sources, or unclear review responsibilities. Update the process and check it again before reducing review.
