# Reel Script: A Better Way to Ask Your Network for Introductions
format: long-form
content-kind: educate
engagement-policy: 1

---
## HOOK
**Duration:** 4s
Narration: Stop asking friends for leads. Try this.

---
## OVERLAY: Make your ask easy to repeat
**Duration:** 18s
Narration: Tell people who you help, the problem you solve, and what a good introduction looks like. For example, say you help local contractors organize project schedules, and you are looking to meet owners who need help coordinating jobs. Then ask, does anyone come to mind? A clear sentence gives someone something useful to repeat.

---
## OVERLAY: Start with people who know your work
**Duration:** 20s
Narration: Make a short list of former customers, coworkers, suppliers, neighbors, friends, and family. Reach out one person at a time. Share what you are building, ask what they are working on, and listen before you ask for an introduction. Keep the conversation personal. A mass message can feel like a pitch, while a real check-in can reopen a useful relationship.

---
## OVERLAY: Offer help before you ask
**Duration:** 19s
Narration: Look for a way to be useful in return. Share a resource, answer a question, or connect two people who would benefit from knowing each other. Ask permission before sharing anyone's contact details. Thank people who make an introduction, follow up promptly, and let them know what happened when it is appropriate. Trust grows through thoughtful follow-through.

---
## OVERLAY: Meet people through shared interests
**Duration:** 20s
Narration: To meet new contacts, choose places where business owners already gather. Try a chamber event, trade association, neighborhood group, volunteer project, class, or community activity. Go more than once. Ask people what they do and what they are trying to solve. Offer one useful idea before talking about your own services. Then send a short note that mentions your conversation.

---
## OVERLAY: Track introductions, not business cards
**Duration:** 18s
Narration: Keep a simple monthly count of conversations, introductions, qualified leads, and customers. Note which activities led to follow-up and which relationships deserve more attention. Do not treat every conversation as a sales opportunity. A smaller network built on trust can be more useful than a long list of names you never contact again.
