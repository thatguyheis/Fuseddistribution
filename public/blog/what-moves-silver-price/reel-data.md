## reel-1
angle: lead_stat
hook: Silver fell 35 percent in March 2020. Then gained 140 percent in five months. Same metal. Same market. Here's why it moves that hard.
hook_type: contrarian_stat

### stats
- text: 2x GOLD'S VOLATILITY
  narration: Silver's annualized price volatility averages around 30 percent. Gold's averages around 15 percent. Both respond to the same macro environment, but silver amplifies the move in both directions. When metals rally, silver tends to rally harder. When they sell off, silver drops further.
- text: 124:1 RATIO PEAK
  narration: The gold-silver ratio tells you how many ounces of silver it takes to buy one ounce of gold. In March 2020 it hit 124 to 1, one of the most extreme readings in recorded history. Over the next 18 months it fell back toward 65 to 1 as silver outperformed gold sharply.

## reel-2
angle: concept
hook: The silver price is set by a market where paper contracts outnumber actual ounces of metal.
hook_type: contradiction

### stats
- text: 1B+ oz PAPER MARKET
  narration: COMEX open interest, the total active futures contracts on silver, routinely exceeds 1 billion ounces. Annual global mine supply runs around 800 to 850 million ounces. Most COMEX participants never take delivery of physical metal. They're traders using silver futures to hedge or speculate.
- text: 58% INDUSTRIAL FLOOR
  narration: 58 percent of all silver demand is industrial. Solar panels, electric vehicles, semiconductors, and medical devices consume silver that can't be easily substituted. That industrial demand creates a structural price floor that pure financial assets don't have. Deep selloffs driven by paper markets tend to be shorter-lived because industrial buyers step in.

angle: cta_direct
hook: If silver's volatility has ever made you consider selling, this is what you need to know first.
hook_type: pain_point

### stats
- text: ETF 550M oz PEAK
  narration: iShares Silver Trust alone held over 550 million ounces of physical silver at its 2021 peak. That's roughly 65 percent of annual mine supply in one fund. When large ETF inflows happen, custodians must buy physical silver. When money leaves, they sell. Those flows amplify whatever price move is already happening.
