## HOOK
Text: An LLC does not automatically lower silver taxes.
**Visual:** Topic intro shot
**Duration:** 8s
Narration: An LLC does not automatically lower federal tax on physical silver. The first question is how the LLC is classified.

---

## OVERLAY: START WITH OWNER AND ACTIVITY
**Visual:** Ownership record and silver beside it
**Duration:** 13s
Narration: Start with who owns the metal, whether it is investment or dealer inventory, and what activity occurs. Entity formation alone does not change investment silver into inventory or stock style tax treatment.

---

## OVERLAY: SINGLE MEMBER DISREGARDED
**Visual:** One owner connected to one LLC record
**Duration:** 13s
Narration: A single member LLC is generally disregarded for federal income tax unless it elects corporate treatment. If you still own the same investment silver, the result is often close to personal ownership. Keep basis and holding period records.

---

## OVERLAY: PARTNERSHIP REPORTING PRESERVES CHARACTER
**Visual:** Two owners and a partnership return
**Duration:** 13s
Narration: A multi member LLC usually defaults to partnership treatment unless it elects corporate treatment. That can change filing mechanics, including a Schedule K-1, but it does not automatically improve the tax category. The asset and each owner’s facts matter.

---

## OVERLAY: INDIVIDUAL SILVER COLLECTIBLES RULE
**Visual:** Lot dates, cost basis, and sale proceeds
**Duration:** 16s
Narration: For an individual investment holder, gain usually starts with sale proceeds minus adjusted basis. Silver held one year or less generally produces short term gain. Long term gains on silver bullion can face a maximum 28 percent federal rate. That is a rate on relevant net gain, not sale proceeds.

---

## OVERLAY: CORPORATE ROUTES NEED SEPARATE REVIEW
**Visual:** C corporation and S corporation paths
**Duration:** 14s
Narration: C corporation treatment adds entity tax and requires a separate analysis of distributions. S corporation treatment does not erase collectibles character: silver bullion remains identified in Schedule D instructions and shareholder reporting. Neither election guarantees savings.

---

## OVERLAY: TRANSFERS AND COSTS ARE FACT SPECIFIC
**Visual:** Transfer list, insurance, and filing calendar
**Duration:** 12s
Narration: Moving existing silver requires a contribution record, original acquisition dates, and basis tied to each lot. Price state fees, annual reports, bookkeeping, insurance, and filing work. A larger position alone does not establish a benefit.

**Duration:** 8s

Subtext: CLASSIFICATION, BASIS, OR COST
Narration: Before changing ownership, which fact would you verify first: classification, basis, or filing cost? Get the actual facts reviewed before you move the metal.
