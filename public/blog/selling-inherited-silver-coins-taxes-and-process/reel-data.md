## segments
- type: overlay text: CONFIRM AUTHORITY narration: Confirm whether the executor, trustee, or beneficiary is authorized to sell. Read the will, trust, appointment, and distribution documents before moving or pricing the coins. A family relationship alone does not settle authority.
- type: overlay text: INVENTORY THE LOT narration: Photograph the collection and assign group IDs. Record count, denomination, date, mint mark, face value, purity clue, weight, packaging, and condition. Preserve holders, and separate common coins from possible collector pieces.
- type: overlay text: CHECK BASIS DATE narration: Ask the executor for the estate’s valuation and basis records. Confirm the valuation date and whether an alternate valuation date was elected. Do not replace documented basis with today’s spot price. Map each lot to the estate inventory and keep the appraisal method and supporting documents. Common bullion and rare collectible coins can require different valuation approaches. Preserve the original records so the professional reviewing the sale can explain where each basis amount came from.
- type: overlay text: COMPARE OFFERS narration: Compare written offers that identify the items, pricing basis, fees, shipping, insurance, payment, and return terms. A dealer quote is a market offer, not automatically an independent appraisal.
- type: overlay text: RECORD THE SALE narration: Keep the signed authorization, invoice, shipping receipt, payment confirmation, and final settlement. Record gross proceeds and each fee separately, then reconcile the payment to the item IDs. For a hypothetical example, one thousand dollars of gross proceeds minus fifty dollars of permitted selling expenses and eight hundred dollars of documented basis leaves one hundred fifty dollars of gain. These are teaching figures. The actual tax rate and allowed expenses depend on the seller’s facts.
- type: overlay text: REVIEW TAX CHARACTER narration: Inherited property is generally treated as long term for holding period purposes. Collectibles can face a maximum 28 percent long term capital gain rate, while personal use losses and investment losses differ. Ask a tax professional to classify the sale.
- type: question text: Which sale record comes first? subtext: DOCUMENT THE SALE narration: Confirm authority, inventory the coins, preserve basis, compare written offers, and keep gross proceeds separate from fees. Which sale record will you complete first?
