## HOOK

**Visual:** Choose the right exit
**Duration:** 27s minimum

Narration: An old web page should not send visitors to a dead end or an unrelated sales page. Before retiring it, decide whether the information needs an update, has a relevant replacement, or is no longer available. The right answer depends on what the visitor came to do. Review the page against its source and the task it serves, not just how old the publish date looks.

---

## OVERLAY: Find pages that need attention

**Visual:** Start with the visitor’s task
**Duration:** 25s minimum

Narration: Look for pages about discontinued services, old processes or changed instructions. Ask what a visitor is trying to do and check the page against its source. Keep the URL, content owner, purpose, last verified date and proposed action in a small inventory. A date alone does not prove that a page is inaccurate. A page can remain accurate for years, while a newer page can become outdated after a service or policy change.

---

## OVERLAY: Update useful content

**Visual:** Verify before you update
**Duration:** 25s minimum

Narration: If the page is still useful, confirm the current information with its responsible owner and update it. If a page moved and has a close replacement, map the old URL to that destination. Google Search Central recommends a permanent redirect for a permanent move. The new page should answer the same need. Check that the redirect reaches its final destination and that the replacement serves the old page’s purpose.

---

## OVERLAY: Retire unavailable pages

**Visual:** Removed with no replacement?
**Duration:** 24s minimum

Narration: If content is gone and there is no relevant replacement, return a true not-found or gone status. Google distinguishes that case from a page that moved. A temporary search removal request does not change the underlying page or make its removal permanent. Check the source guidance before acting. If you request temporary removal from search, treat it separately from changing the page visitors can still reach.

---

## OVERLAY: Check links and record the decision

**Visual:** Verify destination and status
**Duration:** 24s minimum

Narration: Update your own navigation and links, then check the destination, response status and canonical URL after deployment. Keep a map of old URLs and replacements. Record who checked the content, why it changed and when it should be reviewed again. Involve the responsible owner when a page concerns a contract or safety instruction. Keep the old-to-new map with deployment notes so future edits do not create unexplained redirect chains.
