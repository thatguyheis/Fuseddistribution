---
## OVERLAY: Early payoff
**Duration:** 21s
Text: Close the loop you opened
Narration: The fix is to connect the closing action to the promise in the opening. Write three short lines: what you promised, where the video delivers it, and what the viewer can do next. If those lines describe different lessons, the ending needs repair before you add another transition, logo, or polished closing card to the export.

---
## OVERLAY: Make the problem visible
**Duration:** 21s
Text: Separate payoff from action
Narration: Use a fictional reel that promises to help someone write a useful AI video brief. Its payoff is the worked brief: audience, problem, result, and evidence. Its next action is to define the problem for the viewer’s own video. The payoff answers the lesson. The call to action asks the viewer to use that lesson once.

---
## OVERLAY: Review the middle
**Duration:** 22s
Text: One next step beats four asks
Narration: Now compare the number of decisions. Like, follow, share, and visit a link asks for four actions. Define the problem for your next video asks for one. These bars count requests in the two example endings. They are not conversion data and do not prove which ending will perform better. They make the difference in scope visible.

---
## OVERLAY: Keep the ending specific
**Duration:** 22s
Text: Show the connection
Narration: Show that connection rather than only announcing that the video is finished. Put the opening promise beside its payoff, then point to the next action. Use a clear comparison or a short flow diagram. Keep the text away from captions and social controls. A viewer should be able to understand the relationship without needing a decorative symbol explained.

---
## OVERLAY: Review the export
**Duration:** 22s
Text: Check the final frame
Narration: Finally, watch the exact exported file. Confirm the approved hook appears immediately, the body delivers it, and the final question is both spoken and visible. Keep one action on the closing card. After publishing, review retention moments for clues about what deserves another test, while remembering that a dip or spike alone cannot explain why someone watched or left.
