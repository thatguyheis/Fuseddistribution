## Content Brief: Making Accessibility Acceptance Criteria Practical

**Search Intent**
What specific, actionable steps can a local business owner take right now to make website accessibility criteria actually work for getting customers?

**Best Angle**
Stop reading technical accessibility reports. Here is the exact checklist you need to use to fix your website so Google and customers can find you.

**Competitor Gap**
Most articles list accessibility standards (like WCAG) without showing the business owner how to apply them to their actual website content and fix common errors. They focus on the "what" instead of the "how-to-fix-it-fast."

**Key Points (5 bullets)**
*   **Focus on Content First:** Ensure all your main pages (service descriptions, contact forms) are readable by screen readers, not just visually appealing.
*   **Check Your Forms:** Test your contact and inquiry forms. Are fields clearly labeled? Can a screen reader navigate the submission process easily?
*   **Image Alt Text is Non-Negotiable:** Every photo of your work or location needs descriptive alt text so search engines (and users) understand what it is.
*   **Simple Navigation Matters:** Test your main menu. Can someone using a screen reader easily jump from your homepage to your service page?
*   **Google Loves Good Websites:** When you fix these basic criteria, you improve your site's overall health, which helps Google rank you better locally.
