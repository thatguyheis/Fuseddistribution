## Search Intent
Local business owners want a straightforward, actionable checklist for writing website content *before* they hire a designer so they don't waste money on a site that doesn't work.

## Best Angle
Stop worrying about fancy design. Focus only on the content that gets local customers calling you right now.

## Competitor Gap
Most articles focus on design aesthetics or technical SEO jargon. They miss the crucial step: understanding that website content needs to be written with a local business owner's voice, focusing purely on service, not features. They fail to address the fear that a designer will create something confusing or irrelevant to their service area.

## Key Points (5 bullets)
*   **Focus on Service, Not Features:** Write about the *outcome* for the customer (e.g., "We fix leaky roofs, saving you money," not "We use advanced waterproofing materials").
*   **Map Content to Local Searches:** Identify the exact phrases your local customers type into Google (e.g., "Plumber near [City Name]"). Ensure these phrases appear naturally in your service descriptions.
*   **Use Your Voice, Not a Robot:** Write like you talk to a neighbor. Be direct, empathetic, and solve their immediate problem. Avoid marketing fluff.
*   **Integrate Proof Early:** Weave in specific, real-world examples of local success (even anonymized case studies) to build immediate trust.
*   **Optimize for Phone Calls:** Every page needs a clear, easy-to-find way for someone to call *you* immediately, not just fill out a generic contact form.
