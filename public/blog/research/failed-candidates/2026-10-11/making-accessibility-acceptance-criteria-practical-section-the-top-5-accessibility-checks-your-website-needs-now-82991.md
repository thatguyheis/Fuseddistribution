Your website needs to work for everyone. It needs to work for people using screen readers, too. Don't just focus on making things look pretty. Focus on making sure your main pages are readable by screen readers. This means your service descriptions and contact forms have clear structure. If a screen reader can't read it, people can't use it.

Next, test your contact forms. Ask yourself if every field is clearly labeled. Can someone navigating by keyboard easily fill out your inquiry? If the form is a mess, potential customers leave. This isn't about fancy design. It's about making sure the actual way someone reaches you is simple.

Then, image alt text is non-negotiable. Every photo of your work or your shop needs a description. Tell search engines and users what the picture is. If you don't do this, Google won't understand what your photos mean. It’s a basic step that helps your site get seen locally.

Keep your main menu simple. Test your navigation with a screen reader. Can someone easily jump from your homepage to your specific service page? If the menu is confusing, people get lost before they even see your work. Fixing these basic checks improves your site's health. Google likes sites that are easy to use. That makes it easier for you to get found.
