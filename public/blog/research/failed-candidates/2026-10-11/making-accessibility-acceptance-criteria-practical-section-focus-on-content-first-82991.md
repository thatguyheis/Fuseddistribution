Focus on content first. Your service pages and contact forms need to be readable by screen readers. It's not just about making things look pretty. People with disabilities use these tools to find you. If your page is hard to read, Google won't show it well.

Check your forms. Test your contact and inquiry forms. Are the fields clearly labeled? Can someone navigating with a screen reader find the submit button easily? If it’s confusing, people leave. You need to make sure the process of reaching you is simple for everyone.

Image alt text is non-negotiable. Every photo of your work or location needs a clear description. Search engines and users need to know what the picture is about. Don't just name the file; tell Google what the photo shows.

Keep your navigation simple. Test your main menu. Can someone using a screen reader jump from your homepage to your service page without getting lost? Clear paths help Google understand where you are.

When you fix these basic criteria, you improve your site's health. Better site health helps Google rank you better locally. Stop worrying about fancy features. Fix the basics, and you start seeing real results.
