Your website isn't just a brochure. If it’s hard for someone to find what you do, you’re invisible to local customers. Google needs to read your site, and if it can’t, you won't show up when people search for your service in your area.

Start with your main pages. Make sure your service descriptions and contact forms are clear for everyone to read, including those using screen readers. Don't just make things look pretty. Make sure the important information is accessible.

Test your contact forms. Are the fields labeled clearly? Can someone navigate the submission process easily? If a screen reader can’t follow your form, you’re losing potential leads right there.

Every picture on your site needs alt text. If you have a photo of your shop or a job site, describe it. This helps Google understand what the image is. It also helps people who rely on screen readers see what you’re showing.

Keep your main menu simple. Can someone using a screen reader jump straight to your service page from the homepage? A clear menu helps people find what they need fast.

Fixing these basics improves your site's health. When you make your site accessible, you help Google see you as a real business in your community. That’s how you start getting found locally.
