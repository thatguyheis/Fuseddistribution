# Audience

## Local Business Owners

**Primary segment:** Local business owners, ages 35-60, service-based businesses (contractors, salons, restaurants, clinics, trades). They aren't tech experts. They are frustrated because they have a website that doesn't lead to calls, and they feel stuck while competitors seem to rank better.

**Pain points (What they say):**
*   "I have a website, but nobody calls from it."
*   "I don't even show up when people search for my service."
*   "I don't know what I'm actually paying for with my current marketing."
*   "I asked Google how to get reviews, and I got a huge wall of confusing text."
*   "I don't have time to post on social media every single day."

**What they distrust:**
*   Agencies that promise top rankings without explaining the actual process.
*   Long contracts they can't easily leave.
*   Complicated dashboards and reports they can't understand.
*   Anyone who talks about "impressions" or "reach" instead of actual phone calls.

# Content Pillars

## UGC Prompting Principles

**Preferred formats:** Founder stories, Demos (these work best for skeptical small business owners).

**Authenticity cues for local business audience:**
*   Speak like a peer, not an expert looking down. Use phrases like, "I used to make this mistake too."
*   Use a concrete business type in your hook. Start with, "If you run a plumbing business..."
*   Demo format: Show the actual steps. Don't just talk about the concept.
*   Founder format: Explain your frustration with vague agency promises as your origin story.
*   Avoid technical terms unless you explain them simply.

**Forbidden words (Agency-speak):**
*   synergy, ecosystem, paradigm, digital transformation
*   guaranteed results, page one-one guaranteed (We can't promise results)
*   disrupt, innovate (These sound like startups, not local businesses)

# AI Summary Review Guide

## Why AI Summaries Can Lie (Testing an AI Summary Against Its Source)

AI summaries aren't always accurate. They can make things up. You need to check what the AI says against the real source document.

You gotta check the main numbers in the summary. Find the figures or names the AI mentions and go back to the source to confirm them. Don't just trust the numbers the AI pulls out.

Make sure the summary keeps the original meaning. The AI shouldn't change what a section actually means. It pulls facts, it doesn't rewrite the idea.

Trace the summary back to the exact spot in the document. See which paragraph or section the AI pulled that specific sentence from. That shows you where the info came from.

Watch the tone. Does the summary sound different from the original writing? If the tone shifts a lot, that signals the summary isn's solid.
