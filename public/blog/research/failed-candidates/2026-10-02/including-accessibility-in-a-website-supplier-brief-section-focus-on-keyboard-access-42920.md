Focus on Keyboard Access. You need a website that works for everyone. Many sites are impossible to use if someone can't use a mouse. You have to demand that your web supplier proves they can navigate your whole site using only the keyboard. This is a must for many users.

You must check for image descriptions. Every meaningful picture needs alt text. This lets screen readers tell people what the image is about. It makes your site usable for people with different needs.

Color contrast is vital. You need high contrast for text and buttons. This helps people with visual impairments read what you’re saying. It’s not just about following rules. It’s about getting more customers.

Keep your navigation simple. The site map and menus have to be clear. Avoid confusing menus that trip up screen readers. Make sure the structure is logical.

Accessibility isn't just a chore. It expands who can find you. People with disabilities are a big part of your potential market. Ask your supplier how making the site accessible can actually bring in more leads for your business.
