Check for Image Descriptions

You need to make sure your website is usable by everyone. Many people rely on screen readers to see what's on your page. If you don't give those tools descriptions for your photos, they can't understand what the picture is showing. This is especially important for businesses where visuals matter, like showing off your work or your shop.

You must check if your supplier includes proper alternative text for every important image. This text tells the screen reader what the image is about. Don't just upload a picture and hope for the best. Make sure the text describes the image clearly. This helps people with visual impairments use your site.

Also, contrast is key. Make sure the text color stands out clearly against the background color. Users with low vision need that difference to read easily. Ask your supplier to meet specific contrast ratios for all text and buttons. This keeps things simple and clear for everyone.

Don't forget the navigation. Your menu needs to be straightforward. If your site map is confusing, users relying on screen readers get lost. Keep your navigation logical and consistent. Make sure your site is easy to move through from start to finish.

Think about accessibility as a way to get more customers. When you make your site accessible, you open up access to more people. Ask your supplier how making your site accessible can actually help you get more calls and business. It's not just a rule to follow. It's about opening doors to new customers.
