You're spending money on a website that just sits there. Don't wait until you’re losing leads to worry about accessibility later. Building a site that excludes people is a costly mistake.

You need to tell your website supplier exactly what you need. Start by demanding keyboard access. Show them they can navigate your whole site without a mouse. This is a basic need for many people. Then ask for image descriptions. Every picture needs text for screen readers to understand it.

Color contrast matters too. You must set minimum contrast ratios for text and buttons. This helps users with vision problems. A messy site is hard to use, and that stops calls from coming in.

Keep your navigation simple. Make sure the menu makes sense. If it confuses screen readers, people won't find what they need. Accessibility isn't just a rule you follow. It opens up new customers who can't use a mouse. It’s about getting more people to see your business.

If your site isn't accessible, you're leaving potential clients at the door. Fix this now.
