## Why Accessibility Isn't Just a Nice-to-Have for Local Leads

Why Accessibility Isn't Just a Nice-to-Have for Local Leads

You’re worried about getting calls, right? You focus on what sells. But accessibility matters for finding customers. If your website is hard to use, people leave. That’s simple. Many people use screen readers, which are tools for folks who can’t use a mouse. If your site isn't built that way, those users can’t find your service. You miss out on a whole group of potential clients.

You need to check if your website works with a keyboard. Ask your supplier if they can navigate the whole site without a mouse. This is a basic need for many visitors. If they can’t do that, your site blocks out half your potential search traffic. It’s about making sure everyone can see what you offer.

Image text is another thing. If you use photos of your work or your shop, make sure they have descriptions. These descriptions help screen readers tell people what the picture is about. It helps them understand your business instantly. Don't just upload a picture and hope for the best.

Contrast is also key. Make sure your text stands out clearly against the background. People with visual issues need that difference. Simple navigation helps too. Your menu should be clear and easy to follow. Confusing menus confuse everyone. Accessibility isn't just for compliance; it’s about opening up more doors for your local customers.

## Essential Accessibility Checks for Your Website Supplier Brief

When you talk to a website supplier, you gotta ask about accessibility right away. Don't just ask if they meet some generic standard. You need concrete proof they can make your site usable by everyone. Start by demanding they prove they can navigate your whole site using only the keyboard. Most people forget this is a huge part of the experience. If your site is hard to use with a keyboard, people, especially those with mobility issues, can't find what they need.

Next, check how they handle images. You need suppliers to include proper alternative text for every important picture. This text tells screen readers what the image is about. It helps people who can't see the image understand your content. Don't just accept a picture. Make sure the supplier builds that description in. This isn't just compliance; it’s about making sure potential customers can actually see what you offer.

Color contrast matters too. You must tell the supplier to check the contrast ratios for your text and buttons. If the text is too light on a dark background, people with vision problems can't read it. Specify minimum contrast levels. This keeps your site clear for everyone.

Your supplier should also organize your site simply. Ask them to show you their site map and navigation. It needs to be clear and consistent. Avoid confusing menus. If the navigation makes sense to a screen reader, it’s easier for everyone to use.

Don't see accessibility as just a chore. See it as opening up new customers. Ask your supplier how making your site accessible can actually help you get more calls or sign-ups. Frame it as adding more potential business, not just fixing a technical problem.
