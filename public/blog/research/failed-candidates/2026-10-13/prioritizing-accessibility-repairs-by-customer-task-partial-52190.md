# prioritizing accessibility repairs by customer task

You need to prioritize accessibility repairs based on customer tasks. This means you should look at the specific job a customer is requesting and plan your repair schedule around it. We'll show you how to manage your workload effectively with this approach.

## Defining Accessibility Repair Tasks

You need a clear system for sorting customer requests. You must segment each request into specific accessibility repair categories. Think physical barrier removal. Think about assistive technology integration. Think about environmental modifications. This separation lets you see exactly what kind of work you have.

Next, you need a way to score each task. Create a matrix for this. Score tasks based on how complex they are. Score based on the specific skills your team needs. Score based on how much this repair affects the customer's ability to use a space. This scoring helps you see what matters most right now.

Then, you map these tasks to your team's abilities. Draw a visual chart. Show which repairs fit your current skill set. Show which tasks fit your available work time. This stops you from taking on too much work at once.

Your customer's stated goal must guide the initial placement. Document exactly what the customer wants to achieve. This dictates where their task goes in the queue. High urgency, high impact fixes get placed first.

After assigning a task, you must check your process. Review the logic you used for that assignment. See if you need to change how you sort the next set of customer requests. This loop keeps your prioritization accurate.
