You need a system to manage accessibility repair requests effectively. First, you must segment customer requests. Sort every incoming message into clear repair categories. Think about physical barrier removal, assistive technology integration, or environmental modification. This sorting step sets the foundation for everything else.

Next, you'll create a scoring matrix. This matrix will score each task. Score it based on how complex the repair is. Also, score it based on the specialized skills your team needs. Finally, score it based on the potential impact on the customer's access. This scoring helps you see what needs immediate attention.

Map your team's capacity. Make a visual chart showing what skills your team has now. Overlay this with your available time slots. This prevents you from taking on too much work at once. You want to match the task complexity with your current team ability.

When a customer submits a request, document their stated goal. This is their task. Use that goal to place the request in the queue right away. High urgency and high impact accessibility fixes go first. This ensures the most critical needs get addressed immediately.

After assigning a task, check your process. You must review the prioritization logic. Adjust the workflow for the next set of customer tasks. This keeps your prioritization accurate and responsive to real customer needs.
