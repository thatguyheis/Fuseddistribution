Matching Customer Needs to Repair Capacity

You need a way to sort incoming requests. First, you should segment customer needs. Classify each request into specific repair types. Think about physical barrier removal, integrating assistive technology, or making environmental changes. This separation lets you see exactly what kind of work you have.

Next, you must score the complexity of each task. Create a scoring system. This matrix weighs complexity, the skill set required, and how much the fix impacts the customer's access. This helps you gauge the real difficulty of the job.

Then, map these tasks to your team's actual capabilities. Build a visual tool. Show which repair types fit your current staff skills and your open time slots. This stops you from taking on too much work at once.

Crucially, align the customer's stated goal with the repair queue. Document the customer's specific task. This dictates where the repair goes first. High-urgency, high-impact accessibility fixes get priority placement in the queue.

After assigning a task, you must check your process. Make a required review step. Look at your prioritization logic. Adjust the workflow for the next set of customer tasks based on what you learned. This keeps your repair process effective.
