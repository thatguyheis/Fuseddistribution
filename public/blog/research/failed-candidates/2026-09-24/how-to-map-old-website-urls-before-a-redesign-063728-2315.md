## Why You Need to Map Old URLs Before Redesign

You've got an old website. It’s full of pages that don't do anything. You can’t afford to keep everything. Your new site needs to focus on what actually gets you a phone call or a quote. Forget pages just for looking nice. You need to spot where you used to grab a number or an email. Those are your main pages. Keep those front and center.

Look at your old URLs. Match each one to a specific service you offer. If that old page talks about a service you don't do anymore, ditch it. That page is dead weight. You want one clear page for each main thing you sell. Make sure the service matches the page exactly. This keeps things simple for you and for the people searching.

Check your contact details on every old page. Make sure the phone number and address are still correct. Don't just delete the page without double-checking the info. If the info is wrong, you’ll just end up looking messy. Map out what’s useful. What pages actually help people book you or ask for a price? That’s what you keep.

## Step 1 Identify Pages That Actually Get Calls

Step 1 Identify Pages That Actually Get Calls

Stop looking at every page on your old website. You only need to keep the pages that actually get you a phone call or a quote. Forget the pretty brochure pages. Focus on what moves a customer to act. That means pages that have a clear "Call Now" button or a form asking for a specific service.

## The Keep Merge or Delete Decision Matrix


You got a lot of old pages. Your website has pages for everything you ever did. This is a problem. You can't keep everything. You need to decide what stays and what goes. Focus on what actually gets people to call you or request a quote. Pages that just look nice are noise. They don't move your business forward.

First, look for the pages that actually work. Find where people used to call or fill out a form. These are your lead magnets. Keep those pages. They're your direct path to a customer. Everything else is probably dead weight. It just wastes time and confuses search engines.

Next, map your old URLs to your specific services. If a page is just talking about something you stopped doing, delete it. If a page is for a service you still offer, make sure it’s clear and strong. Don't let customers get lost looking for information.

See if you have several pages describing the same thing. Combine them into one clear page. Don't have three different pages for "Plumbing Services." One strong page is better than three weak ones. This makes it easier for Google to understand what you do.

Finally, check the contact info on every page you keep. Make sure the phone number and address are right. If the details are wrong, delete the page. Don't leave people wondering if they should call you. Keep it simple and focused on getting the job done.
