## What is a Canonical URL and why should you care

What is a Canonical URL and why should you care

When you change your website domain, Google needs to know which version of your site is the main one. This is where canonical URLs come in. Think of it as a specific instruction telling search engines, "This new address is the real one." If you don't tell them, Google gets confused. It might keep showing people the old link instead of your new one.

You need to check your code to see if Google is still pointing to the old URL. Use Google Search Console for this. It shows you exactly what Google sees. You also need to test direct links. Try typing both the old domain and the new domain into your browser. Make sure both load the way you expect. They should point to the right place.

Keep an eye on Search Console for any red flags. Look for reports that show a 404 or a redirect loop right after you make the switch. This tells you immediately if something went wrong. Focus on what your customer sees. Your main business pages need to load fast and look normal for them. Search engines care about that too.

Don't wait for problems to show up. Check these steps within 24 hours of switching domains. Catching these issues early saves you time and keeps your customers finding you.
