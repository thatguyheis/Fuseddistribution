# handing accessibility findings to a new website provider

You need to know how to hand accessibility findings to a new website provider. This is the exact business task we're discussing today. You have the reports. You need to pass them on clearly.

We'll walk through the steps. You'll learn how to present the accessibility issues. You'll make sure the new team understands what needs fixing.

## Understanding the Accessibility Landscape Before Handover

Understanding the Accessibility Landscape Before Handover

You need to set clear rules for the new website provider. You must tell them the exact accessibility standard you expect them to meet. This usually means specifying WCAG 2.1 AA compliance. This detail limits what you can find and what you can report. It keeps the scope of the review tight and focused.

Next, you must sort your findings by severity. Group the issues into Critical, Serious, and Minor categories. This lets the provider know what needs immediate fixing and what can wait. This structure helps them plan their work order efficiently. They can tackle the major roadblocks first.

For each problem you report, show them exactly what is wrong. Don't just send an error code. Send specific screenshots or video clips. This proves the issue exists. You must also offer a fix. For every finding, give them a concrete suggestion. This could be a piece of code or a direct instruction for their developers.

Finally, you need a way to talk about fixes. Set up a plan for when they send you their remediation steps. Define how often you will check their progress. This creates a cycle. You and the provider work together to make sure the website meets the required standards.
