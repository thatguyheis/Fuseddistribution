Structuring Your Accessibility Findings for Clarity

First, you must define the exact standard. You need to tell the new website provider which WCAG version they must meet. For example, you specify WCAG 2.1 AA. This sets the exact scope of what you are reporting. This clarity stops guesswork for the provider.

Next, you need to sort your issues by severity. Organize the handover document into distinct groups. Use categories like Critical, Serious, and Minor. This lets the provider know what needs immediate attention and what can wait. This helps them prioritize their fixing tasks.

For each issue, show them exactly what the problem looks like. Don't just send a report. Send specific screenshots or short videos. Show them the exact location of the failure. This stops them from having to guess where the problem is occurring on the site.

Don't just point out the flaw. Give them what to do about it. For every finding, include a suggestion for a fix. This could be a specific code change or a technical approach. This makes the remediation process much faster for their team.

Finally, set up a clear way to talk about fixes. Decide how often you will check in. Set a format for them to send you their plans. This creates a cycle. You work together on the fixes. This keeps the process going until everything is right.
