Establishing the Threshold When to Commit to Production

You need to separate what your pilot proved from what your production plan promises. The metrics that worked in the pilot show feasibility. These are things like user engagement or initial concept validation. These metrics tell you if the idea can actually happen. They do not show sustainability. They do not show how you handle real-world volume.

Production promises require different proof. You must test capacity stress. Don't just test pilot volume. You need to test your supply chain and infrastructure at ten times or one hundred times the pilot load. You must see if your systems break under real pressure. This shows if you can handle scale.

Your cost structure changes too. Pilot costs look different. They often hide fixed overhead. Production plans must show the true cost per unit at high volume. Factor in economies of scale. Calculate the actual marginal cost of goods sold under full operation. This separates promising trends from viable business models.

Map your pilot failures. Identify every potential problem area. Equipment failure, quality control bottlenecks. You need specific plans for these issues before you start mass production. Don't treat these as surprises. Develop pre production fixes for every known weak spot.

Build a clear decision framework. Compare the pilot success metric directly to your production plan. Ask yourself this question. Does the pilot success translate into a predictable, repeatable, and cost effective production method? Or is it just a hopeful trend? Only commit when the answer is yes.
