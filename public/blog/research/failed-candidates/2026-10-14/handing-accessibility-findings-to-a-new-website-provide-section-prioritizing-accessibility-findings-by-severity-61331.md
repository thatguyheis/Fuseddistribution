Prioritizing Accessibility Findings by Severity

You need to tell the new website provider what to fix first. You must define the exact standard. Specify which WCAG version they must meet. For example, you require 2.1 AA compliance. This sets the boundary for all your checks.

Next, you structure the handover document. You categorize every issue. Label them Critical, Serious, or Minor. This lets the provider sort their work. Critical items need immediate attention. Serious issues require planning. Minor items can wait for later stages.

For each finding, show them exactly what you mean. Don't just send an error code. Send specific screenshots or short videos. Prove the problem exists. This removes all guesswork for their developers.

Also, give them solutions. Don't just state the problem. Tell them how to fix it. Provide concrete code examples or technical suggestions. This guides their team's actual work.

Finally, set up a review rhythm. You need a plan for checking progress. Decide how often they report back. Define the format for their remediation plans. This keeps the process a cycle. You check their fixes, they check yours.
