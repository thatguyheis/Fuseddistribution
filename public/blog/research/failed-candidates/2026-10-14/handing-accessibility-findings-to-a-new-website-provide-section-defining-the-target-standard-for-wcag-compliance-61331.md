Defining the Target Standard for WCAG Compliance

You need to set a clear benchmark for your new website provider. You must state the exact Web Content Accessibility Guidelines version they need to meet. For example, you should specify WCAG 2.1 Level AA. This decision limits the scope of what you report. It tells the provider exactly which accessibility requirements they must satisfy.

Next, you must organize the issues by their impact. Group your findings into Critical, Serious, and Minor categories. This structure helps the provider know where to focus their immediate attention. Critical issues require urgent fixes. Serious problems need dedicated time for resolution. Minor issues can be addressed later in the development cycle.

For each finding, you must show proof. Don't just give an error message. Show a specific screenshot or a short video clip. This proves the accessibility barrier exists on the live site. Follow this proof with a direct suggestion. Tell them exactly how to fix it. Give them a piece of code or a clear technical direction.

Finally, you need a way to keep working together. Establish a plan for how the provider will report back. Decide on a regular schedule for receiving their proposed fixes. This creates a cycle. You review their plans. They implement the changes. This process keeps the website accessible over time.
