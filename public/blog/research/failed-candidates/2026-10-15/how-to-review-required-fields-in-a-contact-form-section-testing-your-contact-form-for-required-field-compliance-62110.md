Testing Your Contact Form for Required Field Compliance

You need to check how your form handles empty fields. First, you must define why each piece of information is necessary. Think about your business process. Why do you need a phone number? Is it for immediate callback? Write down the reason for every required field. This clarity guides your setup.

Next, you must look at your form builder settings. Check the specific options in your platform. Gravity Forms or Typeform have their own ways to set field requirements. You need to know exactly where to adjust the settings to make sure the system enforces the rule. This is how you actually make the requirement work.

Then, you need to test the entire user path. Act like a visitor. Try submitting the form with nothing filled out. See if the system stops the submission. If it lets you go through, you've got a problem. You need to confirm that the form correctly blocks submission when a required field is left empty.

Keep required and optional fields separate. Use clear labels to tell visitors what must be provided. This helps them understand what is essential for a successful contact. Finally, review your list of required fields. Do you ask for the same piece of data twice? Removing this redundancy makes the form easier to complete.
