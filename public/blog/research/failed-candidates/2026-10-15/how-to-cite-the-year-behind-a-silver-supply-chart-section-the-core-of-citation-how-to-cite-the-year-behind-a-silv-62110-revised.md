When you show a silver supply chart, you have to state the exact year the data was gathered. Don't just point to the graph. You need to say something like, "This chart shows silver supply data collected in 2024." This tells your reader precisely when the information was recorded. Direct attribution is key.

Next, you must link the visual to the original source. Tell people where you got the chart. Did you pull it from a specific financial database? Name that database. This shows your work is based on verifiable facts. You can use APA or MLA styles for referencing historical silver supply documents. Check your guide for the right format for referencing these kinds of data.

The year provides context for the supply and demand you present. Knowing the year helps explain market shifts. A change in supply during 2024 tells a different story than a change in 2021. Keep the factual year separate from your own thoughts. Don't confuse when the data was collected with what you think it means.
