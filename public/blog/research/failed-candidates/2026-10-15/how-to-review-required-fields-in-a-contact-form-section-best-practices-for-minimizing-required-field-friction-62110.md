Define necessity first. Before making any field required, you must document why you need that data. State clearly what the information serves. For example, you might state, "We need an email address for follow-up communication." This clarity helps you justify the requirement to the user.

Understand your form builder's settings. Check the specific tools you use, like Gravity Forms or Typeform. These platforms give you the exact controls to set field requirements. Use their built-in logic to enforce what you need. This ensures the system handles the enforcement correctly.

Test the visitor experience. You have to test the form yourself. Pretend you are a visitor. Try submitting the form without filling in a required field. Make sure the system stops the submission. This confirms the enforcement works as you expect it to.

Keep required and optional clear. Label your fields plainly. Tell the visitor what must be filled out and what they can skip. This makes the form less intimidating. It shows them what the process demands.

Check for repeated questions. Look at all your required fields together. Are you asking for the same piece of data in several places? Asking for the same thing multiple times creates extra work. Remove any redundant questions. This reduces the effort needed to complete the form.
