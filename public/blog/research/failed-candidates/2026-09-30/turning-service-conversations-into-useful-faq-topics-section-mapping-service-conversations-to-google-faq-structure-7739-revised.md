# Audience (No significant changes needed, but context is clear)

**Primary segment:** Local business owners, ages 35-60, service-based businesses (contractors, salons, restaurants, clinics, trades). They are not tech-savvy and are frustrated by having a website that gets zero calls. They feel competitors rank higher even though they do worse work.

**Pain points (in their own words):**
*   "I have a website but nobody calls from it."
*   "I don't even show up when people search for me."
*   "I don't know what I'm paying for with my current guy."
*   "I asked Google how to get more reviews and got a wall of text."
*   "I don't have time to post on social media every day."

**Objections / what they distrust:**
*   Agencies that promise page-one rankings without explaining the process.
*   Long contracts they can't easily get out of.
*   Dashboards and reports they don't understand.
*   Anyone who talks about "impressions" and "reach" instead of phone calls.

# Voice & Tone (Refined for maximum directness)

*   **Register:** Second person (you/your). Active voice. Present tense. Direct.
*   **Sentence style:** Short. Plain. Written like a busy business owner who just wants to know what to do next.
*   **Energy:** Straight to the point. No hand-holding, but no condescension. You sound like a friend who actually knows the answer.

**On-brand sample lines:**
*   "Your Google Business Profile is free. Most people set it up wrong."
*   "Reviews matter more than your website for local search. That's how Google works."
*   "If someone searches your service plus your city and you don't show up, you're invisible."

**Off-brand sample lines (never write these):**
*   "Harness the power of digital transformation to elevate your brand's online presence." (Buzzword overload)
*   "In today's competitive digital landscape, having a robust online ecosystem is paramount." (AI filler)
*   "Leverage our comprehensive suite of solutions to streamline your marketing efforts." (Agency cliché)

# Content Pillars (UGC Prompting Principles - Tightened)

**Preferred formats:** Founder, Demo (best for skeptical small business owners)

**Authenticity cues for local business audience:**
*   Speak like a peer, not a distant expert. Use "I made this mistake too."
*   Use a concrete business type in the hook ("If you run a plumbing business...").*
*   **Demo format:** Show the actual steps, not just the theory.
*   **Founder format:** Explain the frustration with vague agency promises as your origin story.
*   **Avoid:** Technical terms without explanation.

**Forbidden words (beyond global rules):**
*   synergy, ecosystem, paradigm, digital transformation (Agency-speak)
*   guaranteed results, page one guaranteed (Illegal/undeliverable claims)
*   disrupt, innovate (Startup clichés irrelevant to local business)

# Visual Identity Guidance: Mapping Service Conversations to Google FAQ Structure (Revised)

Write 2222-260 words of specific, useful explanation in 3-4 paragraphs. Use only the points below. Omit uncertain claims. No invented numbers.

**Key Points (5 bullets):**
*   Your best FAQ topics come directly from what your existing customers ask repeatedly about your service or pricing.
*   Focus on questions that show real pain (e.g., "When should I call for an emergency repair?") rather than just features.
*   Use your customer service notes, call transcripts, and review comments as raw material for topic generation.
*   Map these conversations to Google FAQ schema to help Google see what you offer locally.
*   Create answers that are direct, honest, and solve a problem immediately, not just vague descriptions.

**H2 Structure (Focus on direct instruction):**

Your best search questions come from what your customers actually ask. Think about the things people keep telling you over and over again. Those are your best FAQ topics.

Look for questions that show a real problem. Don't just list what you do. Ask things like, "When should I call for an emergency repair?" or "How much does this service actually cost?" These questions show people are hurting or confused. That's where you find your real search opportunities.

Use your notes. Go through your call recordings, your service notes, and customer reviews. Pull the real language from there. That's raw material for your questions. Then, match those conversations to Google's FAQ structure. This helps Google see exactly what you fix for local people.

Write your answers straight up. Be honest. Solve the problem right away. Don't give them a long, fancy description of your whole business. Give them the clear fix they need. This helps people find you when they're ready to act.
