You need to feed your AI the right kind of training data. Don't just dump random questions. Focus on what actually happens when someone needs your specific service. Think about real conversations.

For a plumbing business, don't just ask for "customer questions." Ask about scheduling a repair, or what to expect when a leak happens. Show the AI how to talk about those specific problems. If your salon gets complaints about a specific color or service time, put those in there. This teaches the AI to actually understand the frustration.

You also need to show it the bad stuff. Include questions where people are confused, angry, or just talking about something totally unrelated. This helps the AI learn what to avoid saying. It learns the difference between a real customer need and a distraction.

Use the language your customers use. If your local tradespeople use certain terms, write your examples that way. This makes the AI drafts sound like they were written by someone in your community, not some distant corporate scriptwriter.

Test what you write. Show the AI the examples, see what it spits out. If it sounds off, fix it. This back and forth is how you get the AI to sound exactly like your business when it drafts responses.
