## OVERLAY: READ THE DATED FORECAST

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 21s minimum

Narration: A silver supply deficit describes a market balance over a specified period. It does not mean every dealer has run out of coins, and it does not provide a timetable for the next price move. Identify who produced the estimate, which year it covers, what its categories include, and whether the number is historical or forecast. Keep those details beside the headline when you use it.

## OVERLAY: SEPARATE FORECASTS FROM RESULTS

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 21s minimum

Narration: The Silver Institute's April 15, 2026 release forecast a market deficit of 46.3 million ounces for 2026. It projected total demand of 1.11 billion ounces, lower industrial demand, and broadly flat mine production. Those are the expectations published on that date, not measured results for the whole year. A later article repeating the figure does not establish that the underlying estimate has been updated.

## OVERLAY: CHECK WHAT THE TOTALS INCLUDE

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 21s minimum

Narration: Read the table headings and notes before combining numbers from different summaries. A mine production figure is not automatically a total supply figure, and one industrial category is not total demand. Use a single report's definitions for the calculation you want to understand. If another source handles investment flows differently, identify that difference before comparing totals. Otherwise, you may count the same activity twice.

## OVERLAY: COMPARE ACTUAL RETAIL QUOTES

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 21s minimum

Narration: A market report cannot tell you the complete delivered price of a specific coin or bar at checkout. Collect actual seller quotes for equivalent products and quantities. Include payment costs, shipping, and any tax shown for your destination. Ask prospective buyers what they would offer for the same product and condition. A forecast deficit does not establish that you will recover a retail premium.
