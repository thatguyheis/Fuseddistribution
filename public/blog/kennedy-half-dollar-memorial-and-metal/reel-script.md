# Reel Script: How the Kennedy Half Dollar Became a Memorial and a Marker of Change
format: long-form
content-kind: educate

---
## HOOK
Narration: A new coin was authorized just thirty-eight days after President Kennedy was killed. How did the Mint turn a national memorial into a half dollar that would also mark a change in American money?

---
## OVERLAY: A MEMORIAL AND A NEW LAW
Narration: The story begins on November 22, 1963. Days later, Mint Director Eva Adams asked Philadelphia Mint staff to put Kennedy on a silver coin. They weighed the quarter, half dollar and dollar; Mint engravers recalled selecting the half dollar in late November. The Franklin half had not completed its statutory 25-year design period, so Congress passed legislation to authorize the replacement.

---
## OVERLAY: PORTRAIT TAKES SHAPE UNDER A DEADLINE
Narration: Gilroy Roberts, the Mint’s chief sculptor, adapted a portrait already prepared for a Kennedy medal. Frank Gasparro designed the reverse with the presidential coat of arms. On December 17, Jacqueline Kennedy suggested portrait adjustments, and the revised design was approved on December 27. Mint engravers prepared trial pieces for review. The authorization became law on December 30, 1963.

---
## OVERLAY: MINT PRODUCTION AND FIRST DELIVERY
Narration: Proof dies arrived on January 2, 1964. Denver began regular production on January 30, and Philadelphia followed the next week. On February 11, both mints held ceremonies for the new coin. The Mint’s recollection records an initial delivery of about 26 million half dollars to the Federal Reserve on March 5, 1964, before later public distribution.

---
## OVERLAY: SILVER CONTENT CHANGES
Narration: The first Kennedy half dollars were struck in 90 percent silver. In 1965, Congress changed the metal content of circulating dimes, quarters and half dollars as silver grew scarce. The replacement half dollar used a silver-bearing outer layer and a copper-nickel core, bringing its overall silver content to 40 percent.

---
## OVERLAY: TWO STORIES IN ONE HALF DOLLAR
Narration: So the Kennedy half dollar carries two connected stories. Its portrait records a rapid national act of remembrance. Its metal records a separate pressure on the coinage system, as the United States changed how it made everyday money.
