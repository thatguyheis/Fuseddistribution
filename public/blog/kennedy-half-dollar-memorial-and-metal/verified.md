# How the Kennedy Half Dollar Became a Memorial and a Marker of Change

How the Kennedy Half Dollar Became a Memorial and a Marker of Change. The silver coin, instantly recognizable today, began its life as a poignant tribute and a reflection of a nation grappling with profound loss. Just weeks after the assassination of President John F. Kennedy on November 22, 1963, Congress swiftly moved to authorize the creation of a new half-dollar design, a decision driven by the urgency of the moment and the need to honor a fallen leader. This article will trace the remarkable, accelerated process by which the Kennedy Half Dollar was produced, detailing the key steps from initial design selection to its initial distribution. 

The Mint’s engravers began work on the new [design](/blog/walking-liberty-half-dollar-silver-eagle/) in late November 1963, adapting existing models to meet a demanding schedule. Just thirty days after the assassination, proof dies arrived on January 2, and regular production commenced in Denver and Philadelphia. The first ceremonies took place on February 11, marking the beginning of a project shaped by a revised design, influenced by Jacqueline Kennedy’s suggestions, and ultimately, by a shift in the coin’s composition.

## A memorial and a new law

How did the new half [dollar](/blog/silver-dollar-roll-hoards-hidden-gems/) become legally possible?

The selection of the Kennedy half dollar in late November 1963 occurred shortly before the December 30 authorization by Congress. This action was directly linked to the Franklin half dollar’s status. The Franklin half had not completed its 25-year statutory design period, a requirement for a new design to be authorized. As a result, Congress passed legislation specifically authorizing the new design, paving the way for the Kennedy half dollar’s production. This timing was crucial; the Mint’s engravers selected the half dollar just weeks before the December 30 approval. The authorization provided the legal framework for the Mint to proceed with the design and production of the new coin.

## The portrait took shape under a deadline

Gilroy Roberts designed the obverse, and Frank Gasparro the reverse of the Kennedy half dollar. Mint engravers’ recollections describe this process as adapting existing models during the accelerated project. This meant they worked quickly to finalize the design, responding to the urgent need for a commemorative coin. Jacqueline Kennedy suggested portrait adjustments on December 17, 1963, following her husband’s assassination. These revisions were then approved on December 27, before the December 30 act was formally passed. 

The Mint’s records detail that these adjustments were made to the portrait design. The revised design received approval before the legislation authorizing its production. This rapid turnaround highlights the compressed timeline for creating the coin. 

The urgency of the situation, following President Kennedy’s death, influenced the design process. The Mint sought to quickly produce a memorial coin, and Jacqueline Kennedy’s input was incorporated within this timeframe.

## Proof dies, two mints, one schedule

Regular production of the Kennedy half dollar began quickly following the arrival of proof dies on January 2. Within thirty days, coinage commenced in Denver, and the following week, production began in Philadelphia. These separate minting operations were coordinated by a single production schedule. The Mint’s recollection details a phased rollout, with Denver initiating production on January 30 and Philadelphia joining the effort the subsequent week. Finally, ceremonies took place on February 11, marking the official launch of the new coin. This rapid production schedule was necessitated by the passage of the half-dollar act on December 30, which authorized the new design after the Franklin half dollar’s design period had expired.

## The first delivery reached the Federal Reserve

The Mint’s recollection records an initial delivery of approximately 26 million Kennedy half dollars to the Federal Reserve on March 5, 1964. This delivery represents the first official distribution of the new coin following its production. It is important to distinguish this initial shipment from subsequent public distribution, which occurred over a longer period. The Mint’s records detail this specific date as the point at which the newly minted coins began their journey toward circulation. This initial delivery was a key step in introducing the Kennedy half dollar into the nation’s monetary system.

## Silver content changed with the 1965 coinage

The 1964 half-dollar contained 90 percent silver, while the 1965 composition shifted to 40 percent silver. This change occurred amidst a documented silver shortage. The Mint adjusted the alloy to accommodate limited silver availability. This shift in composition reflects a deliberate response to a specific economic circumstance. The 1965 half dollar’s reduced silver content was a direct consequence of the challenges in securing sufficient silver for coinage production. This change occurred as regular production began in January 1964, and continued through February 1964.
