How did the new half dollar become legally possible?

The selection of the Kennedy half dollar in late November 1963 occurred shortly before the December 30 authorization by Congress. This action was directly linked to the Franklin half dollar’s status. The Franklin half had not completed its 25-year statutory design period, a requirement for a new design to be authorized. Consequently, Congress passed legislation specifically authorizing the new design, paving the way for the Kennedy half dollar’s production. This timing was crucial; the Mint’s engravers selected the half dollar just weeks before the December 30 approval. The authorization provided the legal framework for the Mint to proceed with the design and production of the new coin.
