The 1964 half-dollar contained 90 percent silver, while the 1965 composition shifted to 40 percent silver. This change occurred amidst a documented silver shortage. The Mint adjusted the alloy to accommodate limited silver availability. This shift in composition reflects a deliberate response to a specific economic circumstance. The 1965 half dollar’s reduced silver content was a direct consequence of the challenges in securing sufficient silver for coinage production. This change occurred as regular production began in January 1964, and continued through February 1964.
