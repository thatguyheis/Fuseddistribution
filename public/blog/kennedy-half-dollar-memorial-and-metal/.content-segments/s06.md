What changed because of this part of the story?

The initial delivery of approximately 26 million Kennedy half dollars to the Federal Reserve on March 5, 1964, marked a critical shift in the coin’s composition. This delivery represented the final run of the 90 percent silver version, a design intended to preserve the coin’s value as a tangible store of wealth. However, the Mint’s recollection records foreshadowed a significant alteration. Just months later, the composition of the half dollar was reduced to 40 percent silver-bearing, a change implemented to address rising silver prices and a growing coin shortage. This alteration directly impacted the coin’s intrinsic value and reflected a broader shift in the government’s approach to managing the nation’s silver reserves.
