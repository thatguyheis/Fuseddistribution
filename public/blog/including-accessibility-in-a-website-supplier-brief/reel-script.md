## HOOK

**Visual:** Put access in the brief.
**Duration:** 29s minimum

Narration: Put accessibility in the website supplier brief by naming the pages and journeys in scope, the agreed evaluation target, the evidence you expect, and who will repair problems found before and after release. A general request to “make it accessible” leaves too much room for different interpretations of the work. W3C's planning guidance places accessibility within responsibilities, resources and ongoing management. The brief structure below is an original practical method for discussing those responsibilities with a supplier.

---

## OVERLAY: DESCRIBE THE WEBSITE WORK IN SCOPE

**Visual:** Describe the website work in scope
**Duration:** 19s minimum

Narration: List the main user tasks and the parts of the site involved. Include forms, navigation, downloads, media and relevant third party tools. A supplier cannot make a useful proposal if the brief mentions only page count while the difficult interactions are hidden in a booking service or an embedded form.

---

## OVERLAY: AGREE ON A SPECIFIC EVALUATION TARGET

**Visual:** Agree on a specific evaluation target
**Duration:** 12s minimum

Narration: Ask the supplier to identify the accessibility standard, version, level and scope proposed for the project, and have an appropriately informed reviewer confirm that target. Write it into the brief.

---

## OVERLAY: MAKE CONTENT RESPONSIBILITIES EXPLICIT

**Visual:** Make content responsibilities explicit
**Duration:** 29s minimum

Narration: Specify who writes and approves headings, link wording, image descriptions and instructions. Ask who prepares captions or other media alternatives required by the agreed scope. These decisions affect the experience even when the underlying component code is well implemented. They need time and review ownership of their own. Provide representative content early enough for the supplier to test realistic pages. A demonstration built with short placeholder text may not reveal problems caused by the actual content.

---

## OVERLAY: DEFINE HOW FINDINGS BECOME REPAIRS

**Visual:** Define how findings become repairs
**Duration:** 30s minimum

Narration: Ask for an issue record that identifies the affected page or component, reproduction steps, observed behavior and expected behavior. Include the responsible owner and the result of the later verification. A list of scores or vague warnings is difficult to turn into an accountable repair process. Agree how severity and release decisions will be made. The supplier should not silently redefine a requirement because a fix is inconvenient, and the business should not assume every issue has the same consequence.
