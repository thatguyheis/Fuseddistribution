## OVERLAY: LONG TERM HOLDING GOAL

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 19s minimum

Narration: If long-term holding is the goal, larger bars give you more metal per dollar but less flexibility to sell in pieces. Spot price is the live market price for one troy ounce of silver. When you buy physical silver, you pay spot price plus a premium. The premium covers minting costs, dealer margin, and sometimes a brand markup.

---

## STAT: 1 SPOT PRICE CUSTOMERS SILVER

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 20s minimum

Narration: Before any purchase, ask the dealer two questions: what is today's spot price, and what is the exact premium on this product? If they can't answer both clearly, walk away. In our experience working with new customers, buyers who start with 1-oz silver coins almost always have a better first experience than those who go straight to 10-oz or 100-oz bars.

## STAT: 46 % BARS SILVER DEMAND DECLINE

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 20s minimum

Narration: The per-ounce premium is the lowest available, but these bars are harder to sell in portions and require more buyer trust. ## OVERLAY: SILVER PRICES FLUCTUATE PREMIUMS

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 17s minimum

Narration: Silver prices fluctuate, and premiums mean you need a meaningful price increase to break even on any purchase. Silver is a long-term asset, not a short-term trade. Dollar-cost averaging over months and years reduces your exposure to buying at a single price peak and gives your stack time to build real value.
