- text: 14% MAPLE LEAF PREMIUM

  narration: Canadian Silver Maple Leafs cost less than American Silver Eagles but more than rounds. Typical premiums run 10 to 18 percent over spot. They're universally recognized, .9999 fine silver, and resell easily worldwide. The premium is the tradeoff you pay for that recognition.

- text: .999 FINE SILVER PURITY
  explanation: Both silver rounds and silver coins are .999 fine silver -- the same purity. The metal inside is identical regardless of who minted it.
  graphic_type: none
  explanation: Both rounds and most government coins hit .999 fine purity. Some coins like the Maple Leaf reach .9999. The silver itself is the same. You're not getting better metal by paying a higher premium.
  narration: Here's the part most buyers don't realize. Silver rounds and silver coins contain the same metal. Both are .999 fine silver. The Canadian Maple Leaf hits .9999 -- slightly higher -- but the difference is negligible for a physical reserve. You are not getting better silver by paying a 20 percent premium on an Eagle versus a round.

- text: $300-$600 PREMIUM GAP AT 100 OZ
  explanation: Buying 100 oz of silver as ASEs versus generic rounds costs $300 to $600 more in premiums alone at a $30 spot price -- for identical silver content.
  graphic_type: none
  explanation: At 100 ounces, the difference between round premiums and ASE premiums adds up to $300 to $600 extra cost at a $30 spot price.
  narration: Let's do the math. At a $30 spot price, rounds cost about $31 to $32 per ounce. An American Silver Eagle costs $34 to $37. Over 100 ounces, that gap is $300 to $600 in extra premiums for the exact same amount of silver. That's real money for any serious reserve builder.

- text: 0 NUMISMATIC VALUE (ROUNDS)
  explanation: Silver rounds have zero collector value. They trade purely on silver content. Government coins can develop numismatic premiums worth multiples of the silver melt value.
  graphic_type: none
  explanation: Silver rounds carry no collector value and never will. Government coins can appreciate as collectibles beyond their silver content.
  narration: Silver rounds will never develop collector value. Their price tracks spot price -- nothing more. Government coins are different. Rare American Silver Eagle proof sets and low-mintage years sell for far above their silver melt value. If you want numismatic upside alongside physical silver, rounds aren't your answer.
