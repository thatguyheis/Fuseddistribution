## OVERLAY: Your domain and Gmail
**Visual:** Gmail is the inbox. Your domain controls the address after the at sign.
**Duration:** 24s
Narration: Gmail is the inbox. Your domain controls the address after the at sign. With Workspace, you can use an address like hello at yourcompany dot com in Gmail. The domain and the mail service are different pieces, so first make sure you control the domain and know who can edit its DNS records.

---
## OVERLAY: Choose the right address
**Visual:** Plan the address before you change mail.
**Duration:** 24s
Narration: Choose the address and account plan before you switch. Use an individual sign-in for each person. For shared role mail, such as support or sales, check the Workspace options with your administrator. Write down the existing addresses and current mail provider so nothing important is missed during the change.

---
## OVERLAY: Verify domain ownership
**Visual:** Use the record from Google Admin.
**Duration:** 24s
Narration: Next, follow the current Workspace setup wizard to verify domain ownership. Google provides a DNS record; add that exact value at the domain host, then confirm it in Admin. Verification proves you control the domain. It does not yet route incoming mail. Avoid copying old tutorial values when Admin provides current instructions.

---
## OVERLAY: Route Gmail delivery
**Visual:** Set MX records and activate Gmail.
**Duration:** 24s
Narration: After verification, create the users, update the domain MX records with the values shown in Google Admin, and activate Gmail. Existing mail routing may be affected, so coordinate the change and keep the old service available until tests pass. DNS updates can take time, so check after propagation instead of changing unrelated settings.

---
## OVERLAY: Test and protect the address
**Visual:** Send out, reply back, then review sender authentication.
**Duration:** 24s
Narration: Test from an outside inbox to the new address, then reply from the custom address. Check the sender, inbox, spam folder, and every important user. Google also recommends sender authentication such as SPF; configure that separately using current guidance. The sequence is domain, verification, accounts, MX, activation, and a real delivery test. Test role addresses too, and document who answers customer messages.
