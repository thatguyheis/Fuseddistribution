**Visual:** Supporting article visual

## OVERLAY: INDUSTRIAL PROCESSES PARTICULARLY INVOLVING

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 20s minimum

Narration: Industrial processes, particularly those involving sulfur-containing chemicals, release significant amounts of sulfur into the air. Volcanic eruptions also contribute sulfur dioxide, which can travel long distances and deposit on silver surfaces. Even seemingly innocuous things like decaying vegetation release small amounts of sulfurous compounds.

## OVERLAY: HIGH PURITY SILVER MINIMAL

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 20s minimum

Narration: High-purity silver, with its minimal copper content, tarnish more slowly and develop a more uniform patina. Preventing silver tarnish is far more effective than trying to remove it after it’s formed. Consistent, proactive measures are key to maintaining your silver’s value and appearance.

## OVERLAY: DEHUMIDIFIER BENEFICIAL AREAS PRONE

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 22s minimum

Narration: A dehumidifier can be beneficial in areas prone to dampness. If you’re storing silver in a closet or drawer, ensure the space is well-ventilated. Air Circulation: Just like humidity, stagnant air accelerates tarnishing. Ensure adequate air circulation around your silver. Don’t overcrowd storage containers, as this restricts airflow.

## OVERLAY: GOOD STARTING POINT SILVER

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 24s minimum

Narration: A good starting point is a silver polish containing rhodium, which creates a protective barrier against further tarnishing. Test the polish on a small, inconspicuous area first to ensure it doesn’t discolor the silver. Coating with Wax (for Long-Term Storage): For long-term storage, consider applying a thin coat of carnauba wax to the silver.
