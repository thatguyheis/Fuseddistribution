## HOOK

**Visual:** Silver can ride along
**Duration:** 26s minimum

Narration: Not every ounce of mined silver comes from a mine focused on silver. Some operations recover silver while producing copper, lead, zinc or gold. That connection matters when reading supply reports, because a change in another metal operation can affect what silver is recovered. It may be a secondary output rather than the reason a mine was developed.

---

## OVERLAY: Separate the production labels

**Visual:** Primary, byproduct, coproduct
**Duration:** 24s minimum

Narration: A primary silver mine focuses on silver as a main product. Byproduct silver is recovered while another metal is the main target. Coproducts are multiple products from an operation. The labels describe the relationship between outputs. They do not by themselves tell you the mine’s silver grade, revenue or future output. Reports may not use the labels identically, so check the source notes before treating categories from different tables as equivalent.

---

## OVERLAY: Follow ore through processing

**Visual:** One stream can yield several products
**Duration:** 24s minimum

Narration: Ore can contain several minerals. Mining and processing separate material into products or concentrates, and some operations recover silver from streams associated with another metal. The exact route depends on the deposit and facility. A headline about a copper mine does not tell you how much silver it recovered. A concentrate is intermediate material, not automatically refined silver ready for use.

---

## OVERLAY: Keep the measured stage clear

**Visual:** Mine output is not refined output
**Duration:** 24s minimum

Narration: Mine production, concentrate output, refined metal and recycled material are different measures. Check the report’s date, geography, definitions and units before comparing them. The USGS 2026 summary describes U.S. 2025 production categories. It is not a global percentage or a forecast. Refinery output may also include imported ores or scrap, so it should not be read as domestic mine output.

---

## OVERLAY: Avoid a simple price story

**Visual:** Multiple operating factors matter
**Duration:** 24s minimum

Narration: An operation’s decisions may depend on its main product, ore characteristics, processing capacity and site economics. Silver can be one factor among several. A byproduct share does not show that a mine can quickly raise silver output when prices change, and it is not an investment signal. A summary covers a specific reporting period and scope; it cannot predict each facility’s future choices.
