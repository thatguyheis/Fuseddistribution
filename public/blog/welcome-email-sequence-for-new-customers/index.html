Welcome Email Sequence For New Customers

July 6, 2026 Local Business Marketing

A welcome email sequence is the short series of emails a new customer gets right after they buy from you or join your list. Most local businesses never set one up. A customer hires them once, hears nothing for months, and forgets they exist. The sequence fixes that. It runs on autopilot, it takes an afternoon to build, and it works while you sleep. If you're brand new to email, start with our guide on getting started with email marketing, then come back here. At Fused Distribution we build simple marketing systems for local businesses, and this is one of the first things we recommend because the payoff is so far ahead of the effort.

Why the First Email Matters More Than Any Other

Welcome emails get opened. GetResponse benchmark data puts welcome email open rates above 50 percent, while a normal campaign to the same list might see 20. The reason is simple. The customer just did business with you. You're fresh in their mind, they expect to hear from you, and they actually want the confirmation. That window closes fast. Wait a week and you're a stranger again. Send the first email within minutes of the signup or the sale, not the next morning.

The Four Email Sequence That Works for Local Business

You don't need ten emails. Four is plenty for a plumber, a salon, a dentist, or a landscaper. Here's the structure we set up for clients:

  • Email 1, immediately: the thank you. Thank them for the job or the signup. Tell them exactly what happens next. Include your phone number and hours so they never have to hunt for them. Keep it under 100 words. No selling.
  • Email 2, day 2 or 3: who you are. One short story about your business. Why you started it, who runs it, what you care about. People hire people, not logos. End with one useful tip related to the work you just did for them.
  • Email 3, day 5 to 7: the review ask. This is the money email. They're still happy with the work, so ask now. Link straight to your Google review page and keep the ask to two sentences. Our post on getting Google reviews covers the exact wording that works.
  • Email 4, day 10 to 14: the reason to come back. A maintenance reminder, a referral offer, or a small discount on the next visit. Pick one. This email turns a single job into a repeat customer.

That's the whole sequence. Write each one once, load it into your email tool, and every new customer gets the same treatment without you lifting a finger.

Subject Lines and the One Rule Per Email

Plain subject lines beat clever ones. "Thanks for choosing Ramirez Plumbing" outperforms "You won't BELIEVE what's next!" every time. Write the subject like a text message to a neighbor. Then follow the one rule: each email asks for exactly one thing. One link, one action. An email that asks the reader to review you, follow you on Facebook, and book their next visit gets none of the three. Pick the single action that matters for that email and cut everything else.

Setting It Up Without a Tech Headache

Every mainstream email tool does this. MailerLite, Mailchimp, and Constant Contact all call it an automation or a workflow. You write the four emails, set the delays, and switch it on. The harder question is how people get onto the list in the first place. A signup form on your website, a checkbox on your invoice, or a simple ask at the end of a job all work. We wrote a full walkthrough on building an email list for your local business if that part is missing. Budget an afternoon for the whole setup. It's a one time job.

Mistakes That Kill a Welcome Sequence

The common failures are all avoidable. Selling hard in email 1 gets you deleted before trust exists. Sending daily burns the list out. Writing like a corporation ("Dear Valued Customer") tells people a robot is talking. Skipping the review ask wastes the best timing you'll ever get. And leaving the sequence unchanged for years means new customers get stale offers and wrong hours. Reread your four emails twice a year. That's the whole maintenance plan.
