How To Calculate Your Average Cost Per Ounce Silver

August 16, 2026 Silver Investing

Calculating your average cost per ounce of silver is crucial for understanding your investment returns and making informed decisions. At Fused Distribution, we stock a wide variety of silver bullion and coins, and we recommend careful tracking for all our clients. This guide will walk you through the process, step-by-step, ensuring you have a clear picture of your silver investment performance. Let’s get started.

What to Know About Silver Cost Tracking

Understanding your average cost per ounce isn’t just about knowing how much you paid for a particular purchase. It’s about establishing a baseline for evaluating your overall profitability. If you’re buying and selling silver regularly, this metric becomes even more important. It helps you determine if you’re consistently buying low and selling high, or if you’re experiencing losses. A consistent average cost per ounce provides a valuable reference point, especially when market fluctuations occur. It allows you to assess whether your investment strategy is working for you.

How to Get Started: Gathering Your Purchase Data

The first step is to collect all your silver purchase records. This includes:

  • Purchase Dates: Note the exact date of each transaction.
  • Purchase Price: Record the price you paid per ounce for each purchase. This might be the spot price plus premiums, or the price you paid through a dealer.
  • Quantity Purchased: Record the number of ounces purchased in each transaction.
  • Transaction Type: Distinguish between buying and selling.

Spreadsheets are ideal for organizing this data. You can use Google Sheets, Microsoft Excel, or any other program you’re comfortable with. A simple table with columns for “Date,” “Price Per Ounce,” and “Ounces Purchased” will suffice to begin. If you’re buying and selling frequently, consider using a dedicated investment tracking app.

Calculating Your Total Cost of Silver

Once you’ve compiled your data, it’s time to calculate the total cost of your silver holdings. Sum up the “Price Per Ounce” column for all your purchases. Then, multiply that total by the “Ounces Purchased” column to get the total cost of your silver. For example, let’s say you’ve made the following purchases:

  • January 15th: 10 ounces at $25 per ounce = $250
  • February 20th: 5 ounces at $28 per ounce = $140
  • March 10th: 2 ounces at $27 per ounce = $54

Your total cost of silver would be $250 + $140 + $54 = $444.

Calculating Your Total Ounces of Silver Held

Next, sum up the “Ounces Purchased” column for all your purchases. This will give you the total number of ounces of silver you currently hold. In our example, the total ounces held would be 10 + 5 + 2 = 17 ounces.

Calculating Your Average Cost Per Ounce

Now, you can calculate your average cost per ounce. Divide the total cost of silver ($444) by the total number of ounces held (17). For more on this, see How To Build A Silver Inventory Spreadsheet. Average Cost Per Ounce = $444 / 17 = $26 per ounce. This means that, on average, you paid $26 for each ounce of silver you own.

Tracking Sales - Adjusting Your Average

Your average cost per ounce will change as you sell silver. When you sell, you’ll need to adjust your average cost. Here’s how:

  1. Record the Sale: Note the date and price you sold the silver.
  2. Calculate the Sale Proceeds: Multiply the number of ounces sold by the sale price.
  3. Subtract the Sale Proceeds from the Total Cost: $444 (total cost) - $100 (sale proceeds) = $344
  4. Add the New Ounces Held: 17 ounces (initial holdings) + 3 ounces (ounces sold) = 20 ounces
  5. Calculate the New Average Cost: $344 / 20 = $17.20 per ounce.

Your new average cost per ounce is now $17.20. This reflects the impact of your sale on your overall investment.

Using Your Average Cost Per Ounce for Profitability Analysis

Your average cost per ounce is a key metric for assessing your profitability. It allows you to determine whether you’re making a profit or a loss on your silver investments. Compare your current average cost per ounce to the current spot price of silver.

  • If the Spot Price > Average Cost: You’re making a profit.
  • If the Spot Price < Average Cost: You’re experiencing a loss.

As of today, August 15, 2026, the spot price of silver is $32.50 per ounce. Since your average cost is $17.20, you are currently making a profit of $15.30 per ounce.

Comparing Your Average Cost to Market Trends

It’s beneficial to compare your average cost per ounce to broader market trends. For instance, if silver prices are generally rising, you can expect your average cost to increase over time. Conversely, if silver prices are falling, your average cost will likely decrease. This comparison can help you identify potential buying or selling opportunities. According to a recent report, silver prices have historically shown an upward trend.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not Tracking Purchases: Failing to record all your silver purchases is the most common mistake. Keep meticulous records to ensure accurate calculations.
  • Using Inconsistent Units: Ensure you’re using consistent units (ounces) when calculating your average cost.
  • Ignoring Sales: Don’t forget to factor in the impact of sales on your average cost.
  • Not Updating Regularly: Review and update your average cost regularly, especially if you’re actively buying and selling silver. A quarterly review is a good starting point.
  • Relying Solely on Spot Price: While the spot price is important, your average cost per ounce provides a more accurate reflection of your individual investment performance.
