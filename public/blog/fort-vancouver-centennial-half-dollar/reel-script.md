## HOOK

**Visual:** FURS TO LONDON. LUMBER TO HAWAII.
Text: FURS TO LONDON. LUMBER TO HAWAII.
Narration: This fort shipped furs to London and lumber to Hawaii. A century after its founding, it appeared on an American half dollar. What made Fort Vancouver so important?

---

## OVERLAY: 1825 / A TRADING HEADQUARTERS

**Visual:** 1825 / A TRADING HEADQUARTERS
Text: 1825 / A TRADING HEADQUARTERS
Narration: In 1825, the Hudson’s Bay Company established the fort beside the Columbia River, in what is now Washington. It became the headquarters and supply depot for a vast network of trading posts.

---

## OVERLAY: GOODS OUT. FURS BACK.

**Visual:** GOODS OUT. FURS BACK.
Text: GOODS OUT. FURS BACK.
Narration: Here is how it worked. Food and trade goods went out to smaller posts. Furs came back by canoe, boat and horse brigades, then left aboard company ships bound for London. Fort Vancouver connected inland trade to ocean shipping.

---

## OVERLAY: BEYOND THE FUR TRADE

**Visual:** BEYOND THE FUR TRADE
Text: BEYOND THE FUR TRADE
Narration: And the business went well beyond fur. Farms fed the operation. A gristmill processed grain, and a sawmill produced lumber. By 1828, lumber and smoked salmon were being exported regularly to Honolulu. This wooden fort was part of a Pacific trading network.

---

## OVERLAY: THE PEOPLE BEHIND THE TRADE

**Visual:** THE PEOPLE BEHIND THE TRADE
Text: THE PEOPLE BEHIND THE TRADE
Narration: The people made that system work. Indigenous people, Métis, French Canadians, Hawaiians and others lived and worked here. Families in the village outside the stockade supported the fort’s farms, workshops and transport operations. The story reaches far beyond the walls shown on the coin.

---

## OVERLAY: 1829 / THE FORT MOVES

**Visual:** 1829 / THE FORT MOVES
Text: 1829 / THE FORT MOVES
Narration: Those walls also changed location. The first fort stood on a bluff. In 1829, it was rebuilt on the plain below. Today’s reconstructed fort stands on that later site.

---

## OVERLAY: A BORDER CHANGES THE BUSINESS

**Visual:** A BORDER CHANGES THE BUSINESS
Text: A BORDER CHANGES THE BUSINESS
Narration: Then the border changed the business. The Oregon Treaty of 1846 placed the fort inside United States territory. The U.S. Army established Vancouver Barracks nearby in 1849. The company left Fort Vancouver in 1860.

---

## OVERLAY: 1925 / HISTORY IN SILVER

**Visual:** 1925 / HISTORY IN SILVER
Text: 1925 / HISTORY IN SILVER
Narration: In 1925, the United States commemorated the fort’s hundredth anniversary with this half dollar. John McLoughlin, its chief factor, appears on one side; a frontiersman and the fort appear on the other.

---

## OVERLAY: RIVER. OCEAN. CHANGING BORDERS.

**Visual:** RIVER. OCEAN. CHANGING BORDERS.
Text: RIVER. OCEAN. CHANGING BORDERS.
Narration: A small silver coin remembers a place where river trade, ocean shipping and changing borders met.
