## What Actually Determines Your Local SEO Ranking

If you run a local service business, your website is only one piece of the puzzle. Stop chasing vague rankings. Your actual local ranking depends on five specific, concrete factors that Google uses to decide if people in your city should call you.

You do not need complicated jargon. You need to focus on these factors to get found by local customers.

### The 5 Local Factors That Drive Your Google Ranking

Forget national SEO theories. Local search is about proximity. Google wants to show the most relevant service to the person searching right now. If you don’t control these five elements, you are invisible.

**1. Your Google Business Profile is the #1 Ranking Factor.**
Your Google Business Profile (GBP) is the most important thing you own online. It is free. It is how Google knows who you are, where you are, and what you do. Set it up correctly. Fill out every section completely. This profile is more important than your website design.

**2. Review Velocity Matters More Than Website Content.**
Google prioritizes real customer experience. It cares about how quickly and how often you get reviews. A steady stream of recent, relevant reviews tells Google you are an active, trusted business. A website full of long text does not rank higher than a profile with recent, positive customer feedback. Focus on getting more reviews, not just writing more blog posts.

**3. Local Citations and Consistency Are Essential.**
Your business name, address, and phone number must be exactly the same everywhere online. This is called NAP consistency. Make sure this information is identical on your website, your Google Business Profile, Yelp, Facebook, and any local industry directories. Inconsistent data confuses Google and hurts your ranking.

**4. Service Area Definition is Critical for Contractors and Trades.**
If you serve multiple towns or regions, you must tell Google exactly where you work. If you are a plumber serving three different cities, you must define those areas. This signals to Google exactly which customers you are trying to reach. Failing to define your service area means Google cannot properly match you to local searches.

**5. Optimize for Local Intent, Not Impressions.**
Stop worrying about impressions or reach. These numbers do not tell you if you are getting calls. You need to focus on local intent. When someone searches for "HVAC repair near me," they want a phone number immediately. Your goal is to make the call happen. Focus your efforts on the specific local terms your customers actually use.
