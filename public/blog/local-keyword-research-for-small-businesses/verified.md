# Local Keyword Research For Small Businesses
Are you a small business owner struggling to get found online? You’re not alone. Many local businesses feel lost in the vastness of Google and other search engines. The good news is, you *can* dominate your local market with targeted keyword research. This isn’t some complicated, technical process. It’s about understanding what your ideal customers are searching for when they need your products or services. At Fused Distribution, we build websites and run SEO specifically for small local businesses, and we’ve found that a solid keyword strategy is the foundation of any successful online presence. Let’s break down how you can do it.
## What to Know About Local Keyword Research
Local keyword research is the process of identifying the specific words and phrases people use when searching for businesses like yours in your area. Unlike broad, general keywords, local keywords include your city, state, and often, specific neighborhoods. Focusing on these terms helps you appear in local search results - the ones that show up when someone [searches](/blog/near-me-searches-how-to-rank-for-them/) “pizza near me” or “plumber in {Your City}”. It’s a critical step for driving foot traffic and local sales.
## How to Get Started with Local Keyword Research
Let’s get practical. Here’s a step-by-step guide to get you started:
**1. Brainstorm Your Services & Products:** Make a comprehensive list of *everything* you offer. Don’t just list the main services; include variations, specific products, and related terms. For example, if you’re a landscaping company, think beyond “landscaping” - consider “lawn mowing,” “tree trimming,” “garden design,” “mulch installation,” “weed control,” and even “seasonal landscaping.”
**2. Think Like Your Customer:** Put yourself in your ideal customer’s shoes. What words would *they* use to describe what they need? What problems are they trying to solve? Write down a list of questions they might ask. For instance, a potential client looking for a cleaning service might search for “deep cleaning services,” “move out cleaning,” or “office cleaning.”
**3. Google It - Seriously:** Start typing your core services into Google. Pay close attention to the “People Also Ask” section and the suggested searches that appear below the search bar. These are real searches people are making. You’ll also see related searches at the bottom of the results page - these are goldmines.
**4. Use Keyword Research Tools (Free & Paid):** Several tools can help you uncover more keywords. Here are a few options:
* **Google Keyword Planner:** This free tool (requires a Google Ads account) provides search volume data and keyword suggestions. You’ll need to estimate your monthly ad spend, but even just using the free version is valuable.
* **Ubersuggest:** Neil Patel’s Ubersuggest offers a limited number of free searches per day and provides keyword ideas, search volume, and competition data.
* **AnswerThePublic:** This tool visualizes questions people are asking about your topic, giving you a fantastic starting point for content ideas.
**5. Analyze Your Competitors:** See what keywords your competitors are ranking for. You can use tools like SEMrush or Ahrefs (paid) to get detailed insights into their SEO strategy. Don’t copy them exactly, but use their strategy as inspiration.
**6. Localize, Localize, Localize:** Don’t just add “{Your City}” to your keywords. Think about neighborhood-specific terms. If you serve a specific area within your city, include that in your keyword strategy.
## Common Mistakes to Avoid
Let’s address some pitfalls that can derail your local keyword efforts: For more on this, see [How To Add Photos To Google Business Profile Correctly](/blog/how-to-add-photos-to-google-business-profile-correctly/).
**1. Focusing Only on Broad Keywords:** “Restaurant” is too broad. “Italian restaurant downtown {Your City}” is much more targeted. The more specific you are, the better your chances of ranking.
**2. Ignoring Long-Tail Keywords:** These are longer, more specific phrases that people use when they’re further along in the buying process. For example, instead of “plumber,” consider “emergency plumber {Your City} for leaky faucets.” Long-tail keywords often have lower search volume but higher conversion rates.
**3. Neglecting Location-Based Keywords:** This is crucial! Don’t assume Google knows you’re a local business. Explicitly include your city, state, and neighborhood in your website content, meta descriptions, and Google Business Profile.
**4. Overlooking Voice Search:** People are increasingly using voice assistants like Siri and Alexa to find local businesses. Optimize your website and content for voice search by using conversational language and answering common questions.
**5. Not Tracking Your Results:** Use Google Analytics and Google Search Console to monitor your website’s performance and identify which keywords are driving traffic. Adjust your strategy based on the data.
## Understanding the Balance Between Keyword Volume and Keyword Difficulty - What’s the Difference?
Understanding the balance between keyword volume and keyword difficulty is key to successful local SEO. Keyword volume refers to how many people are searching for a particular keyword. High volume means lots of potential traffic. Keyword difficulty refers to how competitive a keyword is - how many other websites are already ranking for it. If you target a keyword with extremely high difficulty, it will be very difficult to rank, even with a large volume of searches.
