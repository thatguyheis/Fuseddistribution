# Reel Script: what-to-do-when-you-inherit-silver-coins-and-bars
format: long-form

---
## HOOK
**Visual:** Topic intro shot
**Duration:** 10s
Narration: Inherited silver is a possession, a valuation question, and sometimes an estate responsibility. Confirm authority and secure the collection before you clean, divide, or sell anything.

---
## OVERLAY: CONFIRM WHO CAN ACT
**Visual:** Estate documents card
**Duration:** 15s
Narration: Find the will, trust, court appointment, or document identifying the executor, personal representative, trustee, or beneficiary. State probate rules determine who can act. A family relationship alone does not establish authority, so ask the estate professional before moving the silver.

---
## OVERLAY: SECURE AND DOCUMENT IT
**Visual:** Locked storage and handoff log
**Duration:** 15s
Narration: Use a locked, dry location and limit access. Photograph the condition, containers, and any signs of tampering. Record who delivered the collection, when it moved, where it was stored, and who witnessed the handoff. Ask an insurance professional about coverage.

---
## OVERLAY: BUILD AN INVENTORY
**Visual:** Inventory worksheet
**Duration:** 15s
Narration: Give each item or matched group a temporary ID. Record the visible date, maker, denomination, stated fineness, weight, packaging, and condition. Photograph both sides and keep receipts. Do not polish, open sealed packages, or assign a rare-coin grade from a photograph.

---
## OVERLAY: SEPARATE CONTENT FROM VALUE
**Visual:** Bullion and collectible split
**Duration:** 15s
Narration: Separate ordinary bullion, collectible coins, graded pieces, silverware, and uncertain items. A mark is evidence to record, not a complete appraisal. Condition, rarity, provenance, and packaging can change value, so do not assume every piece in one box has the same worth.

---
## OVERLAY: GET THE RIGHT VALUATION
**Visual:** Appraiser report
**Duration:** 15s
Narration: Ask a qualified appraiser what the valuation is for: estate reporting, insurance, division, or sale. Request the date, method, assumptions, and limitations in writing. A dealer offer shows one buyer’s price and is not automatically an independent appraisal.

---
## OVERLAY: PRESERVE BASIS AND SALE RECORDS
**Visual:** IRS records and sale receipt
**Duration:** 18s
Narration: The IRS generally says inherited property basis is fair market value at the date of death, with special rules and possible alternate valuation. Ask the executor for estate records and any Schedule A to Form 8971. Before a sale, keep proceeds, fees, date, items, and basis records with a tax professional’s review.
