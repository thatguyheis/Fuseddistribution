### stats
- text: 5-15% PRECIOUS METALS
  narration: Standard financial advice puts precious metals at 5 to 15 percent of your total portfolio. Silver typically sits at 20 to 40 percent of that slice, depending on how much volatility you're comfortable holding. Most new buyers have no target at all. They just buy when they feel like it.
- text: SILVER IS 3x MORE VOLATILE
  narration: Silver is roughly three times more volatile than gold, measured by monthly price swings over the past 20 years. That's not a reason to avoid it. It means your starting position should be sized smaller, not larger, than your gold position.

## reel-2
angle: concept
hook: GOLD-SILVER RATIO ABOVE 80
hook_type: immediate_value

### stats
- text: RATIO ABOVE 80 = SILVER CHEAP
  narration: The gold-silver ratio tells you how many ounces of silver it takes to buy one ounce of gold. The long-run average is around 60 to 65. When it climbs above 80, silver has historically been undervalued relative to gold and has outperformed in the months that followed.
- text: 654 MILLION OZ IN 2023
  narration: Silver's industrial demand hit a record 654 million ounces in 2023, driven by solar panels and electronics. Industrial demand now accounts for more than half of all silver consumed globally each year. That's the component gold doesn't have.

## reel-3
angle: cta_direct
hook: MOST SILVER BUYERS HAVE NO PLAN
hook_type: pain_point

### stats
- text: BUILD IN 3-5 PURCHASES
  narration: The biggest mistake new silver buyers make is entering at one price point. Silver moves fast. Splitting your target allocation into three to five purchases over six to twelve months averages out the price volatility and removes the temptation to over-commit when excitement is high and knowledge is still developing.
- text: STORAGE COSTS = 0.5-1% ANNUAL
  narration: Physical silver costs money to store and insure. Home safe, bank deposit box, and third-party vault all have different cost structures. If storage runs 0.5 to 1 percent per year, that's your breakeven before any gain. Factor that into your return calculation before deciding how large a physical position makes sense.
