## OVERLAY: 1838: BEFORE THE MORGAN

**Visual:** 1838: BEFORE THE MORGAN
**Duration:** 20s minimum

Narration: Congress authorized the New Orleans branch in eighteen thirty-five, along with Charlotte and Dahlonega. Those two branches concentrated on gold. New Orleans made both gold and silver coins, beginning in eighteen thirty-eight. The Morgan design came decades later. So the building already had a history before the dollar in our photograph existed.

---

## OVERLAY: A WAR INTERRUPTS PRODUCTION

**Visual:** A WAR INTERRUPTS PRODUCTION
**Duration:** 21s minimum

Narration: In eighteen sixty-one, the Confederacy gained control of the southern branch mints. The United States regained the facilities the following year. But taking back a building was not the same as restarting its presses. New Orleans resumed full operations by eighteen seventy-nine. Our nineteen hundred dollar belongs to that later chapter. It is not a Confederate coin.

---

## OVERLAY: THE BUILDING OUTLIVED THE PRESSES

**Visual:** THE BUILDING OUTLIVED THE PRESSES
**Duration:** 25s minimum

Narration: George T. Morgan designed the Liberty portrait and eagle reverse. New Orleans produced Morgan dollars from eighteen seventy-nine through nineteen oh four. Coin production there ended in nineteen oh nine, but the building survived. It opened as a state museum site in nineteen eighty-one, preserving a history that includes both American and Confederate coinage. The photographed dollar came later than the war, but its mint had lived through it.
