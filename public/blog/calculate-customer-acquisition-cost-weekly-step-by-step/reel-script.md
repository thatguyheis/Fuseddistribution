# Reel Script: Step by Step: The One Number Every Small Business Should Check Weekly
format: long-form
segments: 8
target-duration: 120s
content-kind: educate

---

## HOOK
**Duration:** 15s
**Visual:** Open on the approved worksheet graphic. Highlight the channel, date range, and customer count fields.
Narration: Can you tell which channel brought each new paying customer? A short weekly worksheet can connect one campaign's cost with the buyers you can reasonably trace to it. Here are five steps you can repeat.

---

## OVERLAY: PICK ONE CHANNEL AND WINDOW
**Duration:** 15s
**Visual:** Show a blank row labeled channel and date range, then fill in one campaign and matching start and end dates.
Narration: Start with one named channel or campaign. Write down the start and end dates, then use that same window for both costs and customers. If your sales take longer, mark the result as preliminary and choose a follow up date.

---

## OVERLAY: DEFINE WHAT COUNTS
**Duration:** 15s
**Visual:** Separate a first time paying customer from an inquiry card and a website visit card.
Narration: Decide what counts as a new customer before you count. A first time paying customer is different from a click, inquiry, email signup, or quote request. Keep the same definition next week so the comparison means something.

---

## OVERLAY: LIST THE COSTS
**Duration:** 15s
**Visual:** Add a receipt, ad charge, print cost, and event fee into a single campaign cost column.
Narration: List the costs you chose to include. That may be an ad charge, printing, an event fee, or a referral payment. Write down what you left out too. There is no useful comparison if one week includes different costs without saying so.

---

## OVERLAY: COUNT BUYERS AND MARK UNKNOWNS
**Duration:** 15s
**Visual:** Show a customer source question with options for a campaign, a referral, and not sure.
Narration: Ask new customers where they first heard about you. Use a distinct code or link when it fits. Keep an unknown option. If a customer saw three things before buying, do not force a confident answer that your records cannot support.

---

## OVERLAY: DIVIDE COST BY ATTRIBUTED BUYERS
**Duration:** 15s
**Visual:** Display a clearly labeled fictional example: two hundred forty dollars divided by six first time buyers equals forty dollars each.
Narration: Here is a fictional example, not a benchmark. One local campaign costs two hundred forty dollars. Six first time buyers say it brought them in. Divide two hundred forty by six. The estimate is forty dollars per attributed customer.

---

## OVERLAY: LABEL ZERO AND PRELIMINARY RESULTS
**Duration:** 15s
**Visual:** Mark a row as not measurable when the customer count is zero, then add a follow up date to a preliminary result.
Narration: If no new paying customers can be attributed, do not divide by zero or invent a cost. Record that the period was not measurable. If buyers may arrive later, label the result preliminary and update it when the buying window closes.

---

## QUESTION
**Duration:** 15s
Text on screen: What will you record next week?
Subtext: Full guide at fuseddistribution.com.
**Visual:** Return to the worksheet with date range, included costs, customer count, and unknowns in view.
Narration: Compare a channel only with records built the same way. Customer acquisition cost does not tell you profit or repeat buying, so use it as one question to investigate. What will you record next week? Read the full breakdown at fuseddistribution dot com.
