## HOOK

**Visual:** Map the work. Then automate.
**Duration:** 28s minimum

Narration: Map a manual business process by following one real case from its trigger to its verified outcome. Record who acts, what information they use, where decisions occur and what happens when the normal route fails. That map gives an automation proposal something concrete to improve instead of treating a list of software features as a business process. The method below is a proposed working exercise for a small team, not a report of measured savings.

---

## OVERLAY: CHOOSE A PROCESS WITH A CLEAR FINISH

**Visual:** Choose a process with a clear finish
**Duration:** 28s minimum

Narration: Name the triggering event and the observable end state. “Handle enquiries” is broad; “route a new service enquiry to the responsible person and confirm receipt” has clearer boundaries. These are examples rather than assumed facts about your business. Write down what is outside the scope so the exercise does not expand indefinitely. Distinguish the business outcome from an intermediate software event. A draft being generated is different from a message being approved or delivered.

---

## OVERLAY: OBSERVE A COMPLETED CASE

**Visual:** Observe a completed case
**Duration:** 12s minimum

Narration: Ask the person doing the work to describe a recent authorized example. Follow the sequence using the records available, and distinguish what the records show from what someone remembers.

---

## OVERLAY: SEPARATE RULES FROM JUDGMENT

**Visual:** Separate rules from judgment
**Duration:** 29s minimum

Narration: For each decision, ask what evidence permits it and whether the rule is documented. A stable rule with a clear input may be suitable for a deterministic check. A decision that depends on ambiguous context may need human review. Do not call the second case an automation failure before defining what the machine was expected to decide. Write the outcome when information is missing. Does the work stop, return for clarification, or proceed with a documented limitation?

---

## OVERLAY: MAP EXCEPTIONS AND AUTHORITY

**Visual:** Map exceptions and authority
**Duration:** 26s minimum

Narration: List the exceptions observed in the chosen cases and separate them from hypothetical risks raised during discussion. Both can be useful, but they are different kinds of evidence. Start with exceptions that change the next action or the permission required, rather than trying to imagine every possible failure. Assign an owner to each unresolved branch. Say who can correct data, approve an exception, change a rule or contact a customer.
