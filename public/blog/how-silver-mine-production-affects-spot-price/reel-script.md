## OVERLAY: MINE OUTPUT IS ONE FLOW
**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 18s minimum

Narration: Mine output is one supply flow into a larger market. Refined metal also comes from recycling and existing inventories, while demand comes from industrial users, investors, jewelry, and other sectors. A production number must be read with its period and definition.
---
## OVERLAY: BYPRODUCT SUPPLY CHANGES THE SIGNAL
**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 20s minimum

Narration: Silver is often produced alongside copper, lead, zinc, or gold. USGS reported that U.S. silver in 2024 came from four silver mines and 31 base or precious metal operations as a byproduct or coproduct. A host metal decision can matter more than silver’s own price.
---
## OVERLAY: GRADE RECOVERY THROUGHPUT MATTER
**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 16s minimum

Narration: Output depends on ore grade, recovery, and plant throughput, not just rock moved. Maintenance, energy, labor, water, permits, and equipment can change payable output. Contained metal, concentrate, and refined output are different measures.
---
## OVERLAY: NEW MINES TAKE YEARS
**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 16s minimum

Narration: Exploration, studies, financing, permits, construction, commissioning, and ramp up separate a project announcement from commercial production. Higher prices cannot create new mine supply immediately, and a resource estimate is not current production.
---
## OVERLAY: INVENTORIES AND DEMAND CONNECT TIME
**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 18s minimum

Narration: Inventories can absorb a temporary mismatch between supply and demand. Recycling can rise or fall differently from mining. A mine increase can meet stronger demand without building stocks, so output alone does not establish a price direction.
---

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 16s minimum

Narration: Record source, date, unit, actual or estimated status, primary or byproduct origin, recycling, inventories, and demand. Keep facts separate from estimates. Treat production as context, not a promise about a future silver price.
