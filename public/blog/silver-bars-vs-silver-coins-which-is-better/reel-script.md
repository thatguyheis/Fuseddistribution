## HOOK
Narration: Silver Eagles carry a 20 percent premium over spot. A 10 oz bar runs about 4 percent. On a 100 oz position, that gap costs you 480 dollars before silver moves a single penny.

---

## SEGMENT 1: Bar Premium
Narration: When you buy a 10 oz silver bar, you typically pay just 3 to 5 percent above spot price. That covers minting and dealer margin. It is one of the lowest premiums in the silver market.

---

## SEGMENT 2: Eagle Premium
Narration: A 1 oz American Silver Eagle costs 15 to 22 percent above spot at most dealers. That is 4 to 5 times the premium you pay on a bar. You are paying extra for legal tender status, mint guarantee, and brand recognition.

---

## SEGMENT 3: The Math
Narration: At 30 dollars per ounce spot, a 10 oz bar costs roughly 312 dollars. Ten individual Silver Eagles cost around 360 dollars. That is 48 dollars more for the same amount of silver. Scale to 100 ounces and the gap hits 480 dollars.

---

## SEGMENT 4: Premium Chart
Narration: The chart tells the story clearly. A 10 oz bar carries about a 4 percent premium. A 1 oz round runs about 6 percent. A 1 oz Silver Eagle runs about 20 percent. That 16-point gap is what you pay just for the product form.

---

## SEGMENT 5: When Bars Win
Narration: Bars work best for long-term reserve building. Lower premiums mean more silver per dollar. They stack cleanly in a safe without individual capsules. If you are adding 10 or more ounces at a time, bars almost always give you more metal for your money.

---

## SEGMENT 6: When Coins Win
Narration: Coins win on flexibility. You can sell one 1 oz coin without dividing a larger bar. Local coin shops recognize a Silver Eagle or Maple Leaf on sight, no assay required. They are also a good starting point if you are new to buying silver.

---

## SEGMENT 7: Storage Differences
Narration: Coins need individual capsules or plastic tubes to prevent milk spotting, those white cloudy patches from moisture and handling. Silver Eagles are particularly prone to this. Bars are simpler: a hard plastic slab or tray keeps them stable with no capsules needed.

---

## SEGMENT 8: Mixed Strategy
Narration: Many buyers hold both. Around 70 to 75 percent of the position in bars for cost efficiency. Around 20 to 30 percent in coins for small-lot flexibility. There is no rule. The right split depends on how you plan to sell over time.

---

## SEGMENT 9: Verdict
Narration: For building a long-term reserve, bars are the better financial choice. Lower premiums mean more silver per dollar. For flexibility and starting small, coins give you easier selling options and wider recognition. You do not have to pick just one.
