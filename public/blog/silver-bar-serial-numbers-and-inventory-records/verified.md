# Silver Bar Serial Numbers and Inventory Records

A silver bar inventory is a private record of what you own, what the item says about itself, and which documents support the purchase. A serial number can help you match a bar to a receipt or a supplier record, but it is not a stand alone authenticity certificate and it does not create a public ownership history.

If a bar is unnumbered, record that fact instead of inventing an identifier. If a mark is hard to read, write down the uncertainty and keep a close photograph for later review. Good records make discrepancies visible. They do not replace an authentication service, a product specific check, or legal advice about ownership.

## What a silver bar serial number can tell you

Some bars carry a serial number or other manufacturer identifier. A number can distinguish one item from another and can help you compare the physical marking with a product description, invoice, or dealer record. It does not prove that the bar is genuine merely because the number looks neat or because a seller supplied it.

The [LBMA Good Delivery technical standards](https://www.lbma.org.uk/publications/good-delivery-rules/technical-specifications) apply to bars that meet a particular wholesale standard. Their marking requirements are not a universal rule for every retail silver bar. The [LBMA weight list guidance](https://www.lbma.org.uk/publications/good-delivery-rules/annex-c-weight-lists) shows useful fields for commercial silver shipments, including a serial number, brand code, gross weight, assay, and month and year of manufacture. That is a model for organized identification, not a public registry of every bar or its owners.

Read the entire marking as printed. Preserve leading zeroes, letters, punctuation, and the order of each field. Record the refiner or brand mark, marked fineness, marked weight or unit, production date if present, and any assay or product code. Do not normalize a mark by guessing that a smudged character is a zero or an O.

## Build one inventory record for each item

Use one row per bar when the item has its own number. For identical unnumbered pieces, use a clearly defined lot and give each piece a private inventory ID if you can do so without altering the product or its packaging. Keep your internal ID separate from the manufacturer mark. This lets you track the item even when the bar has no serial number. Keep storage notes separate from the general handling advice in our [silver storage guide](/blog/silver-storage-guide/).

A useful record can include:

- private inventory ID
- brand or refiner as marked
- product name or size as shown on the invoice
- marked weight and unit
- marked fineness
- serial number or other identifier, copied exactly
- production date or date mark, if present
- seller and order reference
- acquisition date
- receipt or invoice location
- private photo locations
- current status, such as held, transferred, or sold
- fields that are unknown or still need review

Unknown is a useful value. It distinguishes missing information from a fact you have checked and found absent. Do not fill a blank field with a likely manufacturer, a guessed date, or a claim that a retail product follows a wholesale standard.

## Link photos and receipts without exposing your holdings

Take clear photos of both sides, the edges, packaging, and visible marks when that can be done safely. Keep the original files in a controlled location and link them from the private record. A photo supports your notes, but it is not a substitute for an expert examination. Do not scratch, open, or otherwise modify a bar to make a mark easier to read.

Use neutral filenames and avoid putting a home address, storage location, or full holdings list in a public folder. A private filename such as `inventory-014-front.jpg` is easier to manage than a filename containing a person, address, or account number. Store receipts and correspondence with access controls appropriate to the sensitivity of the information.

The [Perth Mint counterfeit guidance](https://www.perthmint.com/customer-care/scams-and-counterfeits/tips-for-avoiding-fake-coins-and-bars) recommends checking the seller, product specifications, and physical characteristics. Those checks are product specific. Packaging, certificates, and security features vary, so do not turn a feature of one product into a rule for every silver bar.

## Reconcile a purchase against its documents

When a bar arrives, compare the item with the order and invoice before filing it as complete. Check the brand, product description, marked weight, fineness, serial or product identifier, quantity, and packaging description when one is supplied. Note what you actually received and keep the original documentation.

If the details do not match, preserve the mismatch as part of the record. Photograph the item, save the order and delivery documents, and ask the seller to explain or correct the discrepancy. Do not silently edit the inventory to make conflicting records agree. A change log should show what changed, when it changed, why it changed, and which document supports the update.

Do not assume that a matching serial number proves purity or ownership. If authenticity is in question, pause the transaction and use the appropriate product or professional verification process. A personal spreadsheet can organize evidence, but it cannot certify metal content or establish a complete chain of title.

## Record transfers and sales as changes, not replacements

Keep the historical entry when an item is moved, sold, or exchanged. Add a dated event with the other party or account reference as appropriate, the event type, the quantity, the related document, and any new storage or custody note that belongs in the private system. Avoid recording more personal information than you need.

For a sale, retain the original acquisition record and add the sale document. For a transfer between storage locations, retain the item ID and record the old and new status. For a correction, explain the reason and keep the earlier value in the change log. This structure makes it possible to answer a simple question later: which document supports the current status of this item?

## Protect the inventory and its limits

Back up the record using a method you can restore and test. Restrict editing access, protect exported files, and review who can see photos, invoices, and storage notes. Avoid publishing the number of bars you hold or where they are kept. A public post can reveal more than the original article intended.

Review the inventory periodically for duplicate IDs, unreadable marks, missing document links, and items marked as current that have a later transfer record. Keep a short list of unresolved questions rather than hiding them. The goal is a clear, private record with honest gaps.

## A simple record structure

You can begin with four connected records:

1. Item: the private ID, marked identifiers, product details, and photos.
2. Evidence: the invoice, receipt, order, certificate if supplied, and source URL or file path.
3. Change log: dated purchases, transfers, corrections, and sales.
4. Review notes: questions, discrepancies, and the next verification step.

This item to evidence to change log structure is useful because it keeps a serial number in context. It also makes the limits visible. A serial number may help identify an item, while a receipt may support a transaction, but neither one alone proves authenticity, purity, or an unbroken ownership history.

## Source and next steps

Use the [LBMA technical specifications](https://www.lbma.org.uk/publications/good-delivery-rules/technical-specifications) and [LBMA weight list guidance](https://www.lbma.org.uk/publications/good-delivery-rules/annex-c-weight-lists) only for the wholesale fields and standards they actually describe. Use the [Perth Mint counterfeit guidance](https://www.perthmint.com/customer-care/scams-and-counterfeits/tips-for-avoiding-fake-coins-and-bars) as product specific counterfeit awareness guidance, not as a universal checklist.

Start with one item. Copy every visible mark, link the purchase document, record the unknowns, and add one change log entry if the item has moved. If you are deciding between product formats, compare the record fields with our guide to [silver coins, rounds, and bars](/blog/silver-coins-rounds-bars/). Can you trace the current status of that item to the document that supports it? If not, keep the gap visible and make verification the next action.
