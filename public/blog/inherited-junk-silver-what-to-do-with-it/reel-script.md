format: long-form

## HOOK
**Visual:** Topic intro shot
**Duration:** 10s
Narration: Inherited “junk silver” usually means common circulated coins valued mainly for silver content. Confirm authority and secure the collection before sorting, cleaning, dividing, or selling anything.

---
## OVERLAY: CONFIRM WHO CAN ACT
**Visual:** Estate documents card
**Duration:** 15s
Narration: Find the will, trust, court appointment, or document identifying the executor, personal representative, trustee, or beneficiary. State probate rules determine who can act. A family relationship alone does not establish authority, so ask the estate professional before moving the silver.

---
## OVERLAY: SECURE AND DOCUMENT IT
**Visual:** Locked storage and handoff log
**Duration:** 15s
Narration: Use a locked, dry location and limit access. Photograph the condition, containers, and any signs of tampering. Record who delivered the collection, when it moved, where it was stored, and who witnessed the handoff. Ask an insurance professional about coverage.

---
## OVERLAY: BUILD AN INVENTORY
**Visual:** Inventory worksheet
**Duration:** 15s
Narration: Give each group a temporary ID and record count, denomination, date, mint mark, face value, stated fineness, weight, packaging, and condition. Keep dimes, quarters, and half dollars separate. Do not polish or open sealed packages.

---
## OVERLAY: SEPARATE CONTENT FROM VALUE
**Visual:** Bullion and collectible split
**Duration:** 15s
Narration: Many USA dimes and quarters dated 1964 or earlier are 90 percent silver by alloy weight, while Kennedy halves from 1965 through 1970 are generally 40 percent. These are clues, not authentication. Separate key dates, proofs, graded pieces, foreign coins, and uncertain items.

---
## OVERLAY: GET THE RIGHT VALUATION
**Visual:** Appraiser report
**Duration:** 15s
Narration: Ask a qualified appraiser what the valuation is for: estate reporting, insurance, division, or sale. Request the date, method, assumptions, and limitations in writing. A dealer offer shows one buyer’s price and is not automatically an independent appraisal.

---
## OVERLAY: PRESERVE BASIS AND SALE RECORDS
**Visual:** IRS records and sale receipt
**Duration:** 18s
Narration: For a first count, record face value separately from estimated silver value. For example, 20 pre-1965 USA quarters total $5.00 face value, before any metal or collector value. Keep estate records, appraisal date, sale proceeds, fees, and basis records for tax review.
