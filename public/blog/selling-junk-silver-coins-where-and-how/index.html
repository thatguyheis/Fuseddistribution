Understanding Junk Silver: What Makes It Sellable?

Junk silver, also known as bullion silver or scrap silver, refers to pre-1965 United States dimes, quarters, and half dollars. These coins are considered “junk” because they’re no longer legal tender and their intrinsic value - the value of the silver they contain - is significantly lower than their face value. The composition of these coins is 90% silver, which is the key factor determining their market value. However, several factors impact the actual selling price you’ll receive. Condition plays a huge role; circulated coins with wear and tear will fetch considerably less than uncirculated or proof coins. The current spot price of silver is the primary driver, but dealer premiums (the extra amount they add on top of the spot price) can vary. Understanding these variables is crucial before you decide where to sell. A survey of local coin dealers in major metropolitan areas revealed an average premium offered on junk silver coins ranged from 10% to 20%.

Where to Sell Junk Silver Coins Legitimately

So, where can you sell your junk silver coins? Several options exist, each with its own advantages and disadvantages. Local coin dealers are a common starting point. They offer immediate cash and can often provide a preliminary assessment of your coin’s value. However, dealer premiums can sometimes be higher than you’d expect. Established online auction houses, like eBay or Heritage Auctions, provide a broader audience and potentially higher prices, but require more effort to manage the listing and shipping process. Direct sales to private collectors can also be an option, but require careful vetting to avoid scams. We recommend starting with a few local dealers to get a baseline understanding of your coin’s value.

How to Price Your Junk Silver Coins Accurately

Determining the accurate price of your junk silver requires a bit of research. First, find the silver content of each coin: dimes contain 0.77344 ounces of silver, quarters contain 0.36144 ounces, and half dollars contain 0.56704 ounces. Multiply the number of coins you have by the silver content to calculate the total silver weight in ounces. Then, multiply that total by the current spot price of silver. The spot price fluctuates daily, so check a reputable source like Kitco or APMEX for the latest price. Finally, add a dealer premium - typically between 10% and 20% - to arrive at the final selling price. For example, if you have 50 dimes, each containing 0.77344 ounces of silver, and the spot price is $28 per ounce, the total silver weight would be 38.672 ounces. At a 15% premium, the selling price would be approximately $586.54.

The Process: Selling Junk Silver Coins Step-by-Step

Let’s outline the steps involved in selling your junk silver coins:

  1. Identify and Sort: Gather all your coins and sort them into piles based on their condition. Note the date and mint mark for each coin.
  2. Research Value: Use online resources like APMEX, Kitco, or SilverInvest to determine the current spot price of silver.
  3. Contact Dealers: Reach out to several local coin dealers to get quotes. Compare their offers and premiums.
  4. Consider Online Options: If you’re comfortable with online sales, list your coins on eBay or Heritage Auctions.
  5. Secure Payment: Always use a secure payment method, such as a bank wire transfer or PayPal, to avoid scams.
  6. Ship Securely: If shipping your coins, use a trackable shipping service and insure the package for its full value. We recommend insuring your shipment for at least 110% of the value, as it can cover loss and damage.

Avoiding Scams When Selling Junk Silver Coins

The market for junk silver can unfortunately attract unscrupulous individuals. Here’s how to protect yourself from scams:

  • Beware of Unrealistic Offers: If a buyer offers a price significantly higher than the spot price of silver, it’s likely a scam.
  • Research Buyers: Before accepting a sale, research the buyer’s reputation. Check online reviews and feedback.
  • Don’t Share Personal Information: Never share your bank account details or social security number with an unknown buyer.
  • Use Secure Payment Methods: As mentioned earlier, stick to secure payment methods like bank wire transfers or PayPal.
  • Trust Your Gut: If something feels off, don’t proceed with the sale.
