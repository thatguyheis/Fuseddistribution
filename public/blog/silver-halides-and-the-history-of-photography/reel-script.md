## OVERLAY: A DAGUERREOTYPE IS A METAL PLATE

**Visual:** Approved article hero process panel 1
**Duration:** 22s minimum

Narration: A daguerreotype was announced publicly in 1839. The Library of Congress describes it as a direct-positive image on a copper sheet coated with silver. The image forms on that plate without a separate negative. That construction matters when someone describes an old object: a daguerreotype is not a paper print made from a negative. The process name alone still cannot establish the condition or treatment history of a particular plate.

---

## OVERLAY: SALTED PAPER USES A PAPER SUPPORT

**Visual:** Approved article hero process panel 2
**Duration:** 23s minimum

Narration: Salted paper prints were among the early ways to make a photographic print on paper. The Library of Congress describes paper treated with sodium chloride, coated with silver nitrate, then exposed in contact with a negative. This is one example of a silver image on a paper support. It differs from a daguerreotype plate in both support and image formation. A process label should describe the object structure, not just its black-and-white appearance.

---

## OVERLAY: WET COLLODION USES GLASS

**Visual:** Approved article hero process panel 3
**Duration:** 22s minimum

Narration: Wet-plate collodion introduced another construction. The Library of Congress describes a glass plate coated with collodion and sensitized in silver nitrate. The wet negative had to be prepared, exposed and developed before the coating dried. Formats such as ambrotypes and tintypes have their own constructions, so the broad process family does not make every object identical. The support, layers and image formation all help distinguish what a photograph is.

---

## OVERLAY: GELATIN PLATES USE A BINDER

**Visual:** Approved article hero process panel 4
**Duration:** 22s minimum

Narration: Later gelatin dry plates offered glass already coated with a photographic emulsion containing silver halides suspended in gelatin. The Smithsonian Institution Archives says the faster plates allowed shorter exposures than wet collodion. The Library of Congress describes photographs as composite objects with a support, binder and final image material. Gelatin silver papers also became common for black-and-white prints, but a paper print and a glass plate remain different objects.

---

## OVERLAY: IDENTIFY BEFORE YOU LABEL

**Visual:** Approved article hero process panel 5
**Duration:** 24s minimum

Narration: When identifying an old photograph, record what you can observe: support, dimensions, surface, mount and any existing inscription. Keep those observations separate from a process identification. A scan can hide physical details, and appearance alone does not confirm a precise date. Compare the object with trusted collection or conservation references. Avoid cleaning or testing an uncertain photograph as a shortcut. For a valuable or fragile item, ask a photograph conservator before handling or treatment.
