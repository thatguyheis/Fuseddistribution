## OVERLAY: RECOMMEND GETTING INDEPENDENT APPRAISALS

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 20s minimum

Narration: We recommend getting independent appraisals from qualified precious metal dealers or certified appraisers. A professional appraisal will provide a reliable estimate of the silver’s worth. According to the IRS, the fair market value of assets at the time of death is used for estate tax purposes. This means the value at the date of death, not what the items sold for later.

Narration: Proportional Division: This method divides the silver in proportion to each beneficiary’s share of the estate. This is often more fair than equal division. Consult with an estate attorney to ensure the division process complies with the will and applicable state laws. Dividing silver in an estate has significant tax implications.

## STAT: 20% SILVER INDIVIDUALS STATE ESTATE

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 23s minimum

Narration: Capital Gains Tax: When the silver is sold, any profit (capital gain) may be subject to capital gains tax. The tax rate depends on the holding period (how long you owned the silver) and the beneficiary’s income. As of 2024, the highest capital gains tax rate is 20 percent for individuals. State Estate Taxes: Some states have their own estate taxes, which may be in addition to the federal estate tax.

## STAT: 60% SILVER ESTATE RECEIVE VALUE

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 21s minimum

Narration: When the silver collection includes items with significantly different values, a proportional division is often the most equitable approach. For example, if one beneficiary receives 60 percent of the estate, they should receive 60 percent of the silver’s value. You could also consider dividing the silver into categories - high-value items (coins, bars) and lower-value items (silverware, jewelry) - and dividing each category proportionally.
