---

## HOOK

**Visual:** Questions become useful pages
**Duration:** 20s minimum

Narration: Plan a business website by listing the questions a customer needs answered before taking the next step. Then give each important question a clear home, an accurate answer and a useful onward action. This approach connects the site structure to real decisions instead of beginning with a collection of page titles or a visual theme.

---

## OVERLAY: COLLECT ACTUAL QUESTIONS

**Visual:** Collect actual questions
**Duration:** 17s minimum

Narration: Start with questions your team actually receives, using records you are permitted to review. Remove personal information from the working notes. Keep the question's meaning even when you simplify its wording. Do not turn one customer's detailed situation into a public example without the necessary permission.

---

## OVERLAY: GROUP CUSTOMER DECISIONS

**Visual:** Group customer decisions
**Duration:** 19s minimum

Narration: Arrange the questions into a small number of decisions, such as whether the service fits, what preparation is needed, how the work proceeds and how to request the next step. These are examples to adapt, not required sections for every business. Use the actual service and audience to choose the groups.

---

## OVERLAY: OWN EACH ANSWER

**Visual:** Own each answer
**Duration:** 18s minimum

Narration: For each proposed page, write its main question, a short answer, the source of that answer and the person responsible for keeping it accurate. Business details such as service scope, coverage and response expectations should come from the responsible team, not from generic copy or an AI guess.

---

## OVERLAY: TEST THE NEXT STEP

**Visual:** Test the next step
**Duration:** 35s minimum

Narration: Write link labels that describe the destination or action. Check that the destination actually completes the expected step. A page saying “request an estimate” should lead to a functioning request path, not simply to an unrelated homepage. Use the guide to checking broken booking links for a practical example of verifying the destination. Test the page map with a realistic question before polishing the design. If the answer is hard to find, review navigation. If the answer does not help, repair the content. If the next action fails, fix the destination. Assign an owner to keep the information current.
