# Reel Script: The One Number Every Small Business Should Check Weekly
format: long-form
segments: 8
target-duration: 115s


## HOOK
**Visual:** Topic intro shot
**Duration:** 5s minimum

Narration: What does it cost you to win one new customer? That number can help you see whether a marketing effort deserves another week.

---

## OVERLAY: THE CUSTOMER COST FORMULA
Narration: It is customer acquisition cost, often shortened to C A C. Add up what you spent on one marketing effort, then divide by the new customers you can reasonably connect to it.

---

## OVERLAY: MADE UP EXAMPLE
Narration: Here is a made up example. A shop spends three hundred dollars on a local promotion. Ten first time buyers say that promotion brought them in. Three hundred divided by ten means thirty dollars per new customer.

---

## OVERLAY: DEFINE YOUR TERMS
Narration: That thirty dollars is not a universal target or a verdict on the business. First set the rules. Choose a time period, decide what counts as a new customer, and write down which costs are included.

---

## OVERLAY: TRACK THE SOURCE
Narration: Ask each new customer where they first heard about you. Offer a short list and an unknown option. A person may see a social post, hear about you from a friend, then search your name. Attribution is not always certain.

---

## OVERLAY: REVIEW THE WEEK
Narration: At week’s end, total each campaign’s cost and count the new customers you can reasonably attribute to it. Divide cost by customers. If the count is zero, record that result. Do not divide by zero or pretend you know a cost per customer.

---

## OVERLAY: CHECK THE TIMING
Narration: If your sales take longer than a week, mark the result as preliminary and check again later. SCORE’s marketing guide recommends updating estimates with actual sales. One quiet week may not tell the whole story.

---

## OVERLAY: KNOW THE LIMIT
Narration: This number does not tell you profit, customer satisfaction, or whether someone will return. Compare it with your own costs and sales records. For accounting guidance or a major budget choice, speak with a qualified adviser.
Use the same definitions next week so the number can guide what you test next.
