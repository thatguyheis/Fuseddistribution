## HOOK

**Visual:** Read the labels before the numbers
**Duration:** 20s minimum

Narration: Read a USGS silver commodity summary by checking the edition, the period covered, the units and the meaning of each row before using a figure. A compact table can place several different measures close together. Their proximity does not make them interchangeable, and a copied number without its context is difficult to check later.

---

## OVERLAY: IDENTIFY THE EDITION

**Visual:** Identify the edition
**Duration:** 18s minimum

Narration: Open the publication through the USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries collection. Record the edition and the specific silver section or file you read. Keep the source link with your notes so another reader can locate the same material rather than a similarly titled page from a different year.

---

## OVERLAY: KEEP THE ORIGINAL UNITS

**Visual:** Keep the original units
**Duration:** 20s minimum

Narration: Find the unit statement attached to the table or entry. Include it in the same note as the number. A value without units can be mistaken for a count, a weight or a currency amount. Do not rely on memory of an earlier page: a neighboring measure may use a different convention.

---

## OVERLAY: SEPARATE EACH MEASURE

**Visual:** Separate each measure
**Duration:** 19s minimum

Narration: Read the row name and any accompanying definition in full. Production, trade and other measures describe different activities. Do not add or subtract rows just because they all concern silver. First establish whether the definitions, coverage and periods support the calculation you intend to make, including any stated exclusions.

---

## OVERLAY: PRESERVE THE QUALIFICATIONS

**Visual:** Preserve the qualifications
**Duration:** 35s minimum

Narration: Follow symbols and note references before copying a value into a report. Record any qualifications that change how the figure should be understood. If a label identifies an estimate, preserve that distinction in your own wording and graphic. Do not turn a qualified figure into an apparently final measurement by removing the label. Our article on silver in reflective coatings illustrates a different kind of explanation: material context without invented performance percentages. Choose the visual form that matches the evidence. A meaningful illustration can teach more accurately than a numerical chart assembled from figures that do not belong together.
