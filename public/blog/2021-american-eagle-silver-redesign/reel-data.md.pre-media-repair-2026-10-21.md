## claims
- AE1: Program start in 1986 and use of Weinman’s Walking Liberty design on the obverse. Source: https://www.usmint.gov/content/usmint/us/en/learn/coins-and-medals/collectible-coins/american-eagle.html
- AE2: Mercanti’s earlier heraldic reverse and the Damstra/Gaudioso 2021 design. Source: https://www.usmint.gov/news/inside-the-mint/redesigning-the-american-eagle-gold-and-silver-coins
- AE3: The Mint's 2021 reverse-proof set paired the original design struck at West Point with the new reverse struck at San Francisco; both coins were 99.9 percent silver. Source: https://www.usmint.gov/news/press-releases/united-states-mint-to-release-set-of-2021-reverse-proof-american-eagle-silver-coins-on-september-13
- AE4: The Silver Eagle began in 1986; the Mint says the reverse changed after the law's 25-year design period while the Walking Liberty obverse remained. Source: https://www.usmint.gov/news/inside-the-mint/redesigning-the-american-eagle-gold-and-silver-coins
- AE5: Emily Damstra designed the 2021 reverse with an eagle landing at a nest with an oak branch; Michael Gaudioso sculpted the design. Source: https://www.usmint.gov/news/inside-the-mint/redesigning-the-american-eagle-gold-and-silver-coins
- AE6: The Mint describes the obverse as Adolph A. Weinman's 1916 Walking Liberty design and says the 2021 version returned to source artwork for a more faithful rendering. Source: https://www.usmint.gov/news/inside-the-mint/redesigning-the-american-eagle-gold-and-silver-coins
- AE7: The Mint's 2021 transition set presented the old and new reverse designs together, making the change between the two designs visible in one release. Source: https://www.usmint.gov/news/press-releases/united-states-mint-to-release-set-of-2021-reverse-proof-american-eagle-silver-coins-on-september-13
