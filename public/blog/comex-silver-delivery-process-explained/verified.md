# COMEX Silver Futures Delivery: How the Exchange Process Works

COMEX silver futures delivery is an exchange and clearing process between futures market participants. It is not the way a retail customer orders coins from a bullion dealer. The exchange’s contract rules define eligible metal, delivery notices, warrants, timing, and settlement. Anyone considering a futures position should read the current rules for the exact contract and consult a registered futures professional about account-specific obligations.

## What is delivered

The standard COMEX Silver futures contract represents 5,000 troy ounces, subject to the contract’s weight tolerance. The silver must meet the exchange’s minimum fineness and approved brand requirements. The exchange’s rules and contract specifications control; the exact product and quantity are not interchangeable with a retail one ounce coin order.

For physically delivered precious metal futures, CME describes an electronic warrant as the title document used in delivery. The warrant identifies metal held at an exchange approved depository. In the standard process, the seller submits a notice of intention to deliver and identifies the warrant. The clearinghouse assigns the delivery obligation to a long position, and the warrant changes ownership through the clearing system under the contract rules.

## The delivery sequence

The practical sequence is:

1. A market participant holds a position in a contract that is eligible for physical delivery.
2. During the contract’s delivery period, the seller follows the notice procedure and tenders an eligible warrant.
3. The clearinghouse assigns the delivery to an eligible long position.
4. The clearing firms complete the invoice, payment, and warrant transfer required by the exchange.
5. The warrant owner may keep the metal on warrant, cancel the warrant, or arrange load out under the applicable depository procedures and fees.

The notice and settlement schedule depends on the contract rules in effect. CME’s educational material describes transfer two business days after the notice of intent, while its product guide lays out a three business day intent, invoicing, and delivery cycle. Use the current rulebook and your clearing firm’s instructions for exact dates and deadlines.

## Delivery is not a shipment to a home

Receiving a warrant establishes a title and storage relationship for eligible metal in an exchange approved depository. It does not automatically arrange a consumer shipment, select a retailer, or guarantee a particular shipping timeline. Removing metal from a depository requires the holder to follow the depository’s load out process and pay any applicable costs. Delivery arrangements are distinct from buying a coin or bar from a dealer.

Do not rely on claims that a retail dealer can notify COMEX on your behalf or that exchange inventory is an offer of retail metal. This article does not describe a dealer’s order process, and it does not recommend trading futures.

## Check the current contract documents

Before relying on a delivery description, confirm the exchange product code, contract size, delivery month, eligible brands, notice deadline, delivery location, payment process, and load out rules. CME can list more than one silver futures product, and specifications can change. The official contract page and current rulebook take precedence over summaries or social media posts.
