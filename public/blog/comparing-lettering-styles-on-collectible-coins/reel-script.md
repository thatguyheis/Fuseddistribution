## HOOK

**Visual:** Same letter. Different character.
**Duration:** 26s minimum

Narration: Lettering can change the character of a coin design even when the main image is similar. Compare the shape, spacing and placement of the letters before deciding what those differences mean. A visual comparison is an observation exercise, not a way to authenticate a coin or estimate its price. Photograph both pieces at a similar scale, angle and lighting level. Keep an original image as well as any crop.

---

## OVERLAY: DESCRIBE THE LETTERS BEFORE NAMING A STYLE

**Visual:** Describe the letters before naming a style
**Duration:** 31s minimum

Narration: Look at the ends of strokes, their apparent thickness, the proportions of rounded letters and the treatment of corners. Choose a repeated letter to compare across the inscriptions. Plain descriptions such as narrow, rounded or widely spaced are often more useful than an uncertain typeface name. Do not assume an engraved inscription corresponds exactly to a modern computer font. The U.S. Mint anatomy guide identifies inscriptions and the legend as parts of coin design; it does not identify the typeface of every product.

---

## OVERLAY: COMPARE SPACING AND PLACEMENT

**Visual:** Compare spacing and placement
**Duration:** 14s minimum

Narration: Notice how the inscription follows the rim and how it relates to the central image. Are gaps similar between letters, or does one pair look tighter? Record the position of the feature you mean.

---

## OVERLAY: SEPARATE APPEARANCE FROM PRODUCTION CLAIMS

**Visual:** Separate appearance from production claims
**Duration:** 21s minimum

Narration: A different looking stroke does not by itself explain how or why the object was made. Avoid jumping from an interesting letter to an attribution, variety name or manufacturing fault. Those are separate claims that need appropriate evidence and, when necessary, qualified review. For background on the transfer of a design, read how coin dies work.

---

## OVERLAY: KEEP A COMPARISON SHEET

**Visual:** Keep a comparison sheet
**Duration:** 29s minimum

Narration: Use one entry for each observation: object identifier, image reference, letter or word, visible feature, confidence and supporting source. Write an unresolved question when the evidence is incomplete. Do not alter or clean an object just to make a photograph fit a proposed explanation. Finish by describing what you prefer about the design in ordinary language. Personal taste does not require a market claim. You might like generous spacing or a strong relationship between the text and image.
