## OVERLAY: Two different denominators
**Visual:** Side by side table: spend divided by leads, then spend divided by paying customers.
**Duration:** 30s
Narration: In this marketing-only worksheet, cost per lead and customer acquisition cost use the same kind of numerator, but different denominators. Cost per lead is included marketing spend divided by qualified leads. Customer acquisition cost is included marketing spend divided by new paying customers. A form fill, call, or quote request is not automatically a customer. Name the event that qualifies each count before comparing the results in your weekly report.

---
## OVERLAY: Fictional arithmetic
**Visual:** Clearly labeled fictional table: $600 spend, 30 leads, 6 new customers, $20 per lead, $100 per customer.
**Duration:** 30s
Narration: Here is a simplified fictional example, scoped to marketing only. Six hundred dollars of marketing spend divided by 30 leads equals 20 dollars per lead. The same 600 dollars divided by six new paying customers equals 100 dollars per customer. Those results are not contradictory, and they are not a real account benchmark or fully loaded company CAC. This illustration works because each denominator stays visible.

---
## OVERLAY: Match dates and cost scope
**Visual:** Worksheet fields for dates, included spend, sales lag, and attribution.
**Duration:** 30s
Narration: Keep the cost rule and cohort dates consistent. Write whether spend includes ads only, or also creative, software, and fees. Use the same start and end dates, then account for sales lag. A lead created this week may pay next week. Mark the customer result pending instead of treating an open sale as zero customers. If one invoice covers two campaigns, allocate it once and record the rule.

---
## OVERLAY: Check conversion labels
**Visual:** Google Ads conversion actions separating leads, calls, visits, and payments.
**Duration:** 30s
Narration: Check the label behind every conversion count. Google Ads says “All conversions” can include actions outside the main Conversions column, including certain calls, store visits, and view through conversions. Segment the actions before using them as leads. Then connect one unique lead record to one customer event so repeat forms, duplicate sources, and mixed attribution do not inflate either denominator. Small samples deserve a warning for this review.
