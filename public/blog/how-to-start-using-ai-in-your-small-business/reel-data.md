## segments
- type: overlay
  text: START USING SMALL BUSINESS
  narration: Start using AI in your small business by choosing one repeated, low-risk task and building a clear review process around it. Do not begin by buying several subscriptions or asking a chatbot to run an entire department. Pick work with known inputs and an obvious finished result, such as summarizing meeting notes, drafting a follow-up email, or organizing customer questions.
- type: overlay
  text: INCLUDE ADMINISTRATIVE WORK MARKETING
  narration: Include administrative work, marketing, sales follow-up, customer service preparation, and internal reporting. Mark tasks that take time but follow a consistent pattern. Good first projects include turning call notes into a task list, drafting a reply from an approved policy, creating an outline from a service description, or sorting anonymous feedback into themes. These jobs have source material that a person can verify.
- type: overlay
  text: MAY CREATE FIRST DRAFT
  narration: It may create a first draft, extract fields, organize ideas, or compare a result with a checklist. It should not silently replace the parts that require judgment or authorization. For a quote follow-up, the trigger might be a completed estimate. The inputs are the customer name, approved scope, price, expiration date, and booking link. AI can draft the message, but a staff member must confirm those details before sending it.
- type: overlay
  text: CHECK OUTPUT QUALITY EDITING
  narration: Check output quality, editing time, privacy settings, account controls, export options, cost, and whether your team will actually use it. Run the same prompt and source material through each candidate. One useful tool is better than several overlapping subscriptions. Keep the first test small enough that you can cancel it without disrupting customer work. AI output improves when the source information is clear.
- type: stat
  text: 120 WORD APPOINTMENT FOLLOW EMAIL
  narration: Task: State the exact result you need. Audience: Describe who will read or use it. Source: Provide the facts the answer may use. Format: Set the length, sections, and channel. Restrictions: Ban invented facts, promises, or sensitive material. Review: Ask the tool to identify missing information instead of guessing. For example: “Draft a 120-word appointment follow-up email for a homeowner.
- type: overlay
  text: INCLUDE COMPLETE INPUT MISSING
  narration: Include a complete input, missing information, conflicting notes, an unusual customer request, and a case that should be rejected. Review each result against the same checklist. Check factual accuracy, tone, privacy, format, action, and the amount of editing required. If the tool repeatedly invents a price, change the prompt and source format before another test. Do not publish the first impressive response.
- type: overlay
  text: PUBLIC MARKETING ITEM MIGHT
  narration: A public marketing item might require correct claims, current offer details, working links, brand tone, image accuracy, and a clear next step. A customer reply might require identity, account, policy, date, and promised action checks. Keep a record of who approved sensitive or high-value messages. If the AI output is wrong, the business still owns the result. Human approval protects the customer and makes responsibility clear.
- type: overlay
  text: TRAIN STAFF VERIFY RATHER
  narration: Train staff to verify rather than admire the response. AI can produce confident text that is incomplete or wrong. Employees should know how to find the source fact, ask for missing information, and stop the workflow. Set boundaries for customer communication, public claims, financial decisions, and confidential information. Review access when an employee changes roles or leaves the company. After the first workflow performs reliably, add a closely related task.
- type: overlay
  text: REMOVE PROCESSES NOBODY USES
  narration: Remove processes nobody uses and subscriptions that duplicate another tool. Starting small gives your business a useful advantage: you learn where AI actually saves time without giving up control. Pick one job this week, document it, test it with safe data, and make the approval step part of the process from day one.
