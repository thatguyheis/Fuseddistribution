---
## OVERLAY: Early payoff
**Duration:** 20s
Text: See the lesson without sound
Narration: Mute your next draft and ask whether a stranger can identify the topic, follow the explanation, and understand the final action. This is a practical review, not a test of whether music matters. Start with the opening promise, then check what the pictures and captions actually communicate without the voice explaining them.

---
## OVERLAY: Make the problem visible
**Duration:** 20s
Text: Show the missing step
Narration: For a fictional example, imagine a reel showing how to write a useful AI video brief. The weak version shows a laptop while the voice lists the instructions. The clearer version displays the audience, problem, result, and evidence on screen. The viewer can inspect the method instead of guessing what the laptop represents.

---
## OVERLAY: Review the middle
**Duration:** 20s
Text: Five checks, three groups
Narration: Use five checks in three groups. Message has two: a clear topic and a visible next step. Delivery has two: accurate captions and readable safe areas. Action has one: a specific closing question. This pie shows how this example checklist is organized. It is not a statistic about viewers or a score for accessibility.

---
## OVERLAY: Keep the ending specific
**Duration:** 20s
Text: Fix the weak scene
Narration: When a scene fails the silent test, repair the missing information rather than adding a random picture. Replace a vague background with a labeled before and after comparison, reveal the steps in order, or show the actual field being discussed. Keep important words clear of captions and the platform controls along the screen edge.

---
## OVERLAY: Review the export
**Duration:** 22s
Text: Review the actual export
Narration: Then export and repeat the check on the finished video at phone size. Read the opening, pause on the graphic, and inspect the final card. Turn sound back on and compare the captions with the narration. A silent review can expose missing context, but it does not replace checking speech accuracy, timing, or the full viewing experience.
