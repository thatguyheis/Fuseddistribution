hook: Mining stocks and physical silver create different risks, costs, and responsibilities.
hook_type: practical_checklist
## segments
- type: overlay text: COMPANY EXPOSURE narration: A mining share is company ownership. Review production, operating costs, debt, dilution, permits, and filings before treating it as silver exposure.
- type: overlay text: DIRECT OWNERSHIP narration: Physical silver is an exact product. Record weight, fineness, maker, premium, shipping, storage, insurance, and the buyer’s resale terms.
- type: overlay text: OPERATING RISK narration: A stock can rise or fall because of operating costs, financing, or dilution even when silver is steady. Production targets are plans, not guaranteed results.
- type: overlay text: COMPARE COSTS narration: Compare like for like. A mining share quote is not a physical silver retail price, and a spot reference is not a delivered or resale quote.
- type: overlay text: READ PRIMARY RECORDS narration: Read the latest company filing for its business model, risks, cash, debt, production, and share count. Use manufacturer specifications for the exact physical item.
- type: overlay text: PURPOSE AND CUSTODY narration: Physical ownership adds storage and insurance work. A mining share adds company and market exposure. Write down the purpose and all-in costs before comparing the two.
- type: question text: Which risk will you measure first? subtext: COMPARE THE RISKS narration: Choose based on purpose: company participation, direct possession, trading, or a defined combination. Review what could make the decision wrong. Which risk will you measure first?
