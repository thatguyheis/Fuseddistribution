## What is a Troy Ounce?

A troy ounce is a specific, standardized unit of weight meticulously used for measuring precious metals such as silver, gold, platinum, and palladium. It’s a critical distinction from the “ounce” you might encounter in everyday life. The troy ounce is rooted in the historical weight of one troy pound, which itself represents a long-standing unit of measurement. This measurement is essential for bullion - encompassing bars, [coins](/blog/pcgs-ngc-certified-silver-coins-guide/), and rounds - and is the benchmark against which all silver is priced and traded globally. Understanding this standard is the foundational step toward making informed decisions about your silver investments. It’s consistently used in product descriptions and pricing charts, providing a reliable reference point.

## What is a Regular Ounce?

A regular ounce, also frequently referred to as a circulating ounce or a “street ounce,” represents a different measurement of weight. It’s based on the weight of 28.35 grams, which equates to approximately one ounce of avoirdupois weight - the type of weight commonly used in grocery stores. This difference is surprisingly substantial. While a troy ounce weighs 31.1035 grams, a regular ounce weighs considerably less. This discrepancy can lead to confusion and potentially unexpected costs if you’re not fully aware of it. You’ll generally find investment-grade silver sold in troy ounces, not regular ounces.

## Choosing the Right Silver Standard: Investment vs. Collectible

The decision between using a troy ounce and a regular ounce hinges largely on your intended use for the silver. For investment-grade silver - encompassing bars, coins, and rounds - the troy ounce is the universally accepted standard. These products are specifically manufactured and priced based on the troy ounce weight, providing a consistent and reliable benchmark. Collectible silver, on the other hand, may be offered in a variety of weights, including regular ounces. It’s vital to understand the context of the sale to ensure you’re receiving the correct unit of weight and avoiding potential miscalculations. If you’re building a long-term investment portfolio, sticking with troy ounces is generally the most prudent approach. ## Understanding Spot Price and the Impact of Unit Measurement

The “spot price” of silver represents the current market price per troy ounce. This is the price at which silver is traded on the global market, reflecting supply and demand dynamics. However, the price you ultimately pay at the retailer will invariably include premiums - additional costs associated with fabrication, packaging, and the dealer’s profit margin. These premiums can vary significantly depending on the retailer and the product, but the unit measurement (troy ounce vs. Regular ounce) has a direct and measurable impact on the total cost. Always factor in the unit measurement when calculating the true cost of your silver purchase, ensuring you’re making a fully informed decision.
