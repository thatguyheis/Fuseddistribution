# How to Organize Customer Photos for Your Website

A useful customer photo library makes it easy to find a relevant image and confirm that it is suitable for publication. Start with permission and context, then prepare a website copy. Folder names and tags help your team retrieve images; they do not guarantee search rankings.

## Record customer photo permission

Keep a private record of who supplied the image, what it shows, and the uses the customer has agreed to. Confirm permission before publishing recognizable people or private spaces. If the intended use is unclear, hold the image for review instead of assuming a previous approval covers every channel.

## Separate originals from website copies

Keep the original file in a controlled library and make a separate export for the website. Group work by service or project reference, then add a short descriptive label. Avoid customer names, home addresses, and other sensitive details in public filenames, image captions, or download links.

## Choose photos that explain the service

Select a small set that answers a real visitor question: what was repaired, how the finished work looks, or which detail matters. Pair before and after images only when the comparison is accurate. Keep enough context to explain the work without revealing private customer information.

## Prepare a clear website photo

Resize the export for its intended display and compare the compressed result with the original. Inspect faces, text, and important work details for visible damage. Preview the image on a phone as well as a larger screen. A smaller file is useful only when the needed detail remains understandable.

## Write descriptions for people

Use a brief descriptive filename and alt text appropriate to the image and its purpose. Describe visible information rather than filling the text with city names or search phrases. Put a helpful caption beside the photograph when the visitor needs context that the image alone cannot convey.

## Track where each photo is published

Record the website page, public file, permission reference, and publication date in your private index. When a picture is replaced or its approved use changes, use that index to find affected pages. Keep the original and permission record under controlled access rather than uploading the entire project folder.

## A practical working record

A practical index can contain a neutral project ID, service category, capture date if known, original location, permission reference, approved uses, export filename, and live page. Keep the index private. For an illustrative plumbing project, a public name such as tap-replacement-finished.jpg describes the image without exposing an address. Do not add a location you cannot confirm. Review similar images together and publish only those that add useful detail. If your website also distributes downloads or social images, track those copies separately so a later correction reaches all of the places you control.

## Source and next steps

Google recommends descriptive image filenames, useful alt text, relevant surrounding content, and attention to image quality and loading speed. This guidance does not promise a particular ranking or sales result. [Read the primary guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-images).
