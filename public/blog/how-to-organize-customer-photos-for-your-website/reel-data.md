## segments
- type: overlay
 text: RECORD CUSTOMER PHOTO PERMISSION
 narration: Keep a private record of who supplied the image, what it shows, and the uses the customer has agreed to. Confirm permission before publishing recognizable people or private spaces. If the intended use is unclear, hold the image for review instead of assuming a previous approval covers every channel.
- type: overlay
 text: SEPARATE ORIGINALS FROM WEBSITE COPIES
 narration: Keep the original file in a controlled library and make a separate export for the website. Group work by service or project reference, then add a short descriptive label. Avoid customer names, home addresses, and other sensitive details in public filenames, image captions, or download links.
- type: overlay
 text: CHOOSE PHOTOS THAT EXPLAIN THE SERVICE
 narration: Select a small set that answers a real visitor question: what was repaired, how the finished work looks, or which detail matters. Pair before and after images only when the comparison is accurate. Keep enough context to explain the work without revealing private customer information.
- type: overlay
 text: PREPARE A CLEAR WEBSITE PHOTO
 narration: Resize the export for its intended display and compare the compressed result with the original. Inspect faces, text, and important work details for visible damage. Preview the image on a phone as well as a larger screen. A smaller file is useful only when the needed detail remains understandable.
- type: overlay
 text: WRITE DESCRIPTIONS FOR PEOPLE
 narration: Use a brief descriptive filename and alt text appropriate to the image and its purpose. Describe visible information rather than filling the text with city names or search phrases. Put a helpful caption beside the photograph when the visitor needs context that the image alone cannot convey.
- type: overlay
 text: TRACK WHERE EACH PHOTO IS PUBLISHED
 narration: Record the website page, public file, permission reference, and publication date in your private index. When a picture is replaced or its approved use changes, use that index to find affected pages. Keep the original and permission record under controlled access rather than uploading the entire project folder.

## question
text: Can you trace each customer photo to its permission?
subtext: COMMENT BELOW
narration: Start with one service gallery, not the whole archive. Check permission, choose the clearest examples, create suitable exports, and record the pages that use them. Can you find the approval and live page for each customer photo? That simple test reveals whether your library is ready for regular publishing.
