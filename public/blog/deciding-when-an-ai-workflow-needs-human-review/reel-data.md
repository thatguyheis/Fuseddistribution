# When an AI Workflow Needs Human Review

hook: Before the action, place the review.

## segments
- type: overlay
 text: LOCATE THE AI WORKFLOW DECISION POINT
 narration: Map the request, the generated result and the next action. Mark where information leaves your organization or changes an authoritative record. Ask what happens if the result is wrong at that exact point. Our guide to mapping a manual process can help identify these handoffs before you add automation. Keep drafting separate from execution. In a hypothetical appointment workflow, suggesting a reply is different from confirming a time in the booking system.
- type: overlay
 text: CHOOSE EXPLICIT TRIGGERS FOR HUMAN REVIEW
 narration: List the situations that require someone to inspect the output before the next action. Useful starting questions include whether the input is incomplete, whether two sources disagree, and whether the proposed response makes a commitment outside an approved policy. Choose triggers tied to the work rather than a vague instruction to review anything unusual.
- type: overlay
 text: MAKE THE HUMAN REVIEW STEP USABLE
 narration: Show the reviewer the original request, the proposed result and the relevant source record together. Ask for a specific decision, such as approve, revise or hold. Record who decided and what version they saw. If the output changes after approval, the old decision should not silently apply to the replacement. Plan what happens when the reviewer is unavailable.
- type: overlay
 text: TEST THE WORKFLOW BEFORE RELAXING REVIEW
 narration: Use representative examples that include missing information, conflicting inputs and a request outside the intended scope. Record whether the workflow stops at the right point and whether the reviewer can resolve the issue. A good ordinary example does not establish that the exception route works. Keep test cases with the change they were used to assess.

## question
Would you review every AI draft or focus review on the actions with consequences?
