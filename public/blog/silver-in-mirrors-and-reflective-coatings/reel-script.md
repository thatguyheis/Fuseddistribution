## HOOK

**Visual:** Silver reflects. Details matter.
**Duration:** 19s minimum

Narration: Silver is used in some mirrors and reflective coatings because of its optical properties. The presence of silver is only one part of a finished mirror's construction, however. A material description does not tell you how a complete product will perform in a particular room, instrument or outdoor installation.

---

## OVERLAY: SEPARATE THE LAYER

**Visual:** Separate the layer
**Duration:** 18s minimum

Narration: Think of the reflective material and the finished product as different questions. The first asks what substance is used in the reflective layer. The second asks how the assembled product is specified for its use. A brochure may answer one question clearly while leaving the other unanswered.

---

## OVERLAY: READ THE CONDITIONS

**Visual:** Read the conditions
**Duration:** 19s minimum

Narration: A reflectivity figure needs more than a percentage to be useful. Ask what was measured, under what conditions, and whether the claim concerns a material sample or the finished product. If a supplier provides a graph, read its axes and notes before comparing its headline figure with another supplier's number.

---

## OVERLAY: MATCH THE APPLICATION

**Visual:** Match the application
**Duration:** 20s minimum

Narration: Write down what you need the product to do before selecting a material based on a general property. A household replacement, an optical component and an exposed installation may raise different questions. Ask the supplier which product is specified for the actual intended use rather than assuming that one silver-based description settles them all.

---

## OVERLAY: KEEP CLAIMS SEPARATE

**Visual:** Keep claims separate
**Duration:** 37s minimum

Narration: An industrial use explains one reason people use a material. It does not establish the current quantity used worldwide, the value of a discarded mirror, or the future price of silver. Those questions require different evidence. Do not turn a description of reflectivity into a demand forecast. Then compare the notes by requirement rather than by marketing headline. If the question is whether a product meets a specified use, the relevant evidence is the product specification. If the question is why silver is interesting as a reflective material, the USGS overview provides useful context. Keeping those questions separate makes both explanations more accurate.
