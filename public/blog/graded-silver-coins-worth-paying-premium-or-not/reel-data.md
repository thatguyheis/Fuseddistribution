## segments
- type: overlay
 text: DEFINE YOUR BUYING OBJECTIVE
 narration: A premium for a graded silver coin should pay for something you can identify: a particular issue and condition, a useful certification record, or a collecting preference that matters to you. The number on a holder does not by itself establish a fair price. Before comparing offers, decide whether your main objective is to acquire silver weight or to collect a specific certified coin.
- type: overlay
 text: IDENTIFY THE EXACT COIN
 narration: Record the series, date, mint mark where applicable, denomination, finish, grading service, grade and any additional designation. Use the seller's photographs to identify the actual item being offered. A comparison between different issues or finishes may tell you little about the premium on the coin you want. If a listing uses a representative image, establish what will be delivered before treating it as equivalent to an offer showing an individual coin.
- type: overlay
 text: COMPARE THE COMPLETE COST
 narration: If the available examples are old, unmatched or sparse, retain that uncertainty in your decision rather than supplying a confident premium estimate. Compare the complete delivered cost of the certified coin with the complete cost of the alternative you would actually buy. Include the quoted item price and applicable payment, shipping, insurance and tax charges. Note whether the two offers use the same quantity and payment method.
- type: overlay
 text: READ THE GUARANTEE LIMITS
 narration: Compare the physical coin and holder with the certification record. A number that returns a record does not settle whether the object in front of you is the object that record describes. Certification guarantees have specific terms, remedies and exclusions. NGC's published guarantee, for example, sets conditions for covered coins and remedies for certain certification problems. It excludes lost profits and foregone appreciation.
- type: overlay
 text: RETAIN THE PURCHASE EVIDENCE
 narration: Before completing the purchase, retain the product details, comparison evidence, total cost and seller terms in one record. Ask how you would evaluate an offer to buy the coin later, including the costs of selling it. If the case for paying more relies entirely on an unsupported prediction, the evidence is incomplete. Fused's reserve resources offer further educational material. The useful question is what the premium buys today and which parts of that value you can actually verify.
