## stats
- text: ~20% EAGLE PREMIUM
  explanation: A 1 oz American Silver Eagle typically costs 18 to 25 percent over the spot price at online dealers
  graphic_type: percent_fill
  graphic:
    value: 20
    label: Premium
    remainder_label: Silver Value
  narration: A 1 oz American Silver Eagle typically runs about 20 percent over spot. Spot at 32 dollars means roughly 38 dollars and 40 cents at checkout. That gap is the premium. It covers minting, dealer overhead, insured shipping, and profit.
- text: THE PREMIUM MOVES ON ITS OWN
  explanation: Premiums follow physical demand, not the chart. They can climb while spot falls.
  graphic_type: none
  narration: The premium does not follow the chart. It follows physical demand. When buyers rush in faster than mints can produce, premiums climb even while spot falls. Two purchases at the same spot price can cost very different amounts weeks apart.
- text: BARS ~5% VS COINS ~20%
  explanation: A 10 oz bar runs 4 to 6 percent over spot while a government coin runs about 20 percent. Minting cost spreads over more ounces.
  graphic_type: percent_fill
  graphic:
    value: 5
    label: Bar Premium
    remainder_label: Silver Value
  narration: Bars are the cheap way to buy ounces. A 10 oz bar runs 4 to 6 percent over spot. Generic 1 oz rounds run 5 to 8. Government coins like Eagles run about 20. Same silver. The difference is minting cost and brand recognition.
- text: 130% ON FRACTIONAL COINS
  explanation: A 1/10 oz coin can cost 130 percent over spot, versus about 12 percent for the 1 oz version of the same coin
  graphic_type: none
  narration: Small coins flip the math completely. A one tenth ounce coin can run 130 percent over spot. The 1 oz version of the same coin runs about 12. The smaller the product, the bigger the premium per ounce.
- text: +4% FOR PAYING BY CARD
  explanation: Card and PayPal orders cost about 4 percent more than check or wire at most dealers
  graphic_type: percent_fill
  graphic:
    value: 4
    label: Card Surcharge
    remainder_label: Base Price
  narration: Most dealers list two prices. Card and PayPal orders cost about 4 percent more than check, wire, or eCheck. On a 1,000 dollar order that is 40 dollars for the convenience of plastic. Shipping works the same way. Small orders carry the cost badly.
- text: YOU DON'T GET THE PREMIUM BACK
  explanation: Dealers buy back near spot, sometimes 1 to 3 percent under it. The premium is a cost, not an investment.
  graphic_type: none
  narration: Here is the part nobody tells beginners. When you sell, dealers pay near spot, sometimes 1 to 3 percent under it. You do not get the premium back. A 20 percent premium going in means spot has to rise about 20 percent before you break even.
- text: 10 SECOND PREMIUM CHECK
  explanation: Total paid divided by ounces, minus spot, divided by spot. Run it on every quote.
  graphic_type: none
  narration: Run this check on every quote. Take the all-in total including shipping. Divide by the troy ounces. Subtract spot, then divide by spot. A 38 dollar 40 cent coin at 32 dollar spot is 20 percent. Ten seconds, and you know exactly what you are paying for.
