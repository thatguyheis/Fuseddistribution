## OVERLAY: HELP SMALL BUSINESS DRAFT

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 18s minimum

Narration: AI can help a small business draft a service page, answer customer questions, organize research, or plan a week of content. The quality of the result depends on more than the tool. It depends on whether the request gives the tool enough direction to make a useful decision. A strong prompt is a short working brief.

---

## OVERLAY: WRITE ABOUT WEBSITES LEAVES

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 20s minimum

Narration: “Write about websites” leaves too many decisions open. “Draft the opening section for a service page that helps a homeowner decide whether to request a website consultation” gives the tool a job and a reader. A clear outcome also prevents an answer from wandering. Ask for a page outline, a customer reply, a list of options, a rewrite, or a review.

## OVERLAY: USE PLAIN LANGUAGE EXPLAIN

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 21s minimum

Narration: Use plain language and explain the next step without promising a ranking or a guaranteed number of leads.” This context changes the answer. It gives the tool a voice, a customer, and a boundary. Without it, the output may sound polished while describing the wrong audience or making promises the business cannot support. A prompt should identify who will use or read the result.

---

## OVERLAY: WRITE BUSY OWNER WHO

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 21s minimum

Narration: “Write for a busy owner who understands their service but is not comfortable with marketing software” is more useful than “make it simple.” If the result will be used in more than one channel, name each audience. A website page can explain a decision in detail, while a social caption needs a shorter entry point. The prompt should not ask one paragraph to do both jobs.

---

## OVERLAY: DESCRIBE HOW ANSWER SOUND

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 21s minimum

Narration: Describe how the answer should sound and what it must avoid. Useful boundaries include plain language, a calm tone, no inflated promises, no unsupported statistics, no jargon, and no claims that require a source. A voice instruction works best when it is concrete. “Use short paragraphs, explain one idea at a time, and speak to the reader as a practical advisor” is easier to apply.

---

## OVERLAY: COMPARISON DEFINE DECISION CRITERIA

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 20s minimum

Narration: For a comparison, define the decision criteria before asking for a recommendation. Structure reduces repetition because each section has a job. A reviewer can check whether every requested part is present instead of reading a long answer and guessing what it was meant to accomplish. Do not ask for an arbitrary number of sections just to make a document look substantial.

---

## OVERLAY: USE EXAMPLE PROMISE STATISTIC

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 23s minimum

Narration: Do not use an example with a promise, statistic, or brand claim that would be unsafe in the final answer. The tool may treat the example as evidence even when you intended it only as a style reference. Ask for a draft first, then ask for a separate review. Combining “write this and make sure it is accurate” encourages the tool to approve its own work without showing what it checked.

---

## OVERLAY: BEFORE USING ANSWER RUN

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 22s minimum

Narration: Before using an answer, run a verification pass with explicit checks. Ask whether every number, date, quoted statement, and named source appears in the supplied material. Ask whether the title matches the body and whether the call to action matches the actual business offer. For operational content, check that the steps can be performed as written. For marketing content, check that the offer, audience, and destination are consistent.

---

## OVERLAY: GOAL MAKE EVERY INSTRUCTION

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 22s minimum

Narration: The goal is to make every instruction necessary. A concise prompt with a clear outcome and trusted source material will usually outperform a long prompt filled with generic advice. Use this template as a starting point: “Task: describe the result. Constraints: list claims, words, or promises to avoid. If a required fact is missing, mark it NEEDS REVIEW instead of guessing.” Fill in the brackets before sending the request.
