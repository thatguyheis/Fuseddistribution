## HOOK

**Visual:** Check beyond the strike
**Duration:** 25s minimum

Narration: A coin’s quality checks can happen before and after a press strikes it. The U.S. Mint describes checks on incoming planchets and sample inspection after striking. That is different from saying every coin gets the same hand inspection or receives a collector grade. This article focuses on the Mint’s circulating coin description and does not claim all mints use an identical line.

---

## OVERLAY: Check the incoming planchet

**Visual:** Material enters the press
**Duration:** 24s minimum

Narration: A planchet is a prepared metal piece that will become a coin. The Mint says inspectors check purchased planchets against required specifications before production. This check concerns the material going into the process. It does not assign a grade to the finished collectible. A specification check happens before the design is struck and is separate from reviewing the press output.

---

## OVERLAY: Inspect samples after striking

**Visual:** Look for production errors
**Duration:** 24s minimum

Narration: After the press strikes coins, the Mint says an inspector examines samples for errors. The public process description does not state a universal sampling rate, so do not turn it into a claim about every coin being checked individually. A sample inspection is one part of the production process. The Mint’s public page does not give a sample size or list every error category checked, so those details remain unknown.

---

## OVERLAY: Handle pieces outside standards

**Visual:** Circulating output has a reject path
**Duration:** 24s minimum

Narration: The Mint says circulating coins that fail certain standards may be sent to a waffler. That machine bends the coins before they are recycled. Keep the example scoped to the Mint’s described circulating process. Other products and producers may use different checks or handling steps. The page says coins that fail certain standards may be diverted; it does not say all rejects receive the same treatment.

---

## OVERLAY: Count and weigh accepted coins

**Visual:** Inspection then packaging
**Duration:** 24s minimum

Narration: After circulating coins pass inspection, the Mint says they are counted and weighed before bagging. Those steps prepare accepted output for packaging. A bag weight does not tell you the condition of each coin inside, and production inspection is not the same as later collector grading. Counting and weighing prepare output for bulk storage, not individual condition grading.
