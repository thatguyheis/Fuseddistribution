If you find a damaged coin holder, pause the usual inventory work and record the problem before moving the coin. A cracked case, loose closure, or visible residue needs a clear description, not a guessed diagnosis. Keep the coin protected while you decide the next step. Separating the item for review should not mean opening it or transferring it into whatever spare holder happens to be nearby.

## Identify the affected holder

Record the collection ID, holder type, and where the damage is visible. Photograph the holder from a position that does not require extra handling of the coin. Note whether the issue was present when you received the item or was noticed later. If you cannot establish when it occurred, say that. Do not turn a recent discovery into an unsupported claim that the damage is new.

## Separate the record and storage position

Give the item a clearly identified review location within your existing secure storage arrangement. Label the surrounding container or index card rather than adding adhesive to the coin or its holder. Update the inventory location immediately so the item does not appear lost during the next count. Keep unrelated holders out of the review group unless you have observed a reason to include them.

## Retain the original evidence

Keep the purchase record, seller description, and earlier photographs connected to the same inventory entry. Preserve the existing holder and packaging when asking for advice about a problem. Do not scrub residue or polish the coin to improve a photograph. A clean looking image is less useful if the action changes the condition you were trying to document or removes evidence needed to understand the issue.

## Ask a specific question

Describe the holder problem to a qualified coin professional and ask what handling or replacement approach is appropriate. Explain whether the coin is in original packaging or a grading service holder. Avoid assuming that damage to the case establishes damage to the coin. Record the advice, who provided it, and the next agreed step instead of converting a general comment into a promise about condition or value.

## Close the review without losing history

After the chosen action is completed, update the current holder and location while retaining the earlier observation. Add photographs of the result if they can be taken without unnecessary handling. Mark any unanswered question as unresolved rather than deleting it from the record. A useful review ends with a traceable decision and a protected coin, not merely with a box moved back into the main collection.
