The Mint identifies Isaac Scott Hathaway as the coin’s designer; the obverse pairs Washington and Carver, and the reverse shows a map of the United States. This paired issue showcased the contributions of two prominent figures at Tuskegee Institute. The design reflected the dual focus of the commemorative coins: honoring the legacy of Booker T. Washington, the founder of the school, and recognizing the groundbreaking work of George Washington Carver in agricultural science. The map reverse provided a visual representation of the nation, subtly connecting Carver’s work to the broader American landscape and agricultural potential.

The Mint states that the coins were issued no later than August 7, 1954, and that proceeds went to named beneficiary organizations. This final issue date marked the conclusion of a series produced between 1951 and 1954. The proceeds from the sale of these commemorative half dollars were directed to specific organizations, reflecting a deliberate effort to support institutions aligned with the themes of education and agricultural advancement.

Furthermore, the series was the last U.S. commemorative issue until the modern program began in 1982. This signifies a deliberate ending to a specific type of commemorative coin production. The final issue date of August 7, 1954, represents the culmination of this particular commemorative effort.
