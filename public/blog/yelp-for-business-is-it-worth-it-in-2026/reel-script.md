## OVERLAY: SEPARATE FREE PAGES AND ADS

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 22s minimum

Narration: Yelp is worth evaluating as a place where potential customers may check your business, but a free business page and paid advertising are separate decisions. Start with accurate information and a way to handle inquiries. Consider spending money only after defining what a useful inquiry looks like and how you will compare the cost with completed work. There is no reliable universal answer for every trade, town, or business model.

---

## OVERLAY: CHECK LOCAL RELEVANCE

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 22s minimum

Narration: Search for the service a customer would actually need in the area you serve. Note whether the results describe businesses comparable with yours, whether the language matches the work you want, and what questions appear repeatedly. Treat this as an observation of the current customer experience, not a scientific estimate of demand or a ranking guarantee. Speak with your own customers about how they found and evaluated the business.

---

## OVERLAY: UNDERSTAND WHAT A CLICK MEANS

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 19s minimum

Narration: Review your contact page as part of the same exercise so visitors have a consistent next step after leaving a directory. Yelp describes cost per click advertising in its guidance: a click is the billed action for that format, not a promise that the person will become a customer. Confirm the particular product and billing basis you are purchasing.

---

## OVERLAY: COUNT USEFUL INQUIRIES

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 20s minimum

Narration: Count qualified inquiries using the definition you chose before the test. Record which ones became appointments or completed jobs, and note why others did not. A low cost per click can still accompany many unsuitable requests. Compare the completed work with the money and staff time required to obtain and handle it. Use your actual records rather than a universal conversion assumption.

---

## OVERLAY: COMPARE THE SAME OUTCOMES

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 22s minimum

Narration: Use consistent definitions when comparing Yelp with another source of inquiries. If one report counts clicks and another counts completed jobs, their totals answer different questions. Choose a comparable period, identify the same type of work, and include the costs you actually incurred. Note where you cannot confidently attribute a customer to one source. Avoid changing several variables at once if you want to learn from the test.
