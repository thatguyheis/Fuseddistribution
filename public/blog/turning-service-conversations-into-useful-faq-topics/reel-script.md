# Reel Script: turning-service-conversations-into-useful-faq-topics
format: long-form
engagement-policy: 1
content-kind: educate

---

## HOOK

**Visual:** Real questions. Useful answers.
**Duration:** 16s minimum

Narration: Use service conversations to identify questions people actually need answered, then turn the recurring decisions into clear FAQ topics. Preserve the meaning of the question while removing private details. Have the person responsible for the service approve the answer before publishing it.

---

## OVERLAY: PROTECT CUSTOMER DETAILS

**Visual:** Protect customer details
**Duration:** 20s minimum

Narration: Start with conversation records you are allowed to review. Extract the question or uncertainty, not the customer's identity. Remove names, contact details, order references and distinctive circumstances that are unnecessary for the general topic. If a situation is too specific to explain without exposing it, use an original hypothetical example and label it accordingly.

---

## OVERLAY: FIND THE DECISION

**Visual:** Find the decision
**Duration:** 17s minimum

Narration: Customers may ask the same underlying question in different ways. “Can you help with this?” and “Is this included?” could both concern service scope, but context may reveal different needs. Read enough context to understand the decision before merging questions into one topic.

---

## OVERLAY: VERIFY THE ANSWER

**Visual:** Verify the answer
**Duration:** 20s minimum

Narration: Ask the responsible team member to state the answer and its conditions. Use current service documentation where available. If the business has not decided a policy, leave that as an internal action item rather than inventing an answer that sounds helpful. A website should not become the place where accidental promises are created.

---

## OVERLAY: CHOOSE THE RIGHT PAGE

**Visual:** Choose the right page
**Duration:** 45s minimum

Narration: Place a service-specific question near the related service information when that makes the answer easier to find. Use a separate page when the topic requires a substantial explanation or serves a distinct purpose. Do not duplicate a full answer across multiple pages without a maintenance plan for keeping them consistent. Record whether a proposed topic came from repeated observations, one conversation or a team suggestion. That distinction prevents an isolated question from being described as something every customer asks. Review new conversations for gaps, but do not add a new FAQ for every unusual request. Sometimes the right response is a private clarification or a better existing explanation. The goal is a maintainable set of answers that helps visitors make decisions, with evidence and ownership behind the wording.
