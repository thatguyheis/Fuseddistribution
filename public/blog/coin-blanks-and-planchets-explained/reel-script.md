## HOOK

**Visual:** Before the coin has a design
**Duration:** 14s minimum

Narration: A blank is a metal disc used before a coin receives its design. In the United States Mint explanation, a blank becomes a planchet after a raised rim is formed. These terms describe a process, not a guarantee of value.

---

## OVERLAY: FOLLOW THE PRODUCTION STAGE

**Visual:** Follow the production stage
**Duration:** 23s minimum

Narration: The distinction helps you understand what happens before striking. The Mint also notes that people sometimes use blank more broadly for a coin before it is struck. Check how your source defines the word. A precise term in a production diagram may be used more casually in a listing or conversation. The label alone does not resolve that difference.

---

## OVERLAY: STRIKING ADDS THE DESIGN

**Visual:** Striking adds the design
**Duration:** 21s minimum

Narration: During striking, dies impress the design into the planchet. Keep that general explanation separate from the identification of an unknown item. A simplified diagram is a memory aid, not a scale drawing or a test of authenticity. Do not use its illustrated diameter, color or shape as evidence about a real object in your collection.

---

## OVERLAY: SEPARATE CLAIMS FROM EVIDENCE

**Visual:** Separate claims from evidence
**Duration:** 23s minimum

Narration: If a seller calls an object a planchet, save the exact description and any supporting documentation. Distinguish that attributed statement from your own conclusions. A purchase record may establish what was represented and what you paid. It does not automatically prove the material, mint of origin or specialist classification. Write down which questions remain unanswered instead of filling them with guesses.

---

## OVERLAY: KEEP A USEFUL INVENTORY

**Visual:** Keep a useful inventory
**Duration:** 40s minimum

Narration: Connect the listing, invoice and photographs to the same inventory identifier. Describe visible features plainly when a technical term is uncertain. Keep the original images and the source wording. Your notes can preserve a claim without endorsing it. A general minting guide belongs with your research; it is not documentation that a particular object came from that mint. Use blank and planchet to understand the manufacturing story, then look for separate evidence about an individual item. Neither word tells you purity, rarity or price. Do not alter an object to test a theory from an illustration. Keep the item and its records intact. Which coin production term would you like explained next?
