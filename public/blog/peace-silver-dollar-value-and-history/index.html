What to Know About Peace Silver Dollars

Peace silver dollars were minted from 1909 to 1935 to commemorate the end of World War I. They’re instantly recognizable by their obverse depicting Lady Liberty with a helmet and olive branch, and their reverse showing an eagle holding arrows and olive branches. While initially intended as a patriotic symbol, they quickly gained popularity among collectors, and some dates and mint marks are now incredibly rare and valuable. Understanding the history, grading standards, and market trends is crucial to assessing the value of any Peace silver dollar you’re considering.

A Brief History of the Peace Silver Dollar

The story begins with the Spanish Royal Mint's decision to halt silver shipments to the United States due to the ongoing conflict. The U.S. Mint needed a new design to continue producing silver dollars. The result was the Peace silver dollar, designed by Frank H. Meech and engraved by William de Wane. The initial 1909-S (San Francisco) issue was only 417,500 pieces, making it immediately scarce. This scarcity, coupled with the coin’s patriotic significance, fueled early collector interest.

The 1910-D (Denver) and 1910-S (San Francisco) issues were even smaller - just 259,000 and 157,000 pieces, respectively. The mintages continued to decrease throughout the series, with the 1913-S (Philadelphia) being the rarest of all, produced in just 1,400 pieces. The 1914-S, 1916-S, and 1917-S also saw dramatically reduced mintages. The 1921-S, for instance, had a mintage of only 8,400. This fluctuating production based on the mints involved created a wide range of values across the series.

Factors Affecting Peace Silver Dollar Value

Several factors contribute to the value of a Peace silver dollar. These include:

  • Year and Mint Mark: As mentioned, the rarer the year and mint mark combination, the higher the value. The 1913-S is the undisputed king, followed closely by the 1914-S, 1916-S, and 1917-S.
  • Mint Condition: The condition of the coin is essential. Coins are graded on a scale from Poor (P) to Mint State (MS). A coin in Mint State (MS) 66 or higher commands a premium. A coin in Poor (P) condition will be significantly less valuable.
  • Rarity: Low mintages directly translate to higher rarity and, therefore, higher value.
  • Eye Appeal: Even within the same grade, a coin with attractive luster, minimal damage, and a pleasing appearance will be more desirable to collectors.
  • Provenance: A coin's history - its previous owners and where it’s been - can add to its value, especially if it has a documented lineage.

Grading Peace Silver Dollars

Understanding coin grading is essential for determining a Peace silver dollar’s value. The Professional Coin Grading Service (PCGS) and the Numismatic Guaranty Company (NGC) are the two leading grading services. They assess a coin's condition based on seven factors: For more on this, see First Majestic Silver vs Physical Silver Investment.

  1. Surface: The overall smoothness and polish of the coin’s surface.
  2. Marks: Scratches, hairlines, and other imperfections.
  3. Wear: The degree of wear on the coin’s design elements.
  4. Luster: The reflective quality of the coin’s surface.
  5. Tone: The color of the coin’s surface.
  6. Detail: The sharpness of the coin’s design elements.
  7. Cabinet Tone: The color of the coin when displayed in a cabinet.

Here’s a simplified breakdown of common grades:

  • Poor (P): Heavily worn, significant damage.
  • Fair (FR): Moderate wear, some damage.
  • Good (GD): Noticeable wear, some details obscured.
  • Very Good (VG): Moderate wear, most details visible.
  • Fine (F): Moderate wear, details well-defined.
  • Very Fine (VF): Light wear, details sharp.
  • Mint State (MS): Uncirculated, with original luster. (MS60 is the minimum grade for a significant value)

Buying Peace Silver Dollars - Where to Start

If you're looking to add Peace silver dollars to your collection, here’s a breakdown of where to start:

  1. Research: Thoroughly research different dates and mint marks to identify those that align with your budget and collecting interests.
  2. Reputable Dealers: Purchase from trusted coin dealers who offer authentication and grading services. We recommend dealing with established dealers who specialize in silver coins.
  3. Online Auctions: Platforms like eBay and Heritage Auctions can offer opportunities to find Peace silver dollars, but exercise caution and verify the seller's reputation.
  4. Coin Shows: Attending coin shows provides an opportunity to examine coins in person and interact with dealers and collectors.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Ignoring Condition: Don’t overpay for a coin in poor condition. Condition significantly impacts value.
  • Buying Without Authentication: Always purchase authenticated coins from reputable sources.
  • Falling for Fakes: Counterfeit Peace silver dollars exist. Buy from trusted dealers who can guarantee authenticity.
  • Ignoring Mint Marks: Pay close attention to the mint mark, as it’s a key factor in determining rarity and value.

Protecting Your Peace Silver Dollars

Once you’ve acquired Peace silver dollars, proper storage is crucial to preserving their value. Here are some recommendations:

  • Coin Holders: Use coin holders (cards or capsules) to protect coins from scratches and damage.
  • Coin Albums: Coin albums provide a secure and organized way to store and display your collection.
  • Proper Storage Environment: Store coins in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight and extreme temperatures.
