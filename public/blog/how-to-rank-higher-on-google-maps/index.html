How To Rank Higher On Google Maps

August 9, 2026 Local Business Marketing

Are you a small business owner struggling to get noticed on Google Maps? You’re not alone. Millions of businesses compete for visibility in local search results, and it can feel like an uphill battle. But don’t worry, there are proven strategies you can implement to climb the ranks and attract more customers. At Fused Distribution, we specialize in helping local businesses like yours thrive online, and today, we’re breaking down exactly how to rank higher on Google Maps. This isn’t about magic - it’s about smart, targeted efforts that deliver real results. Let’s get started.

What to Know About Google Maps Ranking

Google Maps isn’t just a place to find directions; it’s a powerful search engine for local businesses. When someone searches for “pizza near me,” Google Maps displays a list of relevant businesses, ranked by relevance. Ranking higher means more visibility, more clicks, and ultimately, more customers walking through your door. The good news is that Google’s algorithm is designed to reward businesses that provide valuable information and a positive user experience. It’s not a complex system, but it does require consistent attention and a strategic approach. According to a 2024 study, 88% of people use Google Maps to find local businesses, highlighting the critical importance of optimizing your presence.

How to Get Started: The Foundation

Before you start tweaking your profile, let’s prepare. This is the most crucial stage.

1. Claim and Verify Your Google Business Profile (GBP)

This is non-negotiable. You must claim and verify your GBP listing. This proves you own the business and gives you control over your online presence. It’s free and incredibly simple. Go to Google Business Profile and follow the instructions to claim and verify your listing. Verification typically involves receiving a postcard with a code or a phone call from Google. A 2023 report indicated that businesses with verified profiles consistently outperform those that haven’t been verified.

2. Complete Your Profile Thoroughly

Don’t just claim it; populate it. A complete profile signals to Google that you’re serious about being found. Fill out every section:

  • Business Name: Use your official business name - don’t add extra words like “Inc.” or “LLC.”
  • Address: Accurate and consistent with your signage.
  • Phone Number: A working phone number.
  • Website: Link to your website.
  • Category: Choose the most relevant category for your business. Be specific! For example, instead of just “Restaurant,” choose “Italian Restaurant.”
  • Hours of Operation: Keep these updated, especially during holidays. Accurate hours are vital for customer expectations.
  • Description: Write a compelling description of your business, incorporating relevant keywords. Aim for 750 characters or less. This is your chance to tell your story and entice potential customers.

Optimizing Your GBP for Search

Now that your profile is complete, let’s focus on optimizing it for search.

3. Keyword Research - What Are People Searching For?

Think like your customers. What terms do they use when searching for your products or services? Use Google Keyword Planner or similar tools to identify relevant keywords. For instance, if you own a dog grooming salon, consider keywords like “dog grooming near me,” “pet grooming services,” and “dog haircuts.” Incorporate these keywords naturally into your business name, description, and posts.

4. Strategic Use of Posts

GBP posts are a fantastic way to share updates, promotions, and events. They’re also a ranking factor. Create posts about new products, special offers, or upcoming events. For example, a bakery could post about a new seasonal pie or a holiday sale. Aim for at least one post per month. A recent study found that businesses with active posts receive 67% more views on their GBP listing, demonstrating the value of consistent engagement.

5. High-Quality Photos and Videos

Visuals are crucial. Upload high-quality photos and videos of your business, products, and services. Showcase your team, your storefront, and happy customers. Consider using a professional photographer for a polished look. Google prioritizes businesses with visually appealing profiles.

Gathering and Managing Reviews

Reviews are extremely important for Google Maps ranking. Positive reviews build trust and credibility, while negative reviews can hurt your ranking.

6. Encourage Reviews - Politely!

Don’t ask for reviews directly. Instead, provide excellent customer service and make it easy for customers to leave reviews. You can include a link to your GBP review page in your email signature, on your website, and on receipts. A 2024 study found that businesses with at least 50 reviews are more likely to appear on the first page of Google Maps, suggesting a critical mass is needed for optimal visibility.

7. Respond to All Reviews - Good and Bad

Responding to reviews shows you care about your customers. Acknowledge positive reviews and address negative reviews constructively. Offer solutions or apologies when appropriate. Responding to reviews can improve your ranking and demonstrate engagement. A prompt and thoughtful response can even turn a negative review into a positive experience.

Technical SEO for Google Maps

Beyond your GBP profile, technical SEO factors play a role.

8. Mobile-Friendliness

Google prioritizes mobile-friendly websites and GBP listings. Ensure your website is responsive and loads quickly on mobile devices. Over 60% of Google Maps searches are performed on mobile devices, making mobile optimization absolutely essential.

9. Website Integration

Link your website to your GBP listing. This helps Google understand your business and improves your overall online presence. A smooth connection between your website and your GBP profile strengthens your local SEO strategy.

Ongoing Maintenance and Monitoring

Ranking on Google Maps isn’t a one-time task. It requires ongoing maintenance and monitoring.

10. Track Your Ranking

Use a tool like BrightLocal or Semrush to track your ranking for relevant keywords. This will help you identify areas for improvement and measure the effectiveness of your optimization efforts.

11. Regularly Update Your Profile

Keep your information accurate and up-to-date. Update your hours, posts, and photos regularly. Stale information can negatively impact your ranking.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Let’s address some common pitfalls that can hinder your Google Maps ranking.

12. Incomplete or Inaccurate Information

As mentioned earlier, a missing or inaccurate profile is a major red flag for Google. Double-check everything.

13. Ignoring Reviews

Ignoring reviews is a missed opportunity. Respond to all reviews, both positive and negative.

14. Using Irrelevant Keywords

Stuffing your description with keywords will hurt your ranking. Write naturally and focus on providing valuable information.

15. Lack of Consistency

Inconsistency in your branding, messaging, and online presence can confuse Google and hurt your ranking.

Looking Ahead: The Future of Google Maps Ranking

Google’s algorithm is constantly evolving. Staying ahead of the curve requires continuous learning and adaptation. Focus on providing a great user experience, building trust, and consistently optimizing your GBP listing. In the future, Google will likely place even greater emphasis on local relevance and user engagement. A 2024 Google update emphasized the importance of “E-A-T” (Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) in local search results. By following these strategies, you’ll be well-positioned to succeed in the competitive world of Google Maps.
