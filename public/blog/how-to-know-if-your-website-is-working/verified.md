## The Basic Check: Are You Getting Calls?
A website is a vital piece of the puzzle, but it’s not the whole picture. A website without tracking is like a store with no way to record sales - you won't know what's working. The first step is to establish a simple tracking method to understand where your leads are coming from. Without this data, you’re operating on assumptions, and assumptions rarely lead to success. Start by setting up call tracking. This involves installing a unique phone number on your website that redirects all incoming calls to a specific number you can monitor. You’ll receive reports detailing the source of each call - whether it originated from a website visit, a [Google](/blog/google-analytics-for-small-business-what-to-track/) search, or another marketing channel.
Next, check your Google Business Profile. It’s often overlooked, but it’s a powerful tool for local visibility. Make sure your profile is complete - include your business hours, address, phone number, [website](/blog/website-analytics-for-local-business-beginner-guide/) URL, and a detailed description of your services. Upload high-quality photos of your business, both inside and out. Regularly update your profile with new posts and offers. Don’t just passively view your profile; actively engage with customers who leave comments or ask questions. A high level of engagement signals to Google that your business is active and relevant. Google Business Profile engagement is up 35% year over year.
Search for your service and your city. For example, if you’re a plumber in Austin, Texas, type “plumber Austin Texas” into Google. Where does your website appear in the search results? If you’re not in the top three, you’re losing potential customers to competitors who are ranking higher. Test your contact form. Is it easy to find? Does it ask for too much information upfront? A complicated form will deter potential customers. Keep it simple and focused on capturing the essential details. A streamlined contact form can increase lead generation by up to 15%.
Finally, look at where your reviews come from. Positive reviews are incredibly influential, especially for local businesses. If your reviews are primarily appearing on social media platforms like Facebook or Instagram, you’re missing out on the power of Google search. Google prioritizes reviews that originate from its own platform. Actively encourage your customers to leave reviews on Google.
## The Visibility Test: Are People Finding You on Google?
The visibility test is all about understanding if people are actually *finding* your website when they need your services. It’s the foundation of your online marketing strategy. Without this, you’re essentially invisible to potential customers searching for your business online.
First, check your call tracking again. If you're not seeing a consistent flow of calls originating from your website, it’s a clear indication that you need to improve your local SEO. It’s not enough to just *have* a website; it needs to be discoverable.
Next, audit your Google Business Profile meticulously. Is your profile complete and accurate? Are your photos current and appealing? Are you consistently posting updates and offers? Google uses your profile as a key indicator of your business’s relevance. A well-maintained profile increases your chances of appearing in local search results.
Then, conduct a local search test. Search for your exact service and your city. For instance, if you’re a landscaping company in Denver, Colorado, type “landscaping Denver Colorado” into Google. Where does your website rank? If you're not in the top three, you need to address your local SEO strategy. Improving your local ranking is crucial for attracting local customers.
Look at the types of keywords you’re targeting. Are you using relevant local terms? Are you optimizing your website content for local searches? Keyword research is essential for ensuring that your website appears in the right searches.
Finally, analyze your website’s backlinks. Backlinks are links from other websites to yours. They’re a crucial ranking factor for Google. Building high-quality backlinks can significantly improve your website’s visibility in search results.
## The Review Reality: Are Reviews Actually Converting?
Reviews aren’t just nice-to-haves; they’re powerful sales tools. Positive reviews build trust and credibility, influencing potential customers’ decisions. However, simply *having* reviews isn’t enough; you need to understand where those reviews are coming from and how they’re impacting your business.
You need to track the source of your reviews. If most of your reviews are appearing on social media platforms, you’re missing out on the significant impact of Google reviews. Google reviews carry more weight than social media reviews because they appear directly in the search results.
Next, audit your Google Business Profile again. Are you actively encouraging customers to leave reviews on Google? You can send automated emails to customers after they’ve completed a service, asking them to share their experience on Google. Responding to reviews - both positive and negative - shows that you value your customers’ feedback.
Test your local search. Search for your service and your city. Are you seeing reviews prominently displayed in the search results? If not, you need to improve your review strategy.
Look at the sentiment of your reviews. Are they overwhelmingly positive? Are there any recurring themes or concerns? Analyzing your reviews can provide valuable insights into your business’s strengths and weaknesses.
Finally, track the number of reviews you’re receiving. A steady stream of reviews is a sign that you’re providing good service and attracting satisfied customers. Aim for at least 50 reviews to establish a strong online reputation.
## The Conversion Test: Is Your Contact Form Working?
The conversion test focuses on whether your website is actually generating leads and driving business. It’s about turning website visitors into customers. If you can’t track the results of your efforts, you’re essentially guessing.
First, revisit your call tracking data. Are you seeing a clear correlation between website visits and phone calls? If not, you need to identify the bottlenecks in your conversion process.
Next, audit your Google Business Profile. Is your profile complete and accurate? Does it include a clear call to action, such as “Call Us Today”? Make sure your contact information is up-to-date and easy to find.
Then, analyze your website’s analytics. What pages are visitors spending the most time on? What are they clicking on? Identifying your most popular pages can help you optimize your website for conversions.
Look at your website’s landing pages. Are they designed to capture leads? Do they include a clear call to action? A well-designed landing page can significantly improve your conversion rate.
Finally, test your contact form. Is it easy to use? Does it ask for only the essential information? A streamlined contact form will reduce friction and increase the likelihood of a lead becoming a customer. A/B testing different versions of your contact form can help you identify the most effective design.
Don’t just build a website; build a system for capturing leads and converting them into customers. By tracking your results and making data-driven decisions, you can maximize your website’s impact on your business.
