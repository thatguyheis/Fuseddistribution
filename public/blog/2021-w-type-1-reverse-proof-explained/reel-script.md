## OVERLAY: THE CONTRAST IS REVERSED

**Visual:** THE CONTRAST IS REVERSED
Text: THE CONTRAST IS REVERSED
Narration: Start with the surface. On a conventional proof, the background is reflective and the raised design is frosted. A reverse proof switches that contrast: the background is frosted, while the design has a mirrorlike finish.

---

## OVERLAY: REVERSE PROOF IS A FINISH

**Visual:** REVERSE PROOF IS A FINISH
Text: REVERSE PROOF IS A FINISH
Narration: That means reverse proof describes how the coin looks. It does not mean the eagle is facing backward, or that only the back received a special finish.

---

## OVERLAY: THE TYPE ONE DESIGN

**Visual:** THE TYPE ONE DESIGN
Text: THE TYPE ONE DESIGN
Narration: Now look at the eagle and shield. This is John Mercanti's heraldic eagle, the reverse design introduced with the Silver Eagle in 1986. The W below the design identifies West Point.

---

## OVERLAY: 2021 / A DESIGNER EDITION

**Visual:** 2021 / A DESIGNER EDITION
Text: 2021 / A DESIGNER EDITION
Narration: In 2021, the American Eagle program reached its thirty-fifth anniversary. The Mint marked that transition with a Designer Edition set pairing two reverse proofs: the original design from West Point and the new design from San Francisco.

---

## OVERLAY: A LIMIT ON SETS

**Visual:** A LIMIT ON SETS
Text: A LIMIT ON SETS
Narration: Sales opened September thirteenth. The announced production limit was one hundred twenty-five thousand sets. That was a set limit, not a count of coins graded seventy.

---

## OVERLAY: PF70 / THE CONDITION

**Visual:** PF70 / THE CONDITION
Text: PF70 / THE CONDITION
Narration: The holder here reads Reverse PF70. NGC defines a seventy as having no post-production imperfections visible at five-times magnification. That grade describes condition under its standard, while Type One identifies the design.

---

## OVERLAY: FIRST DAY OF ISSUE

**Visual:** FIRST DAY OF ISSUE
Text: FIRST DAY OF ISSUE
Narration: First Day of Issue is another distinction. NGC's published requirements tied it to purchase and receipt deadlines, supported by evidence. It does not establish the day this individual coin was struck.
