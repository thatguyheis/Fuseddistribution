## HOOK

**Visual:** Proof coins: fewer touches
**Duration:** 20s minimum

Narration: The easiest handling decision for a proof silver coin is to leave it in its intact protective holder when you can inspect it there. If handling a raw coin is necessary, prepare the workspace and destination first. Limit the number of transfers, work on one coin at a time, and stop if the coin cannot be moved with a steady grip.

---

## OVERLAY: KEEP ORIGINAL HOLDERS

**Visual:** Keep original holders
**Duration:** 15s minimum

Narration: Write down the purpose of opening or moving the coin. Reading the certificate, recording the year, or photographing the case may not require opening a capsule. Separate the task you want to finish from the assumption that the coin must be removed to finish it.

---

## OVERLAY: PREPARE THE WORKSPACE

**Visual:** Prepare the workspace
**Duration:** 28s minimum

Narration: Clear a stable table and place a clean, soft work surface where you can see it well. Move drinks, loose tools, and unrelated coins away. Arrange the destination holder and inventory label first, then check that you can reach both without stretching across the workspace. Choose a time when you can finish without interruption. If someone needs the table or you expect to leave shortly, postpone the transfer. A paused task should end with the coin protected, not with a loose coin waiting beside an open holder.

---

## OVERLAY: HANDLE ONE COIN

**Visual:** Handle one coin
**Duration:** 18s minimum

Narration: Start with the inventory record for the coin in front of you. Confirm the holder label, year, and other visible identifying details against that record. Keep the rest of the collection in its normal storage while you work. This makes it easier to notice a mismatched label before it follows the wrong coin into a box.

---

## OVERLAY: PHOTOGRAPH THROUGH HOLDERS

**Visual:** Photograph through holders
**Duration:** 17s minimum

Narration: Set up the camera before bringing the coin into position. Use the holder as part of the photograph when it allows the identifying detail to be recorded. Check the picture on screen while the coin remains protected. A photograph that needs better lighting is a camera problem to solve before another transfer.
