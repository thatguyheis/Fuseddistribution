Local Backlinks: How to Get Them for Free

June 16, 2026 Local Business Marketing

Want more local customers finding your business online? Local backlinks, mentions of your business on other websites, are key for boosting your search engine ranking, especially in Google's local results. They signal to Google that your business is trusted and relevant within your community. The good news? You don't need a huge marketing budget to build them. Pages without any backlinks get no organic search traffic 91% of the time, so even a handful of free local links puts you ahead. This guide shows you exactly how to get them, focused on actions you can take today.

Why Local Backlinks Matter for Your Business

Local backlinks differ from general backlinks. They come from websites within your geographic area, showing Google that your business has local authority. According to Whitespark's 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors, businesses with local backlinks from regional sites, newspapers, and industry associations rank 14% higher in the Local Pack than those without. That translates to:

  • More Local Visibility: Your business shows up higher in Google Maps and local search results.
  • More Foot Traffic: Local customers searching for your services or products are more likely to find you.
  • Better Brand Awareness: Being mentioned by local websites builds trust and credibility within your community.
  • Stronger SEO: Google reads local backlinks as a strong signal of local relevance.

Strategies to Get Free Local Backlinks

Here's a breakdown of strategies, ranked roughly by ease and impact:

1. Local Business Directories (Easy & Highly Effective)

This is the foundation. Get listed on as many relevant local directories as possible. Don't just stick to the big ones. Explore niche directories specific to your industry.

  • Google Business Profile (GBP): Essential. Claim and optimize your GBP listing. GBP signals account for 32% of Local Pack ranking weight, so this is the most important local property you control.
  • Yelp: A powerhouse for local businesses, especially restaurants and service providers.
  • Bing Places for Business: Bing's equivalent to GBP.
  • Apple Maps Connect: Important for visibility on Apple devices.
  • Yellow Pages (Yellowpages.com): Still relevant, particularly for older demographics.
  • Industry-Specific Directories: Search "[your industry] directory [your city]". You'll find tons of options. Examples: Chamber of Commerce sites, local restaurant guides, contractor directories.

Tip: Keep your NAP (Name, Address, Phone Number) consistent across all listings. Inconsistent NAP confuses search engines and hurts your ranking.

2. Local News Websites & Blogs (Medium Effort, High Impact)

Local news sites and blogs are strong sources of backlinks. Given that buying a single backlink averages $361.44 (BuzzStream, 2025), free editorial links from local press are worth real money.

  • Press Releases: Send press releases for newsworthy events. New product launches, community involvement, awards, and so on. Sites like PRWeb (paid, with free trials) or local news outlets often republish press releases.
  • Guest Blogging: Offer to write guest posts for local blogs in your industry or related niches. It's a great way to earn a high-quality backlink.
  • Local Event Listings: Many local news sites keep event calendars. Submit your events for inclusion.
  • Citation Building: Look for local blogs or news sites that mention businesses similar to yours. Reach out and politely ask if they'd be willing to mention you with a link.

Tip: Personalize your outreach. Don't send generic emails. Show you've read their content and understand their audience.

3. Community Involvement & Sponsorships (High Effort, Long-Term Value)

This builds real relationships and earns valuable backlinks.

  • Sponsor Local Events: Sponsoring a local sports team, festival, or charity event often comes with a website listing and a backlink.
  • Partner with Local Charities: Donating to or volunteering with local charities can earn mentions on their websites.
  • Local Business Associations: Join your local Chamber of Commerce or other business associations. These groups often keep member directories with backlinks.
  • Local School & University Websites: Offer to sponsor a school program, donate supplies, or host a workshop. Schools often have alumni networks and sites that link to local businesses.

Tip: Document your involvement. Take photos and share them on social media, tagging the organization. That boosts visibility and reinforces the connection.

4. Local Influencer Outreach (Medium Effort, Targeted Impact)

Finding and engaging local influencers (bloggers, social media personalities) can earn you valuable backlinks and brand awareness.

  • Find Local Influencers: Use tools like BuzzSumo, Followerwonk, or just search social media for people with strong followings in your area.
  • Offer Free Products/Services: Give them a free sample of your product or service in exchange for a review or mention.
  • Collaborate on Content: Suggest co-creating a blog post, video, or social media campaign.

Tip: Focus on micro-influencers (smaller but highly engaged audiences). They often have a stronger connection with their followers and are more open to partnerships.

5. HARO (Help a Reporter Out), Medium Effort, Significant Potential

HARO connects journalists with experts. Sign up for relevant queries and respond to ones that match your expertise. If a journalist uses your input, you'll earn a high-quality backlink from a reputable news source. Since 93.8% of link builders now prioritize link quality over quantity (SEO.AI, 2025), one HARO win can outweigh dozens of low-grade directory links.

Measuring Your Success

Tracking your backlinks is key. Use tools like:

  • Google Search Console: Shows which websites link to yours.
  • Ahrefs: A strong (paid) tool for backlink analysis.
  • SEMrush: Another popular (paid) SEO tool.

Don't Be Discouraged

Building local backlinks takes time and effort. Don't get discouraged if you don't see results right away. Consistency and a clear plan are key. With 46% of all Google searches carrying local intent (Marketing LTB, 2025), every locally-relevant link compounds. Focus on building real relationships and providing value to the local community, and the backlinks will follow. Start small, track your progress, and adjust as needed.
