## STAT: 999 SILVER FINE OFTEN REFERRED

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 21s minimum

Narration: Silver is graded based on its fineness, which describes the proportion of pure silver in the alloy. 999 fine silver, often referred to as “four-nines” silver, indicates that the silver content is 99.9 percent. This is the most common purity mark found on silver bars and coins. However, for specialized applications or higher-end pieces, you might encounter 99.99 fine silver, also known as “five-nines” silver.

## STAT: 999, SILVER STERLING INDICATES PURITY

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 22s minimum

Narration: This number confirms that the silver has been tested and certified to meet a specific purity standard. Look for this number on the bar or coin - it’s usually stamped on the surface. The assay office’s location and certification are often printed alongside the number. Purity stamps are simpler indicators of silver content. You’ll frequently see “999,” “99.9,” “99.99,” or “Sterling” (which indicates 92.5 percent purity, as we’ll discuss later).

## STAT: 999 INSPECTION SPOT HIGHER PURITY

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 19s minimum

Narration: Pay close attention to the details on the product. Don’t assume that markings alone guarantee authenticity; always combine visual inspection with other verification methods. While visual inspection alone isn’t foolproof, it’s a quick and easy first step. You can spot standard purity like 999 or higher purity like 99.99. Look for consistent stamping, clear lettering, and no signs of tampering.
