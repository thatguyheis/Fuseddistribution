## OVERLAY: FOUR WAYS TO REDUCE NO-SHOWS

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 20s minimum

Narration: Online booking reduces no-shows when it does four things well: records the appointment accurately, sends timely reminders, makes cancellation or rescheduling easy, and applies a clear deposit or cancellation policy when the business needs one. The booking page alone is not the solution. Start with a reliable online booking system for a small business, then configure the follow-through that protects the schedule.

---

## OVERLAY: CREATE ONE ACCURATE RECORD

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 22s minimum

Narration: Phone and message-based scheduling creates opportunities for mistakes. A customer may hear the wrong time, staff may write the wrong date, or two people may offer the same slot. An online system writes the selected service, time, location, and contact details into one record and sends the customer a copy. It gives the customer something searchable in email and, when accepted, places the event on a personal calendar.

---

## OVERLAY: SEND TIMELY REMINDERS

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 22s minimum

Narration: A complete confirmation turns the selection into a specific commitment and gives the customer a simple way to correct an error early. People forget even when they intended to attend. Automated reminders remove the need for staff to remember every follow-up. The strongest schedule depends on the service, but a common starting pattern is one reminder 48 hours before the appointment and another short reminder on the day.

---

## OVERLAY: MAKE RESCHEDULING EASY

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 20s minimum

Narration: Treat delivery method as a testable choice. Use the channel your customers reliably read and offer an alternative for people who do not text. Some customers miss appointments because canceling feels harder than disappearing. Put a reschedule and cancellation link in the confirmation and reminders. State the deadline plainly and show what happens after it. This does not mean accepting unlimited last-minute changes.

---

## OVERLAY: MATCH THE POLICY TO THE COST

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 22s minimum

Narration: It also offsets part of the loss when a customer ignores the policy. Requiring payment for a short introductory call may reduce legitimate bookings more than it reduces no-shows. A multi-hour service, custom order, or reserved team may justify a stronger commitment. Disclose the amount, refund rule, reschedule window, and any exception process before payment. The policy should be visible on the booking page and repeated in the confirmation.

---

## OVERLAY: SHORTEN THE BOOKING HORIZON

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 22s minimum

Narration: If appointments booked 45 days ahead miss more often than those booked within 14 days, shorten the public window or add an extra confirmation step for distant bookings. A useful reminder is short and specific. Include the business name, appointment time, service, location, and one clear action. For example: You are booked with Northside Repair on Tuesday at 2 PM. Reply C to confirm or use this link to reschedule.

---

## OVERLAY: MEASURE THE FULL FUNNEL

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 22s minimum

Narration: Start with four numbers: appointments booked, advance cancellations, late cancellations, and no-shows. Divide no-shows by appointments that remained scheduled at the appointment time. Track by service, staff member, lead time, booking source, and reminder method when volume allows. Do not judge the system from one week with ten appointments. Compare a reasonable baseline with several weeks after implementation. An aggressive deposit policy may reduce no-shows while also reducing total bookings.

---

## OVERLAY: TEST THE COMPLETE CUSTOMER FLOW

**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 23s minimum

Narration: Book on a phone, confirm the calendar entry, receive each reminder, reschedule, cancel, and process a test payment or refund. Ask a staff member to view the booking from the operational side. Launch the link on your website and Google Business Profile when eligible. Review the first ten appointments manually. Fix confusing language before expanding to more services or staff calendars.
