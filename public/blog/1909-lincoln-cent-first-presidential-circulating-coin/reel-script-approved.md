# Reel Script: Why Abraham Lincoln Appeared on the Cent in 1909
format: long-form
content-kind: educate

---
## HOOK
Narration: For more than a century, U.S. circulating coins avoided portraits of real people. Then Abraham Lincoln appeared on the cent in 1909. Why did this everyday denomination become a major break with tradition?

---
## OVERLAY: A PRESIDENT ON A CIRCULATING CENT
Narration: The new cent marked the hundredth anniversary of Lincoln’s birth. The Mint records its public release on August 2, 1909. It says more than 22 million had already been struck at Philadelphia by July, before public release. The Mint’s record places that production before the cent reached the public, showing the scale of preparation for the release. Victor David Brenner designed Lincoln’s profile, which stayed on the obverse as the reverse changed.

---
## OVERLAY: LINCOLN’S CENTENNIAL IN 1909
Narration: The Mint identifies the Lincoln cent as the first circulating U.S. coin to feature a real person and a president. Its portrait put a named historical figure into everyday use.

---
## OVERLAY: THE MINT’S RECORD HAS A GAP
Narration: The decision came amid a wider conversation about coin design. A plaque by Brenner had caught President Theodore Roosevelt’s attention, but the Mint says the precise process for putting Lincoln on the cent is not fully documented.

---
## OVERLAY: THE REVERSE CHANGES OVER TIME
Narration: The reverse gives the cent its own timeline. Wheat ears framed ONE CENT from 1909 through 1958. The Lincoln Memorial followed from 1959 to 2008. Four reverses marked Lincoln’s bicentennial in 2009, and the Mint introduced the Union Shield reverse in 2010.

---
## OVERLAY: A PORTRAIT STAYS AS REVERSES CHANGE
Narration: The 1909 cent made a president part of ordinary circulation. Its changing reverses let later generations reinterpret what the coin could commemorate.
