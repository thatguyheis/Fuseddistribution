Victor David Brenner designed the portrait that appeared on the 1909 centennial issue. The Mint’s record indicates that the precise decision process for selecting his design remains not fully documented. This suggests a degree of uncertainty surrounding the selection of Brenner’s portrait among multiple possibilities. The Mint’s documentation confirms that a plaque bearing Brenner’s name had caught the attention of President Theodore Roosevelt. This observation is recorded as a factor in the Mint’s consideration of the portrait. 

The Mint’s account emphasizes that the decision to feature Lincoln on the cent was not the result of a meticulously planned, formally documented process. Instead, it appears the Mint’s record reflects a more organic development, influenced by factors such as public attention and the visual impact of Brenner’s design. 

The Mint’s documentation provides a crucial distinction: while the 1909 cent was the first circulating U.S. coin to depict a real person and president  -  Lincoln  -  the specific steps leading to that decision are not fully captured in their records. This lack of a detailed, formal process highlights the somewhat spontaneous nature of the event.
