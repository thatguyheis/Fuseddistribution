## claims
- LC1: The 1909 cent commemorated Lincoln’s centennial, was released August 2, and was the first circulating U.S. coin with a real person and president. Source: https://www.usmint.gov/learn/history/timeline.html
- LC2: Victor David Brenner designed the portrait; the Mint’s record describes uncertainty about the precise decision process. Source: https://www.usmint.gov/learn/history/historical-documents/history-of-presidents-on-our-coins
- LC3: The Wheat Ears reverse ran from 1909 through 1958, followed by the Lincoln Memorial from 1959 through 2008 and four 2009 bicentennial reverses. Source: https://www.usmint.gov/content/usmint/us/en/learn/coins-and-medals/circulating-coins/penny.html
- LC4: The Mint records the Lincoln cent's public release as August 2, 1909, and says more than 22 million had been struck in Philadelphia by July. Source: https://www.usmint.gov/learn/history/historical-documents/history-of-presidents-on-our-coins
- LC5: The Mint says the precise decision process for putting Lincoln on the cent is not fully documented; a plaque by Victor David Brenner had caught President Theodore Roosevelt's attention. Source: https://www.usmint.gov/learn/history/historical-documents/history-of-presidents-on-our-coins
- LC6: After the Wheat Ears and Memorial reverses and the four 2009 designs, the Mint introduced the Union Shield reverse in 2010. Source: https://www.usmint.gov/content/usmint/us/en/learn/coins-and-medals/circulating-coins/penny.html
- LC7: The Mint identifies the 1909 cent as the first circulating U.S. coin to feature a real person and a president, marking Lincoln's centennial. Source: https://www.usmint.gov/learn/history/timeline.html
