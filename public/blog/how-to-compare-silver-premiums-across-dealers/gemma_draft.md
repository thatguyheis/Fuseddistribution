Most silver buyers compare total prices. That's the wrong number. Two dealers quoting different prices for different quantities can't be compared until you calculate the cost per troy ounce of silver. Once you do the math, the comparison is clear.

## Understanding Silver Premiums

Silver premiums are not fixed. They change based on the product type, dealer volume, and market conditions. Generic silver rounds typically run 5–8% over spot. American Silver Eagles carry 15–22% because the US Mint charges authorized purchasers a statutory premium on top of melt value. Ten-ounce bars run 3–6%. Fractional coins (1/10 oz, 1/4 oz) run 25–35% because each piece costs nearly as much to mint as a full ounce coin.

You cannot trust a single dealer's quoted price without running the math yourself.

## The Formula for True Cost

Two numbers: the current spot price and the dealer's all-in price per ounce.

Pull spot from Kitco or any financial data site that shows real-time commodities prices. Spot is quoted in troy ounces. One troy ounce equals 31.1 grams.

Then calculate:

**Cost per oz = Total price ÷ Troy ounces purchased**
**Premium per oz = Cost per oz − Spot price**
**Premium % = (Premium per oz ÷ Spot price) × 100**

Worked example: Spot is $32.00. Dealer quotes a 10 oz silver bar at $345 including shipping.
- Cost per oz = $345 ÷ 10 = $34.50
- Premium per oz = $34.50 − $32.00 = $2.50
- Premium % = $2.50 ÷ $32.00 × 100 = 7.8%

Run this for every quote. Now you have one comparable number per dealer.

## Comparing Dealer Quotes

Get three quotes for the same product type and weight on the same day (spot price changes daily). Calculate the cost per ounce for each using the formula above.

Example comparison at spot $32.00 for 10 oz silver:
- Dealer A: $338 all-in → $33.80/oz → 5.6% premium
- Dealer B: $355 all-in → $35.50/oz → 10.9% premium
- Dealer C: $342 all-in → $34.20/oz → 6.9% premium

Dealer A wins. You only know that because you calculated cost per ounce, not because you compared headline prices.

Ask each dealer for an all-in total before you calculate. Several costs don't appear in the headline price: shipping ($0–$20+), credit card surcharges (3–4% at many dealers), insurance, and minimum order fees. Add every cost to the total before dividing.

## Factors That Influence the Price

Premium ranges differ by product type because minting and distribution costs differ. Bars are cheaper per ounce to produce than coins. Government-minted coins like the American Silver Eagle carry a legal tender premium on top of melt value. Smaller weight increments cost more per troy ounce because the fixed cost of minting is divided over less silver.

Volume also changes the math. Most dealers tier premiums:
- 1–10 oz: full retail
- 10–100 oz: 1–2% below retail
- 100+ oz: 2–5% below retail on bars

## Your Simple Plan

Pick one product type. Get three quotes. Include all shipping and fees in each total. Calculate cost per ounce for each. Compare the results.
