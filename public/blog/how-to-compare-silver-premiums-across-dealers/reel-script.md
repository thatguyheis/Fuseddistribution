## Segment 1: INTRO
Visual: Formula card or coins on dark background
Text: COST PER OUNCE IS THE REAL NUMBER
Subtext: Not the total price
Narration: The comparison starts with one formula. Take the all-in price, every cost including shipping. Divide by the number of troy ounces. Subtract spot price. That gives you the premium per ounce. Divide that by spot and multiply by 100. Now you have a percentage you can compare across any dealer.

Subtext: Most cost-efficient physical silver
Narration: Generic silver rounds, privately minted with no legal tender status, typically run 5 to 8 percent over spot. They carry the lowest premium of any standard one-ounce silver product. If your goal is maximum silver per dollar, rounds are where the math works best.

## Segment 4: STAT
Visual: Percent fill graphic, 18 percent filled
Text: 15-22% SILVER EAGLE PREMIUM
Subtext: USA Mint statutory pricing
Narration: American Silver Eagles run 15 to 22 percent over spot. The USA Mint charges authorized purchasers a statutory premium above melt value, and that cost passes down the supply chain. Eagles also trade at a collector premium on top of that. Government-backed, but more expensive per ounce of silver.

## Segment 5: STAT
Visual: Percent fill graphic, 5 percent filled
Text: 3-6% BAR PREMIUM
Subtext: Lowest markup by product type
Narration: Ten-ounce silver bars typically run 3 to 6 percent over spot. One-hundred-ounce bars can run as low as 2 to 4 percent. Lower minting cost per ounce and simpler distribution keep the markup lean. Bars are the efficient choice for buyers who want maximum silver per dollar at larger quantities.

Subtext: Fixed minting cost, less silver
Narration: Fractional silver coins, the one-tenth and one-quarter ounce sizes, carry premiums of 25 to 35 percent over spot. Each piece costs nearly as much to fabricate as a full one-ounce coin. That fixed production cost divides over much less silver. Higher premium, but useful when you need smaller, tradeable pieces.
