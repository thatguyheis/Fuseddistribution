## HOOK

**Visual:** The design rises above the field
**Duration:** 24s minimum

Narration: Relief is the part of a coin design raised above the surrounding surface. It affects how the design catches light, and it also matters when a mint plans how a coin will be struck. Think of relief as the difference in height between design and field, not simply how sharp or shiny the whole coin looks. For a specific coin, the issuing mint’s terminology is more useful than an adjective guessed from a photograph.

---

## OVERLAY: DEFINE RELIEF

**Visual:** Raised and incuse details
**Duration:** 21s minimum

Narration: The U.S. Mint defines relief as the raised part of a coin’s design, opposite to incuse details pressed into the surface. The rim is a separate feature. Naming the part you are looking at makes comparisons more precise. The glossary gives you a consistent starting point, while the product record can explain how the particular issue was designed. Keep the two descriptions separate in your notes.

## OVERLAY: FOLLOW THE STRIKE

**Visual:** Design height meets metal flow
**Duration:** 21s minimum

Narration: During striking, the dies press a prepared blank so metal fills the intended details. The Mint explains that relief and the background basin affect metal flow and the pressure needed. Design, material, dies and press work together. The amount and direction of movement depend on shape and other production choices. This example does not tell us the exact pressure or production settings for another denomination.

---

## OVERLAY: LEARN FROM 1907

**Visual:** One design, several relief versions
**Duration:** 24s minimum

Narration: The Mint’s history of the 1907 Saint-Gaudens double eagle describes several relief versions. The earliest ultra high relief attempts were difficult to strike, and later versions reduced the relief for more practical production. That history provides a concrete comparison: a dramatic design can demand different production choices. It does not mean lower relief is a flaw or that a higher relief issue is automatically rarer. This is a specific example, not a universal rule.

---

## OVERLAY: COMPARE WITH CARE

**Visual:** Match issue and lighting
**Duration:** 30s minimum

Narration: Lighting and camera angle can make height look more dramatic or more subtle. Compare the same issue under similar diffuse light, then check the mint’s description. Wear and image conditions also matter. A photograph alone does not prove a variety, grade or value. Turn the coin carefully if it is safe to handle, and keep the camera fixed. Changes in shadow can reveal contours while hiding surface wear. A repeatable photo pair is more useful than a dramatic single image. Keep observations separate from attribution and preserve uncertainty when the evidence is incomplete.
