## OVERLAY: 1878 / SILVER PURCHASE POLICY

**Visual:** 1878 / SILVER PURCHASE POLICY
Text: 1878 / SILVER PURCHASE POLICY
Narration: The Morgan dollar era began in 1878, after the Bland-Allison Act directed the Treasury to buy between two and four million dollars’ worth of silver each month. The Mint turned that policy into millions of silver dollars. Many were stored in Treasury vaults, then released decades later, so coins that seemed scarce could reappear in quantity.

## OVERLAY: 1893-S IN AN 1894-S BAG

**Visual:** 1893-S IN AN 1894-S BAG
Text: 1893-S IN AN 1894-S BAG
Narration: One remarkable surprise came in the early 1960s. Coin dealer John B. Love opened an original bag of 1894-S dollars and found stray 1893-S Morgans mixed among them. The 1894 bag had been sealed in early January, and some coins inside had been struck from the previous year’s dies. PCGS estimates that this one discovery accounts for roughly a quarter of surviving Mint State 1893-S dollars. Fewer than one hundred uncirculated examples are known today. A bag meant to hold one date had preserved a much rarer neighbor.

## OVERLAY: 400 BAGS BEHIND A WALL

**Visual:** 400 BAGS BEHIND A WALL
Text: 400 BAGS BEHIND A WALL
Narration: Another story unfolded after collector LaVere Redfield died in 1974. Behind a false basement wall, investigators found about four hundred bags, or roughly four hundred thousand silver dollars. PCGS reports that around 85 percent were uncirculated. In 1976, the hoard sold as a single lot for 7.3 million dollars. That was the price of the entire historic lot then, not a value for today’s coins.

## OVERLAY: WHEN TREASURY RELEASES CHANGE RARITY

**Visual:** WHEN TREASURY RELEASES CHANGE RARITY
Text: WHEN TREASURY RELEASES CHANGE RARITY
Narration: And Treasury releases changed the story again. In 1962, bags of 1903-O Morgans reached the public; a coin once treated as a great rarity became available in quantity. Hoards can rewrite what collectors think is scarce, but they do not make every roll valuable.
