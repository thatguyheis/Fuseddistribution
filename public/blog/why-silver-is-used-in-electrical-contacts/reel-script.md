## HOOK

**Visual:** Why silver meets the circuit
**Duration:** 14s minimum

Narration: Silver is used in some electrical contacts because it conducts electricity well and can be combined with other materials for a particular application. But a material property does not tell you whether a part is a suitable replacement.

---

## OVERLAY: THE CONTACT SURFACE

**Visual:** The contact surface
**Duration:** 23s minimum

Narration: Start by separating the contact surface from the whole assembly. A reference to silver contacts does not tell you how much silver a component contains or describe every material in the part. Keep composition, component identity and electrical rating as separate questions. Do not fill missing details with a familiar bullion purity or an assumption based on a photograph.

---

## OVERLAY: MATERIALS SERVE DIFFERENT JOBS

**Visual:** Materials serve different jobs
**Duration:** 23s minimum

Narration: Technical reports describe fine silver, alloys and composites in electrical contact applications. That variety is a reason to read the exact specification, not to choose a substitute from a general article. Older reports explain engineering context. They do not establish which replacement is approved for equipment sold today. Use the equipment documentation and the exact part identifier for that question.

---

## OVERLAY: CHECK THE ACTUAL CLAIM

**Visual:** Check the actual claim
**Duration:** 23s minimum

Narration: Contains silver is a composition claim. Uses silver contacts identifies a feature. Suitable replacement is a much more specific claim about use. One statement does not prove the next. Save the source wording, document date and part identifier, then write down what remains unknown. A short note with clear limits is more useful than a confident guess about compatibility.

---

## OVERLAY: KEEP BULLION RECORDS SEPARATE

**Visual:** Keep bullion records separate
**Duration:** 39s minimum

Narration: Industrial contact information is not a bullion specification or a silver price forecast. If you are documenting coins or bars, use the item identification and purchase records. Keep an industrial application note separate from a collection inventory. A conceptual drawing can explain the contact point, but it cannot identify a real component, establish purity or tell you how to repair equipment. Before repeating a material claim, ask whether the source describes the same component and supports the conclusion you want to draw. Keep the date and limits visible. This explanation is about understanding material choices, not opening equipment or replacing parts. Which material claim would you check against its original source?
