## segments
- type: overlay
 text: DEFINE YOUR ACTUAL SERVICE AREA
 narration: Write down where your team currently works and which services are available there. Check this with the person who schedules jobs. Identify boundaries, travel limits, and exceptions. If coverage depends on the job, explain how a customer can confirm eligibility rather than listing every nearby city as equally served.
- type: overlay
 text: CHOOSE WHETHER A SEPARATE PAGE HELPS
 narration: Compare the proposed page with your main service page. A separate area page is useful when it has meaningful local information for a visitor. If the only difference is a replaced city name, improve the existing coverage explanation before creating another page that repeats the same material.
- type: overlay
 text: ANSWER PRACTICAL SERVICE QUESTIONS
 narration: Explain the services offered, the kinds of projects you accept, and any area specific arrangements you can substantiate. Describe how estimates and scheduling work without inventing guaranteed arrival times. Give visitors enough detail to make a relevant inquiry, and clearly distinguish regular coverage from work considered by request.
- type: overlay
 text: USE GENUINE LOCAL EVIDENCE
 narration: Include a project example, original photograph, or customer question only when it accurately relates to that area and you have permission to publish it. Explain the work itself. Do not invent an office, testimonial, address, or project to make the page appear more local than the business actually is.
- type: overlay
 text: MAKE THE NEXT STEP EASY
 narration: Link the page to the relevant service and provide a clear contact route. Ask for the location and job details your team actually needs to assess the request. Avoid collecting unnecessary private information. Check that a phone visitor can understand the coverage and reach the inquiry form without hunting through unrelated pages.
- type: overlay
 text: REVIEW THE SERVICE AREA PAGE REGULARLY
 narration: Assign someone to review coverage when staffing, travel policies, or available services change. Check the inquiry route and remove obsolete promises through a normal page update. Judge usefulness by whether people can submit suitable requests, rather than by the number of place names repeated across the page.
