# Reel Script: how-to-check-contact-form-labels-on-your-website
format: long-form
segments: 6
target-duration: 120s

---

## HOOK

**Visual:** Check Contact Form Labels on Your Website
**Duration:** 11s minimum

Narration: Review contact form labels by making a list of every field, selection, and checkbox a visitor encounters. Include controls revealed after another choice, not just the fields visible when the page first opens.

---

## OVERLAY: READ EMPTY FIELDS

**Visual:** Read empty fields
**Duration:** 21s minimum

Narration: Look at the empty form and explain what belongs in each field. Note wording that could mean several things, such as a field called details with no context. Distinguish a visible label from example text inside the field. Enter a harmless sample locally to see whether the explanation remains understandable as you type, but do not submit private information while conducting a routine review.

---

## OVERLAY: CHECK ASSOCIATIONS

**Visual:** Check associations
**Duration:** 22s minimum

Narration: Ask the developer to verify that the visible text is associated with the correct form control in the page markup. A label placed beside a field is not enough evidence by itself. Test each label separately, especially in repeated address or contact sections. Keep the wording change and the technical association check in the same issue so one cannot be marked finished while the other remains unresolved.

---

## OVERLAY: REVIEW CHOICES

**Visual:** Review choices
**Duration:** 21s minimum

Narration: Read each checkbox and option as an instruction a customer must act on. Check whether required and optional information is understandable before the visitor attempts to submit. If two fields ask for similar information, explain why both are present or ask whether one can be removed. Do not rewrite a consent statement casually as part of a label cleanup; route that wording to its responsible owner.

---

## OVERLAY: CHECK PHONE LAYOUT

**Visual:** Check phone layout
**Duration:** 22s minimum

Narration: Open the form on a phone and check whether the label still appears beside the intended control after the layout changes. Long instructions should remain readable without covering another input. Look at fields that appear after a selection and confirm they have their own meaningful labels. Record the screen and interaction that expose a problem so the developer can reproduce that state instead of testing only the initial view.

---
