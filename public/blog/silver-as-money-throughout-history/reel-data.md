  text: 30% SILVER TOTAL PRECIOUS METAL
  narration: The stability of silver as a commodity - its consistent density and recognized value - made it a preferred choice for trade and commerce. It’s estimated that silver accounted for approximately 30 percent of the total precious metal used in ancient economies, demonstrating its central importance. As civilizations evolved, silver transitioned into a crucial component of early banking systems.
- type: stat
  text: 14 SILVER CENTURIES CONSTITUTED TOTAL
  narration: Also, the inherent value of silver provided a degree of stability that was lacking in other forms of currency at the time. It’s estimated that during the 14th and 15th centuries, silver constituted over 60 percent of the total value of money circulating in Europe. The role of silver as a store of value persisted throughout the medieval and early modern periods.
- type: overlay
  text: VAST QUANTITIES SILVER MINED
  narration: The vast quantities of silver mined in the Americas were shipped to Europe, bolstering the Spanish economy and contributing to the growth of trade and commerce. Even during periods of war and conflict, silver continued to be held as a reliable store of value. The fact that it could be easily melted down and transported made it a practical choice for preserving wealth.
- type: stat
  text: 500 YEARS SILVER OUTPERFORMED MANY OTHER
  narration: Studies have shown that over the last 500 years, silver has outperformed many other asset classes, including stocks and bonds, on a risk-adjusted basis. Let’s examine some specific figures to illustrate silver’s enduring value. In 1900, one ounce of silver traded at approximately $0.90. Today, that same ounce of silver is valued at around $24. This represents an increase of over 266 percent in value over a century.
- type: stat
  text: 1870 TO 1914 SILVER PLAYED CRITICAL ROLE
  narration: During the Gold Standard era (roughly 1870 to 1914), silver played a critical role in maintaining the stability of the global monetary system. Many countries, including the United States, tied their currencies to silver, with the value of the dollar directly linked to the price of silver. This meant that the supply of currency was theoretically tied to the supply of silver, helping to prevent excessive inflation.
