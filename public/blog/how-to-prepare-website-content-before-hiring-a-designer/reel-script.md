# Reel Script: how-to-prepare-website-content-before-hiring-a-designer
format: long-form
segments: 7
target-duration: 120s

---

## HOOK
**Visual:** Topic intro shot
**Duration:** 10s minimum
Narration: Before you hire a website designer, prepare the content that explains who you serve, what you do, and what visitors should do next.

---

## OVERLAY: DEFINE THE AUDIENCE
**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 24s minimum
Narration: Start with one primary audience. Write down who needs your service, what problem they have, what area you serve, and what a useful first step looks like. Add the questions customers ask before they call, the objections that hold them back, and the words they use when describing the problem. This gives the designer a real brief instead of a vague request to make the site look modern.

---

## OVERLAY: EXPLAIN THE OUTCOME
**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 22s minimum
Narration: For every service, describe the customer problem, the result you can honestly provide, what is included, what is outside the scope, and the best next step. Add the process in plain language so a visitor knows what happens after they contact you. Replace internal terms with language a customer can understand, and keep technical details where they help someone make a decision.

---

## OVERLAY: BRING REAL PROOF
**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 22s minimum
Narration: Gather approved photos, testimonials, case studies, certifications, service details, and contact information. For each testimonial or case study, record who approved it, what work was completed, and which details can be published. Do not invent customers, locations, testimonials, or performance numbers to fill a gap. Honest evidence gives the designer better material to work with.

---

## OVERLAY: MAP THE PAGES
**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 22s minimum
Narration: Draft the page list before design begins. For each page, note the visitor, the question they have, the proof you can show, and the action they should take. Decide which page owns each important answer, then remove repeated copy that belongs somewhere else. That keeps every page from becoming the same sales pitch and gives the designer a cleaner navigation plan.

---

## OVERLAY: MAKE ACTIONS CLEAR
**Visual:** Supporting article visual
**Duration:** 22s minimum
Narration: Choose one main action for each page, such as calling, requesting a quote, booking, or sending a question. Give the designer the exact wording, phone number, hours, response expectations, and confirmation message. Decide what happens when someone submits a form and who owns the follow-up. A call to action only helps when the business can answer it.
