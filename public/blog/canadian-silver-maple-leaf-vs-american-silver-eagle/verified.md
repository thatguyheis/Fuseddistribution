# Silver Maple Leaf vs. American Silver Eagle: Specifications Compared

The Canadian Silver Maple Leaf and the American Silver Eagle are government issued one troy ounce silver bullion coins. Their documented specifications differ in fineness, face value, diameter, and security details. Those facts can help identify the product being offered, but they do not establish a current retail price, resale value, or which coin is right for a particular buyer.

## Compare the published specifications

| Specification | 2026 Silver Maple Leaf | American Silver Eagle |
|---|---|---|
| Fine silver | 99.99% | 99.9% |
| Fine silver weight | 1 troy ounce (31.11 g) | 1 troy ounce (31.103 g) |
| Face value | 5 Canadian dollars | 1 U.S. dollar |
| Diameter | 38 mm | 40.60 mm |
| Notable security details | Radial lines, micro-engraved maple leaf, Bullion DNA feature | Variable reeded edge on redesigned bullion coins |

These specifications come from the issuing mints. Check the year and product type: proof, uncirculated, privy-marked, and bullion products can have different packaging or features. A coin’s face value is its legal denomination, not an estimate of its metal or collector value.

## What the fineness difference means

The Maple Leaf’s published fineness is 99.99 percent, while the American Eagle’s is 99.9 percent. Both contain one troy ounce of fine silver according to their respective mint specifications. Fineness describes the proportion of silver in the alloy; it does not show the coin’s market price or guarantee that a particular coin is genuine.

## Design and authentication features

The 2026 Maple Leaf bullion issue uses radial lines, a micro-engraved maple leaf, and the Royal Canadian Mint’s Bullion DNA authentication system. The Mint says the system supports authentication of Maple Leafs dated 2015 and later. American Silver Eagles redesigned for 2021 and later have a variable reeded edge among their additional security features. These features are useful reference points, but visual inspection alone should not be treated as a complete authentication test.

## Price and resale are separate questions

The U.S. Mint says its bullion coins are sold through authorized purchasers, with prices based on prevailing precious-metal prices plus a premium. The Royal Canadian Mint likewise directs buyers to select bullion dealers and says retail bullion prices vary with international market prices. Retail premiums and buyback offers can change by date, seller, payment method, order size, and availability. Compare written, current quotes for the exact year and condition rather than relying on a fixed price difference.

Neither mint’s product specifications establish that one coin is always cheaper, easier to resell, or a better investment. If you are comparing offers, record the quoted price, shipping, taxes, payment fees, and any buyback terms separately.

## A practical comparison checklist

- Confirm that both items are one troy ounce bullion products, not proof or special collector issues.
- Match the year and condition before comparing dealer quotes.
- Check the issuing mint’s specifications and security features for that issue year.
- Treat the face value, silver content, retail price, and collector value as different measures.
- Use current written quotes and avoid assuming past premiums or resale prices will repeat.
