# Adaptation Blueprint: Speak So People Ship

- Source work: The Art of Public Speaking by Dale Carnegie and J. Berg Esenwein (1915)
- Audience: modern builders
- Publishing target: amazon-kdp
- Positioning: communication, demos, pitches, meetings, webinars, technical talks, and leadership presence for builders and operators

## Core Promise

Help readers turn clear thinking into spoken influence. This adaptation keeps that lesson architecture but relocates it into Remote meetings, product demos, technical talks, sales calls, investor pitches, webinars, podcasts, async video, and team leadership.

## Audience Lens

Write for ambitious but financially uneven builders: founders, freelancers, creators, operators, salespeople, and technical professionals who earn but have not yet translated income into durable assets.

## Modern World

Remote meetings, product demos, technical talks, sales calls, investor pitches, webinars, podcasts, async video, and team leadership

## Tone Rules

- Make communication practical, direct, and useful for people who build.
- Translate stage-era advice into demos, meetings, launches, and technical explanations.
- Keep the voice confident without sounding theatrical or manipulative.

## Source-Aligned Cast Map

Before drafting, create or confirm a cast map that lists each important source character, the adapted character name, and the naming rationale.

- Keep adapted names visibly connected to the source name's sound, rhythm, or recognizable root wherever practical.
- Use parody through fictional composite behavior, modern roles, status signals, and setting details rather than direct public-figure references.
- Avoid real public-figure names, near-identical names, active brand names, founder names, celebrity names, and trademarked company names.
- Make source-name lineage more prominent than any modern celebrity or founder echo.

## Core Lesson Map

1. Clear speech begins with clear thinking.
2. Confidence is built through preparation and repeated exposure.
3. Audience attention must be earned quickly and held with structure.
4. Concrete examples persuade better than abstractions.
5. Voice, pacing, and presence affect trust.
6. A strong talk moves listeners toward a specific action.
7. The speaker's character and sincerity shape the message.

## Chapter Transformation Plan

| # | Chapter | Purpose | Modern frame |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The Fear of the Room | Reframe stage fright as a manageable system problem. | A founder freezes during a product demo and learns how preparation reduces fear. |
| 2 | Think Before You Talk | Show that clear speaking comes from clear structure. | Technical ideas become memorable when organized around one promise, one audience, and one next action. |
| 3 | Make the Abstract Concrete | Teach examples, stories, demos, and analogies. | Developers explain complex systems to customers, investors, and nontechnical teams. |
| 4 | Hold the Room | Translate attention, gesture, voice, and pacing into modern presentation habits. | Zoom calls, webinars, podcast appearances, conference talks, and live sales calls. |
| 5 | Persuasion Without Theater | Show how sincerity, evidence, and usefulness create influence. | A builder pitches adoption without hype. |
| 6 | The Talk That Ships | Tie communication to action. | A good presentation creates decisions, buy-in, sales, funding, recruiting, or better execution. |

## Prohibited Changes

- Do not imply the original authors wrote this adaptation.
- Do not teach manipulation or deceptive persuasion.
- Do not preserve dated gender, platform, or stage assumptions when modernizing examples.

## Editorial Risks

- Drifting into generic motivation instead of practical behavioral change.
- Overusing startup jargon at the expense of clarity.
- Compressing the original lesson sequence so hard that the parable logic disappears.
