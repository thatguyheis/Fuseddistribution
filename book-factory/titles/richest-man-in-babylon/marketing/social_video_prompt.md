# Social Video Prompt

## Project

- Book: **The Richest Dev in the Valley**
- Source manuscript: [manuscript.md](/Users/nick/Documents/New%20project/book-factory/titles/richest-man-in-babylon/manuscript.md)
- Goal: generate a high-energy, social-first movie-trailer-style book preview
- Target platforms: Instagram Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts, X
- Primary output: 35 to 50 second vertical trailer

## Core Angle

Make this feel like a cinematic trailer for a modern money parable set in Silicon Valley. The video should feel urgent, stylish, sharp, and addictive, like a prestige startup drama colliding with a financial awakening story. It should hook ambitious people who earn well but still feel broke.

The tone should be:

- cinematic
- high-status but stressed
- clever and emotionally charged
- aspirational without looking fake
- modern, premium, and algorithm-friendly

Use fictional characters only. Keep the parody energy of Silicon Valley wealth culture, but do not depict real public figures or real companies. The character `Arkad` should feel like a fictional larger-than-life tech billionaire archetype, with no resemblance to any specific real person.

## Nano Banana Master Prompt

```text
Create a 9:16 vertical, ultra-cinematic social video trailer for a modern narrative finance book titled "The Richest Dev in the Valley."

The trailer should feel like a cross between a prestige startup drama, a viral motivational short film, and a luxury finance movie teaser. The visual world is modern Silicon Valley: glass office buildings, Palo Alto coffee shops, glowing laptop screens at night, expensive electric cars, startup warehouses, clean coworking spaces, group chats buzzing, crypto charts, rent bills, credit card statements, and tense late-night ambition.

Center the story on a fictional broke-but-talented app developer named Bansir. He is smart, exhausted, behind on bills, and surrounded by signs of wealth he does not actually possess. He watches richer people glide through the Valley while he is drowning in overdue payments, unfinished code, and quiet financial panic. Then he hears about Arkad, a fictional wealthy tech operator with an almost mythic reputation for understanding money. Arkad should feel calm, understated, precise, and quietly powerful. He is not flashy. He is the man who understands the rules.

The trailer should escalate like a movie preview:
1. Open with money stress, tech ambition, and lifestyle illusion
2. Show Bansir and his friends realizing they are earning but not building wealth
3. Introduce Arkad like a legend
4. Reveal that ancient laws of money still control modern wealth
5. Land on a powerful transformation promise
6. End with the book title as a prestige reveal

Use fast, rhythmic trailer editing with premium lighting, dramatic push-ins, quick cuts, glitchy financial UI accents, moody Bay Area dusk shots, luxury detail shots, and emotionally charged closeups. Every shot should look expensive and intentional.

Visual motifs:
- overdue bills on a kitchen counter
- code on a laptop at 2 a.m.
- a white luxury EV rolling past modest apartments
- coffee shop conversations with startup founders
- a quiet, powerful entrance for Arkad
- crypto charts, stock graphs, banking apps, legal pads with numbers
- Bansir and his friends walking with purpose through Palo Alto or San Jose
- moody conference-room scenes, polished warehouse office, city lights at dusk
- text-message stress, payment notifications, silent panic
- then a shift into control, discipline, compounding, financial clarity

Color arc:
- beginning: cool blue, silver, gray, neon, financial stress, polished emptiness
- middle: darker dramatic contrast, tension, obsession, mystery
- ending: warmer gold tones, confidence, wealth, momentum, earned control

The trailer should feel invigorating for social media. Build strong pattern interrupts in the first 2 seconds. Make it immediately scroll-stopping.

Use bold on-screen text and trailer-style title cards. Suggested text moments:
- "You live in the richest place on earth..."
- "...so why are you still broke?"
- "Some people earn."
- "A few learn the rules."
- "Money follows laws."
- "The Richest Dev in the Valley"

Voiceover tone:
deep, controlled, cinematic, slightly dangerous, high-trust, modern movie-trailer delivery. Avoid cheesy self-help energy. Make it sound expensive, intelligent, and inevitable.

Add subtle social virality cues:
- a first-second hook image
- emotionally legible tension
- prestige startup aesthetic
- clean typography
- one or two quotable lines that could be clipped alone

Important constraints:
- Do not use real company logos
- Do not depict real public figures
- Keep every character fictional
- Avoid cartoonish crypto bro aesthetics
- Avoid generic AI slop visuals
- Avoid cheesy motivational stock-video smiles
- Avoid low-budget meme aesthetics

Make the final 3 seconds feel like a must-watch, must-share book reveal.
```

## Trailer Script Prompt

```text
Generate a 40-second vertical cinematic trailer for "The Richest Dev in the Valley."

Use this pacing and script energy:

0:00 - 0:03
Hook with rapid, high-status stress images:
overdue bills, glowing code editor, rent note, luxury EV outside, crypto chart down, Bansir staring at numbers.
On-screen text:
"You live in the richest place on earth..."

0:03 - 0:08
Show Silicon Valley illusion:
founders in coffee shops, polished offices, startup parties, expensive lifestyles, everybody performing success.
Voiceover:
"In the Valley, everyone looks rich. Few actually are."

0:08 - 0:14
Show Bansir and his crew:
smart, talented, overworked, still broke, sitting on front steps, talking late at night, frustrated but hungry.
Voiceover:
"They could build apps. Raise money. Ship products. But they still couldn't keep a dollar."
On-screen text:
"Earning isn't wealth."

0:14 - 0:21
Introduce Arkad like a legend:
quiet entrance, understated power, clean warehouse office, calm face, everyone watching him.
Voiceover:
"Then they went looking for the one man who understood the rules."
On-screen text:
"Arkad"

0:21 - 0:31
Escalate into the philosophy:
money moving through markets, charts, contracts, notebooks, investing, discipline, direct eye contact, table conversations, financial awakening.
Voiceover:
"Because money doesn't reward talent alone. It rewards discipline. Ownership. Patience. And the people who know its laws."
On-screen text flashes:
"Pay yourself first"
"Control your spending"
"Make your money work"
"Protect your stack"

0:31 - 0:37
Shift into transformation:
Bansir walking with purpose, cleaner workspace, stronger posture, better decisions, calm power, golden light entering the frame.
Voiceover:
"The game was never about making more. It was about keeping what mattered... and putting it to work."

0:37 - 0:40
Prestige end card reveal.
Big title:
"THE RICHEST DEV IN THE VALLEY"
Subtitle:
"Ancient money laws. Modern Silicon Valley."
Final whisper line:
"Read the rules. Or keep learning them the hard way."

Style notes:
- intense cinematic soundtrack with rising pulse
- luxury startup-drama visuals
- fast cuts early, slower confident cuts near the end
- premium typography
- emotionally sharp, social-media addictive, must-share energy
```

## Optional Hook Variants

Use any of these as the opening line:

- `You can work in the richest industry on earth and still die broke.`
- `Silicon Valley creates millionaires every day. So why are you still behind?`
- `Most people in tech learn how to earn. Very few learn how to keep.`
- `He could build products. He just couldn't build wealth.`

## Creative Notes

- Favor realism over fantasy.
- The first five seconds matter most.
- Keep the financial pain emotionally clear before the wealth lesson lands.
- Arkad should feel mysterious, disciplined, and authoritative, not loud.
- The ending should sell a transformation, not just a book.
