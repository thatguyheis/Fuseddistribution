# Chapter 5: Seven Cures For a Lean Wallet

The Valley is legendary for its wealth.

Ask anyone who lives there and they'll tell you the same thing. The rents are insane. The cost of living is brutal. Everyone seems to be making good money and nobody seems to have any. The wealth of the region has a curious tendency to pool in a small number of hands while everyone else spends it as fast as it arrives.

Which is exactly what happened in ancient Babylon, too.

When CEO Sarge, founder of The Valley's largest tech conglomerate, returned from a grueling board meeting, he found his CFO waiting in the glass-walled conference room he used for bad news.

Rhonda had that look.

"The company is thriving," she said, in the tone of someone who is about to say *but.*

"But?" said Sarge.

"But the people aren't. After years of growth, the platform buildout, the server expansion, the AI division, the money has flowed through our workforce like water through a sieve. Most of our employees are living paycheck to paycheck. The gig economy workers are broke. The local economy is weakening. Wealth has concentrated into very few hands."

Sarge looked at the data she slid across the table. "Why does this keep happening?"

"Because a small number of people know how to build wealth," she said. "And the majority have never been taught."

"Then we teach them. Who in this city understands money better than anyone else?"

Rhonda didn't need to think about it.

"Arkad Morrow."

---

CEO Sarge called Arkad in and asked him to teach a cohort of one hundred people, employees, contractors, gig workers, developers, baristas with software side projects, the exact system by which he had built his wealth.

Arkad showed up the following Tuesday to a large conference room in the company's HQ. A hundred people on folding chairs. Nervous energy. Someone had put a bowl of kombucha on ice, which was a very Valley touch.

Arkad sat on a stool at the front.

"I'm not going to give you a motivational speech," he said. "I'm going to give you a system. Seven steps. Each one builds on the last. Try to poke holes in them. I welcome it. These aren't theories. They're the things I actually did."

He looked around the room.

"I started as broke as every person in this room. My first portfolio was embarrassingly lean. I hated how empty it felt. So I found seven cures. Here they are."

---

## THE FIRST CURE
### Pay Yourself First

"How many of you, when your paycheck hits, immediately pay everyone else, rent, subscriptions, food delivery, student loans, credit cards, and then wonder why there's nothing left for savings?"

Every hand went up.

"That's the trap. You've organized your financial life so that you are the last person to get paid. No wonder you're broke.

"The fix is absurdly simple. Before any bill gets paid, before your brain even has time to route that money somewhere else, ten percent goes to you. Not into a checking account you can touch. A separate account. A brokerage. A Bitcoin wallet, hardware or software, something you have to make a deliberate decision to access.

"Think of an egg farmer with ten eggs who sets one aside before selling the rest. By the end of the year, that basket is overflowing. Your money works the same way.

"For every ten dollars you earn, put one away. Automate it. Forget it exists. It will not hurt. You will not miss it. And here's the strange part: you'll start earning more, because something shifts in your psychology when you have a growing stack. Wealth seems to attract people who can hold it.

"This is the First Cure: **automate 10% to yourself before you pay anyone else.**"

---

## THE SECOND CURE
### Budget Like a CFO, Not a Wishful Thinker

"I hear people say: 'I can't save ten percent, my expenses are too high.' I hear it from people making fifty thousand a year. I hear it from people making five hundred thousand. Income levels change. The complaint doesn't.

"Your 'necessary expenses' will always grow to fill your income unless you fight back. That's lifestyle inflation, and it will rob you blind on a sliding scale. No dramatic theft. Just a slow, comfortable bleeding.

"A budget is not a punishment. It's a tool. Write down what you actually spend. Be honest. Then ask: which of these things truly matter to me? Cut the rest. You don't need four streaming subscriptions. That subscription box that's been renewing for eighteen months is not serving you.

"Live on ninety percent of what you earn. That first ten percent is sacred. The other ninety has to cover everything, and ninety percent is more than enough when you're intentional about it.

"This is the Second Cure: **design a budget that protects your savings first and funds a real life second.**"

---

## THE THIRD CURE
### Make Your Money Work for You

"Saving is not enough. Ten percent sitting in a savings account earning nothing is ten percent slowly losing to inflation. You have to put it to work.

"Every dollar you save is a tiny worker who can hire other workers. Those workers hire more. This is compound interest. The most powerful force in personal finance, and the one that almost no one in their twenties takes seriously because it's slow at first and then shockingly fast later.

"Where do you put it? Start safe. Index funds. Bitcoin, dollar-cost averaged, small amounts, consistently, over time. Every cycle, every halving, just keep buying a little. A DeFi protocol with a real track record. Lending platforms with legitimate underwriting. You don't need to find the next 100x gem. You need steady, compounding, reliable returns.

"If you're building AI tools, running automation side hustles, freelancing with AI agents, that income counts too. Every dollar from every stream, ten percent off the top, put to work. The source of the income is irrelevant. The discipline is everything.

"My first investment went sideways. I gave my savings to Azmur the Crypto Bro to put into an NFT scheme. He was a brickmaker trying to sell me jewels. I lost everything. Learned a lot.

"My second investment went to Agar, who ran a legitimate DeFi lending protocol with real revenue. He paid back promptly, every time. I reinvested the returns. That compounding machine is still running ten years later.

"This is the Third Cure: **put every saved dollar to work in legitimate, compounding investments.**"

---

## THE FOURTH CURE
### Guard Your Stack from Loss

"In this industry, someone is always pitching you something. The DM slides in. The Discord invite arrives. The guy at the party tells you about the token that's about to 1000x. The friend of a friend has a 'can't miss' opportunity.

"First law of not losing money: if it sounds too good to be true, it is. Every rug pull in history was sold to someone as a great opportunity. Every single one. The pitch was always compelling. The urgency was always manufactured. The exit was always pre-planned.

"Before you invest anything, ask: is my principal safe? Can I get it back if I need to? Is this run by people with a real track record? Would someone who has actually made money in this space approve of this?

"Protect the principal. A small, boring, safe return beats a 'huge potential' investment that goes to zero. The penalty for too much risk is often total loss. Slow money that stays beats fast money that vanishes.

"This is the Fourth Cure: **never invest in what you don't understand, and always protect the principal.**"

---

## THE FIFTH CURE
### Build Equity in Something Real

"Renting forever means paying someone else's mortgage forever. Every month, your money builds their net worth, not yours.

"I know. The Valley is expensive. Every market is expensive. I'm not telling you to do something impossible. I'm telling you to make it a goal. Understand what you need for a down payment. Work toward it systematically. Even if it takes five years. Even if the first place you buy isn't where you want to live forever.

"Owning your home is not just about pride or stability, though it provides both. It's about building an asset that appreciates while you live in it, that builds equity every single month, and that provides a foundation of security no landlord can take away with a rent increase letter.

"Steady, consistent, building something instead of spending on nothing.

"This is the Fifth Cure: **work toward owning your home and building real, tangible equity.**"

---

## THE SIXTH CURE
### Invest in Your Future Self

"Thirty-year-old you thinks sixty-year-old you is abstract. A theory. Someone else's problem.

"Sixty-year-old you will have opinions about this.

"The best gift you can give your future self is starting to fund their life *now*, while time is on your side. A retirement account, maxed out. An investment you add to consistently and never touch. Insurance for your family if something happens to you. A plan.

"Here's the math: a small, consistent investment started at twenty-five is worth three to four times more at sixty-five than the same investment started at thirty-five. Time is the variable. You can't buy it back.

"Start now. Even small. The machine compounds regardless of the size of the input, just proportionally. Ten dollars a month, invested early, beats a hundred dollars a month started too late.

"This is the Sixth Cure: **fund your future self now. Retirement accounts, consistent investing, protection for your family.**"

---

## THE SEVENTH CURE
### Invest in Your Ability to Earn

"The final cure is the one people least expect in a money book: invest in yourself.

"Your income is your greatest asset. The more valuable your skills, the more you earn. The more you earn, the more you can save and invest. It compounds just like money does.

"Read. Take courses. Find mentors. Learn the skill that makes you twice as valuable. If you're a developer, learn to sell. If you're a salesperson, learn the product deeply. If you're a creative, learn finance. Every new capability you build is a lever on your income.

"Right now, in 2025 and 2026, the highest-leverage thing many people can do is learn how to deploy AI. Not just use it. Deploy it. Build an agent that automates something a business currently pays a human to do. Sell that automation. The income ceiling for someone who can build and sell AI tools is genuinely open-ended, and the tools have never been more accessible. If you are reading this as a developer or a side-hustle builder, that skill is a cure unto itself.

"Pay your debts promptly, because debt drags on your future earning power. Take care of your people, your team, your clients, your family, because reputation compounds too. Set goals specific enough to be actionable. 'I want to be rich someday' is not a goal. 'I want to earn X by Y date by doing Z' is a goal.

"This is the Seventh Cure: **continuously invest in your skills, your reputation, and your earning capacity.**"

---

Arkad stepped back from the front of the room.

"Seven cures. One system. It works. Not because it's clever, but because it's consistent. Most people try it for three months and abandon it. The people who stick with it for three years look back and can't believe the difference.

"The Valley has wealth enough for all of us. There is no shortage of opportunity. Opportunity without a system, though, is just a nice story you tell at parties.

"Go build the system."
