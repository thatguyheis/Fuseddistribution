### Based on the timeless financial wisdom of George S. Clason

## Foreword

Money is plentiful for those who understand its simple rules. It is scarce for those who don't. It has always been this way, and no amount of technological progress has changed it.

This book is about those rules. Not in a boring, spreadsheet-lecture kind of way. In the way a wise, slightly smug billionaire friend explains things over overpriced sushi: with stories, with humor, and with the kind of blunt truth your financial advisor is too polite to say out loud.

The principles in these pages were not invented in Silicon Valley. They weren't born in a Stanford dorm room or a Y Combinator pitch session. They are older than any IPO. Men and women practiced them thousands of years ago in a city called Babylon, arguably the original tech hub of the ancient world. No oil. No forests. No natural advantage whatsoever. Just human ingenuity, ambition, and an uncanny ability to make money work.

The names and places have changed. The principles have not.

Money still flows toward those who understand its simple laws. It still vanishes from those who don't. It doesn't care what generation you're from, what app you use, or how many followers you have.

**Money is plentiful for those who understand the simple rules of its acquisition.**

The rules:

1. Pay yourself first
2. Control your spending
3. Make your money work for you
4. Protect your stack from bad bets
5. Own your home (or work toward it)
6. Secure your future income
7. Invest in your ability to earn

Simple? Yes. Easy? Apparently not, or we wouldn't still need this book a hundred years later.

Read on. Try it. The results may surprise you.

## About the Author (Original)

George Samuel Clason was born in Louisiana, Missouri, in 1874. He founded the Clason Map Company, publisher of the first road atlas of the United States and Canada. In 1926, he began writing a series of pamphlets on personal finance, using parables set in ancient Babylon to bring money wisdom to life. These were so good that banks and insurance companies distributed them by the millions. The most famous became *The Richest Man in Babylon*.

Nearly a century later, his parables are still being passed around, photocopied, gifted, underlined, and dog-eared. Because the truth doesn't expire.

This version updates the setting. The wisdom stays exactly the same.
