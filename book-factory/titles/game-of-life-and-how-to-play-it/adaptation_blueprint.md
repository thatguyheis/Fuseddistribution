# Adaptation Blueprint: The Game of Life in Debug Mode

- Source work: The Game of Life and How to Play It by Florence Scovel Shinn (1925)
- Audience: modern builders
- Publishing target: amazon-kdp
- Positioning: mindset, language discipline, self-command, and practical optimism for technical and creative workers

## Core Promise

Show readers how language, expectation, and inner posture influence choices and outcomes. This adaptation keeps that lesson architecture but relocates it into Personal operating systems, self-talk, startup stress, creative risk, decision loops, confidence, intuition, and emotional regulation.

## Audience Lens

Write for ambitious but financially uneven builders: founders, freelancers, creators, operators, salespeople, and technical professionals who earn but have not yet translated income into durable assets.

## Modern World

Personal operating systems, self-talk, startup stress, creative risk, decision loops, confidence, intuition, and emotional regulation

## Tone Rules

- Preserve the source's confidence while grounding claims in behavior and decision quality.
- Translate metaphysical language into practical self-command without mocking the source.
- Avoid promising magical outcomes.

## Source-Aligned Cast Map

Before drafting, create or confirm a cast map that lists each important source character, the adapted character name, and the naming rationale.

- Keep adapted names visibly connected to the source name's sound, rhythm, or recognizable root wherever practical.
- Use parody through fictional composite behavior, modern roles, status signals, and setting details rather than direct public-figure references.
- Avoid real public-figure names, near-identical names, active brand names, founder names, celebrity names, and trademarked company names.
- Make source-name lineage more prominent than any modern celebrity or founder echo.

## Core Lesson Map

1. Words shape attention and behavior.
2. Expectation changes what people notice and attempt.
3. Fearful speech can reinforce fearful action.
4. A disciplined inner life improves outer decisions.
5. Intuition deserves attention but must be paired with judgment.
6. Generosity and goodwill reduce self-sabotage.
7. The game is played through repeated choices, not wishes alone.

## Chapter Transformation Plan

| # | Chapter | Purpose | Modern frame |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The Game Loop | Translate the source's game metaphor into repeated choices, feedback, and behavior. | A technical worker sees life as a loop of inputs, beliefs, words, actions, and results. |
| 2 | Your Words Are Commands | Show how language directs attention and behavior. | Self-talk, team language, launch anxiety, and identity statements. |
| 3 | Expectation Is a Filter | Turn expectation into a practical tool for seeing options and acting sooner. | Opportunity recognition, confidence, and founder psychology. |
| 4 | Debugging Fear | Teach readers to inspect fear without obeying it. | Decision logs, nervous system pressure, and high-stakes work. |
| 5 | The Inner Prompt | Use intuition as a signal that must be tested by judgment. | Gut checks, pattern recognition, and responsible risk. |
| 6 | Play Clean | Connect goodwill, clarity, and generosity to better outcomes. | Reputation, relationships, and avoiding self-sabotage in small markets. |

## Prohibited Changes

- Do not make unsupported medical, financial, or supernatural claims.
- Do not imply Florence Scovel Shinn wrote the adaptation.
- Do not reduce the work to generic affirmation content.

## Editorial Risks

- Drifting into generic motivation instead of practical behavioral change.
- Overusing startup jargon at the expense of clarity.
- Compressing the original lesson sequence so hard that the parable logic disappears.
