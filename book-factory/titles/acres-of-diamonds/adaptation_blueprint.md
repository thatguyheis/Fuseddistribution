# Adaptation Blueprint: Hidden Gold in Plain Sight

- Source work: *Acres of Diamonds* by Russell H. Conwell (1890)
- Audience: modern builders
- Publishing target: amazon-kdp
- Positioning: a narrative business parable about finding overlooked opportunity in ordinary markets

## Core Promise

The original work argues that people often travel far in search of opportunity while ignoring the value hidden in the ground beneath their feet. This adaptation keeps that lesson intact while relocating it to modern business: overlooked local markets, boring service businesses, operational gaps, creator ecosystems, and the hidden value embedded in the people and problems already nearby.

The manuscript should read like a business parable rather than a sermon. One central operator, Luke Mercer, becomes the through-line. He leaves a practical business to chase prestige, loses money and clarity, then slowly rediscovers the wealth hidden in ordinary work through conversations with a disciplined operator named Nora Vale and the evidence right in front of him.

## Audience Lens

Write for builders who are ambitious enough to chase large outcomes but prone to overlooking unglamorous opportunities. They are often distracted by startup mythology, internet hype, and the fantasy that the best market must be somewhere else.

## Modern World

Service businesses, local markets, creator ecosystems, operational bottlenecks, infrastructure, logistics, home services, under-loved trades, and the practical economics of real demand.

## Tone Rules

- Keep the prose practical, energetic, and human.
- Celebrate real work over fantasy and real demand over trend worship.
- Use contemporary business detail without turning the book into jargon soup.
- Let the book sound like a seasoned operator telling a story with authority.

## Core Lesson Map

1. Opportunity is often hidden in familiar ground.
2. Most people overlook value because familiarity makes them blind.
3. Disciplined observation turns ordinary markets into wealth.
4. The person willing to solve unglamorous problems sees value before the crowd does.
5. Most people chase glamour because they do not understand boring demand.

## Narrative Spine

- Luke Mercer once ran a practical field-services company near Sacramento.
- Embarrassed by how ordinary the work looked, he sold part of it and moved to Austin to chase startup prestige.
- After burning money and confidence, he returns home and begins seeing the market he abandoned with new eyes.
- Nora Vale, a blunt operator who built a regional facilities-services company by mastering boring work, becomes the book's clearest voice on opportunity.
- Each chapter should advance both the argument and Luke's ability to see what was always there.

## Chapter Transformation Plan

| # | Chapter | Purpose | Modern frame |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Foreword | Frame the book around opportunity blindness in modern work. | Builders chasing distant upside while ignoring nearby demand |
| 1 | The Man Who Left Home | Show the cost of abandoning familiar ground for glamorous fantasy. | Luke leaves a practical business for startup mythology and loses useful context |
| 2 | The Gold Under the Asphalt | Reveal that ordinary local problems often hide extraordinary value. | Luke sees neglected service markets with fresh eyes after returning home |
| 3 | What People Ignore, Markets Reward | Explain why overlooked work can produce high trust and strong margins. | Nora Vale teaches that recurring pain, not trendiness, is what pays |
| 4 | The Trade in Front of You | Argue that proximity plus skill beats novelty plus distance. | Luke realizes the best market is often adjacent to work he already understands |
| 5 | The People Already Near You | Show the reader how opportunity is embedded in current relationships. | Existing clients, vendors, and neighbors reveal hidden demand networks |
| 6 | Build Where You Stand | Move from insight into a repeatable way of seeing and acting. | Luke stops admiring the lesson and starts building on it |
| 7 | The Map Beneath Your Feet | Turn the story into a practical method readers can use. | A reader-facing operating system for mapping pain, trust, proximity, and demand |

## Prohibited Changes

- Do not promise effortless wealth.
- Do not imply the original author wrote this adaptation.
- Do not glamorize shallow trend-chasing over real service and real demand.
- Do not use real public figures, company names, or scenes that read like nonfiction biography.

## Editorial Risks

- Drifting into generic motivational business language.
- Losing the force of the original parable by making everything purely tactical.
- Overstating local opportunity in a way that sounds naive instead of disciplined.
