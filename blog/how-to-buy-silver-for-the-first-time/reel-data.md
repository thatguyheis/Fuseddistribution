## stats
- text: 18-28% EAGLE PREMIUM
  explanation: American Silver Eagles carry 18-28% above spot because of US Mint recognition, not more silver
  graphic_type: percent_fill

  narration: American Silver Eagles carry an 18 to 28 percent premium over the silver spot price. That premium pays for the US Mint's reputation and legal tender status, not extra silver. Every Eagle contains exactly 1 troy oz of .999 fine silver, same as a generic round that sells for 5 to 10 percent over spot. Most first-time buyers pay that premium without knowing there is a cheaper alternative.

- text: 5-10% ROUND PREMIUM
  explanation: Generic silver rounds from private refineries carry 5-10% premium, giving the same .999 silver as Eagles
  graphic_type: percent_fill
  graphic:
    value: 7
    label: Typical Round Premium
    remainder_label: Spot Price
  narration: Generic 1 oz silver rounds carry a 5 to 10 percent premium over spot. They contain the same .999 fine silver as government coins. The difference is name recognition. A round minted by Sunshine Minting or SilverTowne does not carry the government's stamp, but it trades on the secondary market near spot and buys the same silver exposure for meaningfully less money.

- text: 31.1 GRAMS PER TROY OZ
  explanation: One troy oz equals 31.1 grams, not a standard 28.35g ounce. Use this to verify weight on delivery
  graphic_type: none
  narration: One troy oz of silver weighs 31.1 grams. Not 28 grams. Not 28.35. A troy oz is 10 percent heavier than the standard avoirdupois oz used for everyday weight. Check every delivery on a precision scale. If your 1 oz round weighs 28 grams, you received a fake. Every legitimate silver product matches the 31.1 gram standard within a tolerance of 0.1 grams.

- text: 3-4% CREDIT CARD FEE
  explanation: Most silver dealers add 3-4% for credit card payments. Paying by check or ACH eliminates this fee
  graphic_type: percent_fill
  graphic:
    value: 3.5
    label: Card Surcharge
    remainder_label: Check/ACH Price
  narration: Most silver dealers charge a 3 to 4 percent surcharge for credit card payments. At a $32 spot price with a 7 percent round premium, paying by card adds another $1.10 to your per-oz cost. Paying by check or ACH eliminates that fee entirely. Most dealers post two prices per listing: a credit card price and a check price. The check price is the real market rate. Use it for every order once you trust the dealer.

- text: $9.99 FLAT SHIPPING
  explanation: Shipping costs drop from $9.99/oz on a single-oz order to under $2/oz when buying 5+ oz at once
  graphic_type: none
  narration: Shipping is the biggest variable cost in a small silver order. At 1 oz with $9.99 flat-rate shipping, you are paying an effective $9.99 per oz in freight. Buy 5 oz in one order and that same $9.99 shipping drops to $2 per oz. Buy enough to hit a free shipping threshold, which most major dealers set between $99 and $199, and shipping drops to zero. Buy in batches, not one at a time.

- text: $1,500 INSURANCE CAP
  explanation: Standard homeowner insurance typically caps precious metals theft at $1,500 before a scheduled floater is needed
  graphic_type: none
  narration: Standard homeowner and renter policies cap precious metals theft coverage at $1,500. A 10 oz stack at $32 spot is worth $320 in raw silver value, but premiums bring your actual cost to over $350. By the time you own 30 to 50 oz, you have almost certainly outgrown your standard coverage. A scheduled floater costs under $2 per $100 of value per year and closes the gap completely.

- text: 0.715 OZ PER $1 FACE VALUE
  explanation: Every $1 face value of pre-1965 US junk silver coins contains exactly 0.715 troy oz of silver
  graphic_type: none
  narration: Pre-1965 US dimes, quarters, and half dollars are 90 percent silver. Every dollar of face value in those coins contains 0.715 troy oz of actual silver. That is a fixed ratio that makes junk silver easy to price relative to spot. A roll of 20 junk quarters at $5 face value contains 3.575 troy oz of silver. Premiums typically run 3 to 8 percent over spot, making junk silver one of the lowest-cost ways to buy real silver.
