## stats
- text: 211M OZ ANNUAL DEFICIT
  narration: Silver's global market ran a 211 million ounce deficit in 2023. That means industrial users, investors, and jewelers together consumed 211 million more ounces than miners and recyclers produced. According to the Silver Institute's 2024 World Silver Survey, it was the third consecutive year demand exceeded supply.

- text: 3RD STRAIGHT YEAR IN DEFICIT
  narration: The deficits in 2021 and 2022 were smaller, but the trend has been consistent every single year. A one-year imbalance can be a blip. Three consecutive years in deficit is a structural signal about the relationship between supply and demand in this market.

- text: 998M OZ SUPPLY VS 1.2B DEMAND
  narration: In 2023, global silver mine production reached about 820 million ounces. Recycling added another 178 million. Total supply came to 998 million ounces. Total demand across all categories hit 1,209 million ounces. That 211 million ounce gap had to be covered by drawing down above-ground inventories.

- text: 161M OZ SOLAR DEMAND IN 2023
  narration: Solar panel demand consumed 161 million ounces of silver in 2023. That is nearly double the 88 million ounces consumed in 2020. Silver paste is used in photovoltaic cells, and no viable substitute has reached commercial scale. Each new solar installation locks in ongoing demand that mine supply cannot outpace.

- text: 72% BYPRODUCT MINING
  narration: Roughly 72 percent of mine supply comes as a byproduct of gold, copper, zinc, and lead mining. That means silver output depends on production decisions made for other metals entirely. When copper mines cut output due to weak prices or capital constraints, silver production falls with them, regardless of silver's own price.

- text: 8-12 YEARS TO BUILD NEW MINE
  narration: From discovery to production, a new silver mine typically takes 8 to 12 years to develop. Even with strong silver prices, the production response is slow. You cannot significantly increase supply in a year or two.

- text: COMEX STOCKS DOWN 30%
  narration: COMEX silver warehouse stocks dropped from approximately 400 million ounces in early 2021 to under 280 million ounces by early 2024. That is a decline of more than 30 percent over three years, tracking closely with the deficit period. Each consecutive year of deficit reduces the buffer available for the next disruption.

- text: PHYSICAL PREMIUMS WIDEN IN DEFICITS
  narration: When exchange inventories tighten and industrial users compete with investors for physical metal, the premium you pay above spot for coins, rounds, or bars tends to increase. Buyers who own physical metal acquired at lower premiums are insulated from that effect.

- text: INDUSTRIAL DEMAND IS PRICE-INELASTIC
  narration: Industrial users buy silver because their processes require it, not because of price. Solar manufacturers and EV makers do not cut back when silver gets expensive. That base of inelastic demand gives the physical market a floor that pure financial demand cannot provide.
