## stats
- text: 5 MIN TO VERIFY ANY DEALER
  explanation: A 5-minute checklist of address, phone, SSL, and reviews catches nearly all fraudulent silver dealers
  graphic_type: none
  narration: Verifying any online silver dealer takes about 5 minutes. You need a real street address, a working phone number, https on the checkout page, and a review history spanning multiple years. APMEX, JM Bullion, and SD Bullion pass all four. Most fraudulent listings fail the first two.

- text: 3-4% CREDIT CARD SURCHARGE
  explanation: Card payments cost 3-4% more but provide buyer protection. First orders with new dealers should use a card
  graphic_type: percent_fill
  graphic:
    value: 3.5
    label: Card Surcharge
    remainder_label: Check Price
  narration: Most silver dealers add 3 to 4 percent for credit card payments. That fee buys you chargeback rights if the order goes wrong. For your first order with any dealer, pay by card regardless. Once you know the dealer ships legitimate silver, switch to check or ACH and keep that 3 to 4 percent as metal.

- text: $0 RECOURSE WITH WIRE TRANSFER
  explanation: Wire transfers offer no buyer protection. No recourse if you send a wire and don't receive your order
  graphic_type: none
  narration: Wire transfers offer zero buyer protection. Once the money leaves your account, it is gone. Most established dealers accept wire transfers for large orders because they have a reputation to protect. Fraudulent sellers often demand wire transfer or crypto as the only payment option. That alone is a reason to walk away.

- text: 31.1 GRAMS TO VERIFY ON ARRIVAL
  explanation: Every 1 troy oz piece must weigh 31.1 grams. A scale verification takes 10 seconds and catches every weight-based fake
  graphic_type: none
  narration: When your silver arrives, weigh it immediately. One troy oz is 31.1 grams. If your 1 oz round weighs 28 grams, you received a silver-plated piece. A digital scale accurate to 0.1 grams costs under $10. Check every order from every dealer, including the established ones, until you know what a good product feels like.

- text: 5% BELOW MARKET = RED FLAG
  explanation: Silver premiums are competitive within fractions of a percent. A listing more than 5% below major dealer prices is a warning signal
  graphic_type: none
  narration: APMEX and JM Bullion compete on fractions of a percent. A listing showing silver 10 or 15 percent below their prices is not a deal. It is either counterfeit, misrepresented purity, or a non-delivery setup. If you see a price that looks too good, it is. Check the same product on two established dealer sites as a baseline before buying anywhere else.

- text: 7 RED FLAGS TO KNOW
  explanation: Seven warning signs identify fraudulent silver sellers: no address, no phone, wire only, no buy-back, new domain, no SSL, fake reviews
  graphic_type: none
  narration: Seven red flags cover nearly every online silver scam. No physical address. No working phone. Wire transfer or crypto only. No published buy-back program. Domain registered within the last 2 years. No SSL on checkout. And review profiles that all appeared in the last 30 days. Any single one of these is a reason to find a different dealer.

- text: ICTA MEMBERSHIP
  explanation: ICTA membership means a dealer belongs to the precious metals trade body that sets conduct standards
  graphic_type: none
  narration: The Industry Council for Tangible Assets is the trade body for USA precious metals dealers. ICTA membership isn't legally required, but most established dealers have it. You can verify membership on their website in under a minute. It signals the dealer operates under professional conduct standards, not just as an anonymous website.

Anonymous social media and private listings carry the highest risk.
