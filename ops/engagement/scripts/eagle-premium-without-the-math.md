## HOOK

**Visual:** Price the preference
**Duration:** 21s minimum

Narration: My take: stop treating a familiar coin name as a reason to skip the arithmetic. If you are choosing a Silver Eagle because you like that particular coin, say so. If your goal is a lower delivered cost for a comparable amount of silver, compare the complete quotes before deciding that a premium is justified.

---

## OVERLAY: USE COMPLETE QUOTES

**Visual:** Use complete quotes
**Duration:** 20s minimum

Narration: Write down the product, quantity, quoted price, delivery, payment charges and any applicable tax. Keep the date and seller with the quote. Do not compare one seller's checkout total with another seller's headline price. You have changed the basis of comparison before you have even started discussing what the extra money buys.

---

## OVERLAY: LABEL THE EXAMPLE

**Visual:** Label the example
**Duration:** 22s minimum

Narration: Here is a hypothetical arithmetic example, not a current market quote. Imagine two options that each deliver the same stated silver amount. One totals fifty dollars and the other totals forty-five. The difference is five dollars. That calculation describes the example; it does not establish that an Eagle, a Maple Leaf or another product actually has either price today.

---

## OVERLAY: NAME WHAT MATTERS

**Visual:** Name what matters
**Duration:** 20s minimum

Narration: There can be reasons to prefer a particular item that a simple price comparison does not capture. Design, collecting interest, seller confidence and the terms of the transaction may matter to you. Put those preferences beside the numbers. Do not invent a future resale advantage to make the more expensive option look mathematically inevitable.

---

## OVERLAY: SEPARATE PREFERENCE FROM PREDICTION

**Visual:** Separate preference from prediction
**Duration:** 37s minimum

Narration: My position is that an unexplained premium is a weak reason to spend more. A clearly understood personal preference is a different decision. This is not a claim that Eagles are bad purchases or that the lowest quote is always the best choice. Check the actual item, seller and conditions, and do not treat a hypothetical comparison as a buying instruction. Save both complete quotes before you choose. If quantities, delivery conditions or payment methods differ, note the difference instead of hiding it in a single headline comparison. Your record should explain the decision you actually made, including any preference that mattered beyond the total.
