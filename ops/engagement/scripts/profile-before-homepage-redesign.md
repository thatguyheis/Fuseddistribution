## HOOK

**Visual:** Fix the wrong answer
**Duration:** 21s minimum

Narration: My take: if your business profile gives people the wrong hours or contact details, fix that before spending the afternoon polishing a homepage animation. That is an order of work for this specific situation. It is not a claim that a profile always matters more than a website, or that one channel replaces the other.

---

## OVERLAY: START WITH OBSERVABLE FACTS

**Visual:** Start with observable facts
**Duration:** 20s minimum

Narration: Open the information your customer actually sees and compare it with the business's confirmed details. Check the name, hours, contact route and service information that the profile supports. Record the discrepancy and have the responsible owner approve the correction. A suggested edit is not proof that the public listing now shows the corrected information.

---

## OVERLAY: KNOW THE SOURCE LIMITS

**Visual:** Know the source limits
**Duration:** 21s minimum

Narration: Google says complete, accurate information helps it understand a business and match it to relevant searches. Its local results depend mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. That guidance does not supply a ranking promise for your business. Do not turn a profile cleanup into a guaranteed number of leads or a claim that proximity stops mattering.

---

## OVERLAY: KEEP THE WEBSITE USEFUL

**Visual:** Keep the website useful
**Duration:** 19s minimum

Narration: The website still has a job. It can explain services, show work, answer detailed questions and provide a clear next step. If that route is broken, it also deserves attention. Rank the problems by their demonstrated effect on a customer task instead of choosing a winner in an abstract profile-versus-website argument.

---

## OVERLAY: VERIFY THE REPAIRED ROUTE

**Visual:** Verify the repaired route
**Duration:** 37s minimum

Narration: For this example, the first useful result is accurate public information and a working contact path. Check the live listing after the edit and follow its website link. Keep the observed result in the change record. Then return to the next highest-priority website problem. That is a concrete maintenance sequence, not evidence of a measured conversion lift. Start with a specific observed error, such as an outdated contact route, rather than assuming the whole profile is ineffective. Give that repair an owner and verify it from the public view. If the information is already accurate, this example does not justify delaying a needed website repair.
