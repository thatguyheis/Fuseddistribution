## HOOK

**Visual:** Separate schedule from plan
**Duration:** 21s minimum

Narration: My take: a regular buying schedule should not be asked to answer every question in a financial plan. Dollar cost averaging describes investing equal amounts at regular intervals regardless of market movements. Calling it merely an admission would dismiss a real approach without examining its purpose. The useful debate is what else the decision needs.

---

## OVERLAY: NAME THE UNANSWERED QUESTIONS

**Visual:** Name the unanswered questions
**Duration:** 22s minimum

Narration: A schedule alone does not tell you whether a particular asset suits your goals, how much risk you can bear, or when you might need the money. Those are separate questions. Writing them down before choosing an interval makes the limits of the schedule visible, instead of letting the convenience of automation stand in for a complete decision.

---

## OVERLAY: USE THE TERM PRECISELY

**Visual:** Use the term precisely
**Duration:** 21s minimum

Narration: Investor.gov describes dollar cost averaging in terms of equal portions and regular intervals. If the amounts or timing change with a market prediction, describe that variation honestly. A label should help someone understand what is happening. It should not make a discretionary decision sound like an unchanged rule or imply that a particular result is guaranteed.

---

## OVERLAY: INCLUDE REAL TRANSACTION COSTS

**Visual:** Include real transaction costs
**Duration:** 21s minimum

Narration: When discussing physical silver purchases, record the actual delivery charges, payment costs, applicable tax and other transaction terms in your comparison. Those details vary by order and seller. Do not assume that splitting one hypothetical budget into more orders leaves every cost unchanged, and do not invent current premiums to make one approach appear superior.

---

## OVERLAY: MAKE THE COMPARISON FAIR

**Visual:** Make the comparison fair
**Duration:** 39s minimum

Narration: My position is that consistency can be useful while still needing a reasoned plan around it. A comparison should state the assumptions, time period, asset and costs. It should also acknowledge what it cannot predict. This educational discussion is not a recommendation to buy silver now, to wait for a particular price, or to use a schedule that does not fit your circumstances. Write down the purpose of the schedule and the circumstances that would make you review it. A recurring reminder can help carry out a decision, but it cannot decide your priorities or absorb unexpected costs. Keep those questions visible rather than letting repetition become the whole explanation.
